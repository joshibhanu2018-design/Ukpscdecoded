## 16.1 NATURAL VEGETATION — ALTITUDINAL ZONATION


### Five Altitude Zones

| Zone | Altitude | Forest Type | Key Species |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Tropical** | Below 1,000m | Moist/Dry Deciduous (Terai-Bhabar, lower Shivalik) | **Sal** (dominant), Khair, Shisham, Haldu, Bamboo, Dhhak, Semal, Gular, Jamun, Ber |
| **Subtropical** | 1,000–2,000m | Coniferous (Pine forests) | **Chir Pine** (Pinus roxburghii) — almost total dominance |
| **Temperate** | 2,000–3,000m | Moist Evergreen + Coniferous | **Banj Oak**, Moru Oak, Kharsu Oak, Deodar, **Buransh** (Rhododendron), Cypress (2,000–3,000m) |
| **Sub-Alpine** | 3,000–3,600m | High-level forests north of main ranges | **Birch (Bhojpatra)**, Silver Fir, Juniper, Willow, Ribes |
| **Alpine** | Above 3,600m | Tundra → Perpetual ice | **Bugyals** (alpine meadows): velvety grasses, Mosses, Lichens, Blue Poppies, Edelweiss, **Brahma Kamal** (state flower) |

> **KEY:** Vegetation ceases entirely in the perpetually frozen zone above 4,800m.

### The Pirul Paradox (Chir Pine Problem)

- Monocultures of Chir Pine shed highly flammable needles (Pirul)
- Drives catastrophic forest fires + soil acidity
- Replaces Oak = reduces groundwater percolation = "Spring Crisis"
- **Policy Response:** Pirul Lao-Paise Pao campaign (see Ch.14 for details)

## 16.2 SOIL TYPES & DISTRIBUTION

### Complete Soil Classification

| Soil Type | Location | Characteristics |
|---|---|---|
| **Terai Soil** | US Nagar, Haridwar (southern plains) | Alluvial, marshy, rich in clay; highly fertile for sugarcane + paddy; deficient in nitrogen & phosphorus |
| **Bhabar Soil** | North of Terai, south of Shivaliks | Large pebbles, boulders, thick sands; **highly porous** — unsuitable for agriculture |
| **Tertiary Soil** | Shivalik hills, Doon Valley | Light, sandy, porous; less moisture-holding capacity |
| **Loamy (Domat) Soil** | Doon Valley, lower Shivalik slopes | Moisture-absorbent; rich in lime and iron |
| **Forest Brown Soil** | Forested hilly regions | Acidic; deficient in lime & phosphorus due to leaching |
| **Red Soil** | Mountain slopes and edges | Unconsolidated; formed by weathering of underlying rocks |
| **Alpine Pasture Soil** | Grasslands above 4,000m | Granitic sandy loam; slightly alkaline; high organic matter |
| **Highest Mountain Shallow Soil** | Above pastures | Thin, dry, immature; extreme cold + no vegetation |
| **Volcanic & Quartz Soil** | Bhimtal region (Nainital) | Quartz from schist/shale; volcanic from igneous rocks |

### Administrative Land Classification (Agriculture)

| Category | Location | Features |
|---|---|---|
| **Talaon (Kotlav)** | Valley bottoms | Levelled, irrigated, highly fertile; ideal for paddy + wheat |
| **Upraon (Ukhad)** | Mid-slopes | Terraced but unirrigated (rain-fed); red/brown gravelly loam |
| **Katil / Khil** | Forest margins | Stony, immature, poor fertility; intermittent cultivation (Mandua) |

### Soil Erosion

| Parameter | Data |
|---|---|
| **Extreme erosion rate** | **96 tonnes/hectare/year** (Tehri, Chamoli, Uttarkashi) |
| **Cause** | Road cutting (disturbs angle of repose), deforestation, HISD rainfall |
| **Mitigation shift** | From Gabion check dams → **Bio-engineering** (coir geo-textiles + deep-rooted grasses on Char Dham routes) |

## 16.3 GLACIERS OF UTTARAKHAND

### Overview

| Parameter | Details |
|---|---|
| **Local Names** | Himnads, Himadri, Bamak, Bank |
| **Formation** | Pleistocene Ice Age |
| **Type** | Primarily valley-type glaciers |
| **Typical Length** | 3–5 km (most); some much larger |
| **Movement Rate** | 8–13 cm/day at sides; 20–30 cm/day in middle |
| **Surface Feature** | Extensive superficial moraine and rock waste covering upper surface (appear as "floating debris") |
| **Legal Status** | Uttarakhand HC declared Gangotri + Yamunotri glaciers as **"living entities"** with rights, duties, liabilities |

### Glacier Retreat Data (State-Wide)

| Parameter | Data |
|---|---|
| **Area Loss (1990–2020)** | **12%** |
| **Snow Cover Decline** | **~40%** |
| **Average Retreat** | 5 m/year (Himalayan avg) |

| **GLOF Risk** | NDMA identified **13 high-risk glacial lakes** |
| **Hydropower Threat** | Long-term threat to state's 25,000 MW potential |
| **Siltation Paradox** | Accelerated melt → more sediment → decreased turbine life |
| **Discharge Contribution** | Glacier melt = **30–50%** of total discharge in Ganga system |

### Analytical Dimensions (For Mains)

| Dimension | Details |
|---|---|
| **Black Carbon Feedback** | Forest fire smoke/ash deposits on glacial surfaces → darkens ice → reduces albedo → accelerates melting |
| **Nivation Zone Failure** | 2025 Dharali: NOT a cloudburst but structural ice collapse (69M kg) — new "structural" drainage threat |
| **Elevation-Dependent Warming** | Himalayas warming 2–5× faster than global average → permafrost destabilization |
| **Living Entity Status** | UK High Court declared Gangotri + Yamunotri glaciers as "living entities" — legal protection framework |
| **Monitoring** | Moving to Continuous Glaciological Monitoring using SAR (Synthetic Aperture Radar) + drones |
| **EWS** | WIHG + NDMA installing automated water-level sensors in 13 high-risk glacial lakes |

## 16.4 LAKES OF UTTARAKHAND

### Classification by Type

| Type | Description | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Glacial (Tarn/Cirque)** | ~95% of high-altitude lakes; formed by glacial erosion | Roopkund, Hemkund, Vasuki Tal, Homkund |
| **Tectonic** | Formed by folding/faulting; common in Kumaon | Nainital, Bhimtal |
| **Temporary (Landslide-blocked)** | Created when landslides dam rivers; pose flood risk | Gohna Lake |
| **Artificial (Man-made)** | For power/irrigation | Tehri Lake, Nanak Sagar, Asan Barrage |
| **Paternoster** | Chain of connected glacial lakes | Tara Tal Complex |

### Lake Issues & Governance (For Mains Analysis)

| Issue | Details |
|---|---|
| **Carrying Capacity Breach** | Nainital/Mussoorie exceeded structural limits; built-up area around Naini Lake increased 34% |
| **Eutrophication** | High-altitude lakes (Roopkund, Hemkund) face "Organic Overload" from mule/human waste; zero decomposition in freezing temps → oxygen depletion |
| **GLOF Threat** | 13 high-risk glacial lakes (NDMA) — thinning moraine dams + accelerated melt |
| **Governance Tool** | **Bhagirath App** (2025) — citizens map and report endangered water sources |
| **SARA** | Spring & River Rejuvenation Authority — maps recharge zones scientifically (see Ch.12) |
| **GEP** | Uttarakhand = first state to launch GEP Index (July 19, 2024) — measures Air, Water, Soil, Forest quality (see Ch.14) |

## 16.5 CLIMATE CHANGE — INTEGRATED ANALYSIS

> *Cross-cutting theme — primary facts here; policy responses in Ch.14*

### The Cryosphere-Climate Feedback Loop

| Process | Impact |
|---|---|
| Elevation-Dependent Warming | Himalayas warming **2–5× global average** |
| Glacier Fragmentation | Not just retreat — fragmenting into unstable patches |
| Permafrost Destabilization | Causes structural collapses (Dharali 2025) |
| Black Carbon | Forest fire ash on ice → reduces albedo → accelerates melt |
| GLOF Proliferation | 13 high-risk moraine-dammed lakes |

### Hydro-Ecological Impact — The "Spring Crisis"

| Parameter | Data/Outcome |
|---|---|
| **Springs Drying** | 50%+ of Naulas/Dharas drying or becoming seasonal |
| **Cause** | Winter snow deficit + replacement of Banj Oak with Chir Pine = reduced percolation |
| **River Impact** | Kosi River: perennial length shrunk from 225 km to just 41 km |
| **Aquifer Puncturing** | Rail tunneling (Rishikesh-Karnaprayag) severs underground veins |

### Bio-Phenological Disruptions

| Shift | Details |
|---|---|
| **Timberline Migration** | Upward at **37 m/decade** → Bugyals shrinking |
| **Buransh Mismatch** | Blooming Jan/Feb instead of spring → disrupts pollinators |
| **Apple Belt Shift** | Traditional belt migrating to higher reaches (Harsil); lower areas shift to Kiwi/aromatics |
| **Invasive Encroachment** | Lantana camara (1,334 sq. km) outcompeting native species |

### Institutional Response

| Initiative | Details |
|---|---|
| **GEP** (first state globally) | Launched July 19, 2024; measures Air, Water, Soil, Forests |
| **Green Bonus Demand** | ₹10,000 crore from Finance Commission (ecological services payment) |
| **SARA** | Springshed Management — maps exact recharge zones |
| **Pirul Lao-Paise Pao** | Pine needle collection → biofuel (see Ch.14) |
| **Doppler Radars** | Mukteshwar, Surkanda, Lansdowne — early warning |
| **SAR + Drone Monitoring** | For glacial lakes and unstable ice bodies |
| **Dehradun Declaration (2025)** | From World Summit on Disaster Management — resilient infrastructure over reactive relief |
