## 16.1 NATURAL VEGETATION — ALTITUDINAL ZONATION

### Five Altitude Zones

| Zone | Altitude | Forest Type | Key Species |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tropical | Below 1,000m | Moist/Dry Deciduous (Terai-Bhabar, lower Shivalik) | Sal (dominant), Khair, Shisham, Haldu, Bamboo, Dhhak, Semal, Gular, Jamun, Ber |
| Subtropical | 1,000–2,000m | Coniferous (Pine forests) | Chir Pine (Pinus roxburghii) — almost total dominance |
| Temperate | 2,000–3,000m | Moist Evergreen + Coniferous | Banj Oak, Moru Oak, Kharsu Oak, Deodar, Buransh (Rhododendron), Cypress |
| Sub-Alpine | 3,000–3,600m | High-level forests north of main ranges | Birch (Bhojpatra), Silver Fir, Juniper, Willow, Ribes |
| Alpine | Above 3,600m | Tundra → Perpetual ice | Bugyals (alpine meadows): velvety grasses, Mosses, Lichens, Blue Poppies, Edelweiss, Brahma Kamal (state flower) |

Vegetation ceases entirely in the perpetually frozen zone above 4,800 m.

### ISFR 2023 (18th Report) — Key Data

The total forest cover of Uttarakhand is 24,303.83 sq. km, constituting 45.44% of the state's total area. The Forest Survey of India (FSI) was established on June 1, 1981, with its headquarters in Dehradun, and has issued biennial reports since 1987. The 2023 report shows an increase of 211.57 sq. km in Very Dense Forest (VDF), while Moderately Dense Forest (MDF) has declined by 250.42 sq. km — a phenomenon described as the "hollowing out" of the middle-tier forest canopy.

### The Pirul Paradox (Chir Pine Problem)

Monocultures of Chir Pine shed highly flammable needles (Pirul) that drive catastrophic forest fires and increase soil acidity. The replacement of Oak forests by Pine reduces groundwater percolation, contributing to what is called the "Spring Crisis." The state has responded with the Pirul Lao-Paise Pao campaign to incentivize collection of pine needles for biomass energy.

### Soil Erosion

Extreme erosion rates of 96 tonnes/hectare/year have been recorded in Tehri, Chamoli, and Uttarkashi districts. The primary causes include road cutting (which disturbs the angle of repose), deforestation, and High-Intensity Short-Duration (HISD) rainfall events. Mitigation efforts have shifted from gabion check dams to bio-engineering approaches using coir geo-textiles and deep-rooted grasses, particularly along the Char Dham routes.

## 16.3 GLACIERS OF UTTARAKHAND

### Overview

Uttarakhand's glaciers, known locally as Himnads, Himadri, Bamak, or Bank, were formed during the Pleistocene Ice Age. They are primarily valley-type glaciers, with most measuring 3–5 km in length, though some are considerably larger. Movement rates range from 8–13 cm/day at the sides to 20–30 cm/day in the middle. Their surfaces are covered with extensive superficial moraine and rock waste, giving them the appearance of "floating debris." The Uttarakhand High Court has declared Gangotri and Yamunotri glaciers as "living entities" with legal rights, duties, and liabilities.

### Analytical Dimensions

The Black Carbon Feedback mechanism operates when forest fire smoke and ash deposits on glacial surfaces, darkening the ice, reducing albedo, and accelerating melting. The 2025 Dharali incident demonstrated a new threat category — nivation zone failure involving structural ice collapse (69 million kg) rather than a cloudburst. Elevation-dependent warming means the Himalayas are warming 2–5× faster than the global average, destabilizing permafrost. Monitoring has shifted to continuous glaciological methods using Synthetic Aperture Radar (SAR) and drones, while WIHG and NDMA are installing automated water-level sensors in the 13 high-risk glacial lakes as part of an Early Warning System.

### Lake Issues & Governance

The primary challenges facing Uttarakhand's lakes include carrying capacity breaches (Nainital and Mussoorie have exceeded structural limits, with built-up area around Naini Lake increasing by 34%), eutrophication (high-altitude lakes like Roopkund and Hemkund face "Organic Overload" from mule and human waste, with zero decomposition in freezing temperatures leading to oxygen depletion), and GLOF threats from 13 high-risk glacial lakes where thinning moraine dams combine with accelerated melt. The Bhagirath App (2025) allows citizens to map and report endangered water sources, while SARA (Spring & River Rejuvenation Authority) maps recharge zones scientifically. Uttarakhand became the first state to launch a GEP (Gross Environment Product) Index on July 19, 2024, measuring Air, Water, Soil, and Forest quality.

## 16.5 CLIMATE CHANGE — INTEGRATED ANALYSIS

### The Cryosphere-Climate Feedback Loop

The Himalayas are experiencing elevation-dependent warming at 2–5× the global average. Glaciers are not merely retreating but fragmenting into unstable patches. Permafrost destabilization is causing structural collapses, as demonstrated at Dharali in 2025. Black carbon from forest fire ash deposited on ice surfaces reduces albedo and accelerates melting. GLOF proliferation threatens 13 high-risk moraine-dammed lakes across the state.

### Hydro-Ecological Impact — The "Spring Crisis"

Over 50% of Naulas and Dharas (traditional springs) are drying or becoming seasonal. The causes include chronic winter snow deficit combined with the replacement of Banj Oak forests with Chir Pine, which reduces percolation. The impact on rivers is severe — the Kosi River's perennial length has shrunk from 225 km to just 41 km. The Rishikesh-Karnaprayag Rail Link's tunneling has also punctured aquifers, severing underground water veins.

### Bio-Phenological Disruptions

The timberline is migrating upward at 37 m/decade, causing Bugyals to shrink. Buransh (Rhododendron) is now blooming in January and February instead of spring, disrupting pollinator cycles. The traditional apple belt is migrating to higher reaches such as Harsil, while lower areas are shifting to Kiwi and aromatics. Invasive species encroachment continues, with Lantana camara covering 1,334 sq. km and outcompeting native species.

### Institutional Response

Uttarakhand launched the GEP (Gross Environment Product) on July 19, 2024, becoming the first state globally to do so, measuring Air, Water, Soil, and Forest quality. The state has demanded ₹10,000 crore as a Green Bonus from the Finance Commission for ecological services. SARA has been established for Springshed Management to map exact recharge zones. The Pirul Lao-Paise Pao programme converts collected pine needles into biofuel. Doppler Radars have been installed at Mukteshwar, Surkanda, and Lansdowne for early warning. SAR and drone monitoring is being deployed for glacial lakes and unstable ice bodies. The Dehradun Declaration (2025), emerging from the World Summit on Disaster Management, emphasizes resilient infrastructure over reactive relief.
