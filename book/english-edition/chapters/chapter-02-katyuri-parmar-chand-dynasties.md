## PART A: THE KARTIKEYAPUR (KATYURI) DYNASTY (700 CE – 11th Century)

> *The first unified historical dynasty of Uttarakhand — their reign is called the "Golden Age" for architecture and sculpture.*

---

## 2.1 FOUNDATION & KEY RULERS

| Parameter | Details |
|---|---|
| **Founder** | Basant Dev (700 CE) |
| **Imperial Title** | Paramabhattaraka Maharajadhiraja Parameshwar |
| **First Capital** | Joshimath (Chamoli) |
| **Later Capital** | Vaidhnath-Kartikeyapur (Katyur Valley, Baijnath) |
| **Capital Shifted By** | Subhikshraj Dev (folk ballads attribute it to Narsingh Dev) |
| **Period** | 700 CE – 11th Century |

### The Three Key Rulers

| Ruler | Achievement | Significance |
|---|---|---|
| **Ishtagan (Nimbar Dynasty)** | United entire Uttarakhand into one political entity | Shaivite ruler; first true unifier |
| **Lalitsur Dev** | Most powerful ruler; great builder | Maximum copper plates belong to him; described as **Varaha (boar) incarnation** who uplifted the earth |
| **Bhudev** | Staunch supporter of Brahmanism | Opposed Buddhism; heavily contributed to Baijnath temple construction |

---

## 2.2 ADMINISTRATION & MILITARY STRUCTURE

### Territorial Division (Highly Centralized & Hierarchical)

```
STATE (King)
    ↓
BHUKTI / Province (headed by UPARIKA)
    ↓
VISH / District (headed by VISHPATI)
    ↓
PALLIKA / Village (headed by MAHATTAM or SAYAN)
```

### Key Administrative Officials

| Official | Role |
|---|---|
| **Saudabhangadhikrit** | Chief Architect for state construction |
| **Prantpal** | Protector of state borders |
| **Ghattapal** | Protector of mountain passes |
| **Narapati** | Managed river ghats; collected crossing taxes |
| **Akshapatlik** | Chief Auditor |
| **Bhogpati** | Tax collector |
| **Bhatt & Char** | Responsible for extracting begar (forced labour) |
| **Pramavatar / Prabhatar** | Land measurement officer |

### Military Divisions

| Division | Commander Title |
|---|---|
| Infantry | Gaulmika |
| Cavalry | Ashvabaladhikrit |
| Elephant Corps | Hastibaladhikrit |
| Camel Corps | Ushtrabaladhikrit |

## 2.5 ARCHITECTURE — THE ZENITH OF TEMPLE CONSTRUCTION

The Katyuri period represents the **architectural Golden Age** of Uttarakhand, heavily influenced by the Nagara style.

### Two Temple Styles

| Style | Description | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Chhatra-Type (Pagoda)** | Distinct wooden umbrella (chhatra) roof; wood-based | Kedarnath Temple, Lakhamandal, Bageshwar |
| **Shikhara-Type** | Square, stone-built; no wooden umbrella; often built in family groups | Dwarahat Group (Gujar Deval), Katarmal Sun Temple |

### Key Architectural Sites

| Site | Significance |
|---|---|
| Jageshwar | 100+ temples; Nagara & Shikara styles |
| Dwarahat (Gujar Deval) | Incomparable example of Shikhara style |
| Katarmal Sun Temple | 2nd largest Sun Temple in India |
| Baijnath | Main Shiva shrine + 17 subsidiary shrines |

## 2.6 DECLINE & FRAGMENTATION

### Causes of Decline

| Factor | Details |
|---|---|
| **Weak Successors** | Internal administrative decay |
| **Nepalese Invasion 1** | King **Ashokchalla** (1191 CE) |
| **Nepalese Invasion 2** | King **Krachaldeva** (1223 CE) — subjugated Katyuri & early Chand territories |
| **Internal Collapse** | Dynasty fractured into multiple branches |
| **Last Ruler** | **Brahmadev (Veerdev)** — highly tyrannical and oppressive |

### Fragmented Branches

- Rajwars of Askot
- Mallas of Doti
- Rising Chand Dynasty eventually ended Katyuri supremacy

> **KEY FACT:** According to the folk ballad *Jiyarani*, the last ruler Brahmadev's misrule was the primary internal cause of the dynasty's collapse.

## 2.10 KING AJAY PAL (1490–1519 CE) — THE BISMARCK OF GARHWAL

> *The 37th ruler — historically compared to Ashoka, Napoleon, and Bismarck for his unmatched military and administrative vision.*

| Achievement | Details |
|---|---|
| **Political Unification** | Conquered and consolidated **52 independent Garhs** (forts) into unified 'Garhwal' |
| **Key Victory** | Defeated chieftain **Kaffu Chauhan** of Uppoo Garh |
| **Capital Shift 1** | Chandpur Garhi → **Devalgarh** (1512 CE) |
| **Capital Shift 2** | Devalgarh → **Srinagar** (1517 CE) — became "Delhi of Uttarakhand" |
| **Economic Reform** | Introduced **'Dhuli Patha Paimana'** — standardized measurement (1 Patha ≈ 2 Kg) |
| **Social Reform** | Instituted **Sarola Brahmin practice** — food cooked by them acceptable to all varnas; eliminated caste discrimination in army |
| **Religious Patronage** | Installed family deity **Goddess Rajrajeshwari** in Devalgarh |
| **Later Life** | Adopted Gorakhnath sect; earned title 'Adinath' in Sawari Granth |

---

## 2.11 OTHER PROMINENT PARMAR RULERS

| Ruler | Period | Key Achievement |
|---|---|---|
| **Mahipat Shah** (Garbh-Bhanjak) | 1631–1635 | Repelled Tibetan (Dapa) invasions; commanders: **Madho Singh Bhandari** (built Maletha canal), **Rikhola Lodhi**, Banwari Das |
| **Rani Karnavati** ('Nak-Kati Rani') | 1635 | Regent queen; defeated Shah Jahan's commander Najabat Khan; amputated noses of Mughal soldiers; built **Rajpur Canal** (Dehradun) |
| **Prithvipati Shah** | 1658 | Gave asylum to fugitive prince **Suleiman Shikoh** (son of Dara Shikoh); angered Aurangzeb |
| **Fateh Shah** | 1664–1710 | **Golden Age of Garhwal** — details below |
| **Pradeep Shah** | 1717–1773 | Sent 3 lakh rupees to assist Kumaon's Kalyan Chand IV during Rohilla invasion (Dunagiri War, 1743) |
| **Lalit Shah** | 1779 | Won Battle of Bagwali Pokhar against Kumaon; defeated Mohan Chand; installed his son Pradyumna Shah as Kumaon's ruler |

## 2.12 KING FATEH SHAH (1664–1710) — THE GOLDEN AGE OF GARHWAL

> *Historically compared to Shivaji (military prowess) and Akbar (court patronage).*

### Military Campaigns

| Campaign | Details |
|---|---|
| Territorial Expansion | Captured **Paonta, Jaunsar, and Saharanpur** (established Fateh Nagar) |
| **Battle of Bhangani (1688)** | Fought against Sikh Guru Gobind Singh |
| Other Wars | Multiple wars against Sirmaur and Kumaon states |

### Patron of Arts & Literature

| Feature | Details |
|---|---|
| **Court System** | Adorned with **"Nine Gems" (Navaratnas)** — like Akbar |
| **Scholars** | Suresh Anand Barthwal, Hari Datt Nautiyal, Dhasmana |

### Literary Works of Fateh Shah's Era

| Text | Author | Content |
|---|---|---|
| **Fateh Prakash** | Ratan Kavi | Described Fateh Shah as "Protector of Hindus" |
| **Vritta Kaumudi** | Matiram | Literary classic |
| **Fatehshah Yashovarnan** | Ramchandra | Praise of the king |

### Religious Tolerance

- Invited Sikh **Guru Ram Rai** to Dehradun in **1676**
- Donated three villages (**Khudbuda, Rajpur, Chamasari**) to build the famous **Dehradun Gurudwara**

## 2.13 PARMAR ADMINISTRATION & JUSTICE

### Central Administration

| Position | Role |
|---|---|
| **King** | Supreme authority — revered as incarnation of God (**Bolanda Badrish**) |
| **Mukhtar / Wazir** | Chief Minister |
| **Diwan** | Policies + Military/Senapati duties |
| **Daftari** | In-charge of Royal Orders |
| **Bakshi** | Dispatcher/Messenger |

### Territorial Division

```
STATE (King)
    ↓
MANDALS / PARGANAS (headed by FAUJDAR or military officer)
    ↓
PATTIS (headed by SAYANA or KANUNGO)
    ↓
VILLAGES (managed by PRADHAN and PATWARI)
```

### Justice System

| Level | Authority |
|---|---|
| Village level | Panchayats handled disputes |
| District level | Chief Court under the Diwan |
| Supreme level | King held absolute judicial authority |
| Severe punishments | Confiscation of property; exile |

> **NOTE:** A codified formal law system — *'Narendra Hindu Law'* — was compiled much later in **1918 by Harikrishna Raturi** during the Tehri phase.

## 2.14 PARMAR ECONOMY & LAND SYSTEM

### Land Grant Types

| Type | Purpose |
|---|---|
| **Vishnupriti / Goonth / Agrahar** | Tax-free land donated to Brahmins or temples |
| **Raut** | Land given to soldiers for exceptional bravery |
| **Jagir** | Land granted to state officials in lieu of salary |

### Taxation System

| Tax | Description |
|---|---|
| **Tihar** | Primary land tax — **1/3 of agricultural production** |
| **Sirti** | Cash tax |
| **Paud Tuti** | Import-export tax on goods moving through borders |
| **Dand** | Monetary fine |
| **Bhent** | Gifts/tributes given to King by subordinates |

### Measurement System

| System | Details |
|---|---|
| **Grain** | Dhuli Patha (1 Patha ≈ 2 Kg) — introduced by Ajaypal |
| **Land** | Measured in Jyula, Nali, and Mutthi |

### The Garhwal School of Painting

| Feature | Details |
|---|---|
| **Origin** | Influenced by Mughal and Kangra art; initiated when Suleiman Shikoh brought painters **Shamdas and Hardas** to Srinagar |
| **Greatest Exponent** | **Mola Ram Tomar** (1743–1833) |
| **Mola Ram's Legacy** | Founded the **Manmath Panth**; authored *Garhraj Vansh Kavya* |

| **Peak Period** | Under Fateh Shah and successors |

### Civic Architecture — Unique Elements

| Element | Function |
|---|---|
| **Tharp** | Square platforms near temples for panchayat meetings |
| **Manda** | Travel route mandaps (rest houses) |
| **Mangra / Bau** | Water channels / tanks |
| **Chhina** | Water tunnels cut through mountains (e.g., famous Maletha canal) |

## 2.16 GORKHA INVASION & FALL OF THE PARMAR DYNASTY (1804)

### Reasons for Decline (Multi-Factor Analysis)

| Factor | Details |
|---|---|
| **Natural Disasters** | 1795: Severe famine (*Ikavani Bavani*); 1 Sept 1803: Catastrophic 9.0 earthquake destroyed infrastructure |
| **Empty Treasury** | Continuous calamities + looting by step-brothers + heavy annual tributes to Gorkhas (Rs 25,000 after 1791 Battle of Langurgarh) |
| **Internal Conspiracies** | Khanduri vs Dobhal ministerial factions; rebellion by Parakram Shah |
| **External Treachery** | Kumaoni Diwan **Harshdev Joshi** ('Chanakya of Kumaon') actively invited and assisted Gorkhas |
| **The Final Battle** | Gorkhas under **Amar Singh Thapa** and **Hastidal Chautariya** attacked (1803) |
| **Pradyumna Shah's End** | Sold royal throne and jewelry; raised makeshift army in Saharanpur; **martyred at Battle of Khurbura (May 14, 1804)** |

> **KEY FACT:** The fall of the 1100-year-old Parmar dynasty was not a sudden military defeat, but the culmination of severe internal decay, natural disasters, and external manipulation.

## 2.19 KEY CHAND RULERS — CHRONOLOGICAL TABLE

| Ruler | Period | Key Achievement / Title |
|---|---|---|
| **Garud Gyan Chand** | ~14th C. | First Chand ruler to visit Delhi; presented tribute to **Firoz Shah Tughlaq**; received title 'Garud'; granted control over Terai |
| **Bharti Chand** | 1451–52 | Fought **12-year war** against Doti kingdom; permanently ended Kumaon's subordination to Nepal; genesis of **Nayak (Katwal) Caste** |
| **Balo Kalyan Chand** | 1560s | Shifted capital to **Almora**; built **Lalmundi Fort** (Fort Moira) |
| **Rudra Chand** | 1565–1597 | **Scholar King** — detailed below |
| **Baj Bahadur Chand** | ~17th C. | Rose from shepherd to king; expanded boundaries; imposed Sirti tax on Bhotiyas; banned exploitative begar (>1 day/month); titled 'Bahadur' by Shah Jahan |
| **Jagat Chand** | ~18th C. | **Golden Age of Kumaon** — revenue peaked at 9 lakh rupees; defeated Garhwal's Fateh Shah; temporarily captured Srinagar |

## 2.20 KING RUDRA CHAND (1565–1597 CE) — THE SCHOLAR KING
