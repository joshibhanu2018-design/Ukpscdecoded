## 3.3 KING RUDRA CHAND (1565–1597 CE) — THE SCHOLAR KING

> *The most multifaceted ruler of the Chand dynasty — balancing military might with scholarly and social contributions.*

### Mughal Relations

| Aspect | Details |
|---|---|
| **Contemporary of** | Mughal Emperor Akbar |
| **Met Akbar** | Personally in Lahore (1588) to accept suzerainty |
| **Result** | Kumaon listed under Delhi Suba in **Ain-i-Akbari** |
| **Founded** | Town of **Rudrapur** |

### Social Engineering — Traivarnik Dharmanirnay

Rudra Chand fundamentally restructured Kumaoni society by composing this text:

| Brahmin Class | Status/Duty |
|---|---|
| **Chauthani** | High-caste (highest tier) |
| **Tithani / Panchbirriye** | Middle tier |
| **Pitaliye / Haliye** | Lower tier |
| **Oli Brahmin** (unique creation) | Sole duty: warn public by beating plates during hailstorms |

### Literary Works

| Text | Subject |
|---|---|
| **Yatha Usharagodaya** | Political administration |
| **Shyenik Shastra** | Art of hunting / the chase |
| **Yayati Charitram** | Sanskrit drama |

### Military & Architecture

- Fought **Badhanagarh (Gwaldam) War** against Garhwal ruler Balbhadra Shah
- Commander **Purush Pant** killed in this battle
- Built **Malla Mahal** fort in Almora

## 3.4 CHAND ADMINISTRATION & JUSTICE SYSTEM

### Territorial Division

```
STATE (King — Supreme Authority)
    ↓
MANDALS (ruled directly or by feudatories)
    ↓
PARGANAS (administered by SIKDAR)
    ↓
GARKHS / PATTIS (managed by NEGI)
    ↓
VILLAGES (headed by SAYANA or BOODHA)
```

### State Employees (Three Tiers — by Lakshmi Chand)

| Tier | Role |
|---|---|
| **Sardar** | Ruler of a Pargana |
| **Faujdar** | Military officer |
| **Negi** | Officer who collected customary dues in grain |

### Village Administration

| Official | Role |
|---|---|
| **Sayana / Boodha** | Village head; also served as local judge |
| **Kotal** | Kept revenue accounts |
| **Prahari** | Village watchman |
| **Village Panchayat** | Called **Boodhachoura** |

### Justice System (Reformed by Lakshmi Chand)

| Court | Jurisdiction |
|---|---|
| **Nyovaali** | Civil / public cases |
| **Bishtaali** | Military cases |

**Punishments:**
- Death penalty (hanging from a tree)
- Mutilation
- Exile
- Confiscation of property

## 3.5 ECONOMY, LAND POLICY & THE 36 TAXES (CHHATISI)

### Land Ownership Hierarchy

| Class | Role |
|---|---|
| **King** | Supreme landowner |
| **Thatwan** | Landholders (under the King) |
| **Khaikar** | Tenant farmers (under Thatwan) |
| **Sirtan** | Lowest tier — sharecroppers paying annual rent |

### Land Grant Types

| Type | Purpose |
|---|---|
| **Raut** | Land granted for bravery in war |
| **Goonth** | Tax-free land for Brahmins / temples / pilgrims |

### Revenue Rate

- **Galla-Chhahada** = Land revenue fixed at **1/6th of agricultural produce**

> **COMPARISON:** Parmar tax (Tihar) = 1/3 of produce vs. Chand tax = 1/6 of produce. The Chands had a lighter direct tax but compensated with 36 indirect taxes.

### The 36 Taxes — CHHATISI (Key Taxes to Remember)

| Tax Name | Description |
|---|---|
| **Sirtan / Sirti** | Primary cash tax (later imposed specifically on Bhotiyas by Baj Bahadur Chand) |
| **Koot / Khoot** | Tax payable strictly in grain |
| **Katak** | Dedicated tax collected for the army |
| **Jyuliya / Sanga** | Toll tax at river bridges |
| **Kheni Kapilni** | **Forced unpaid labour (Begar)** |
| **Rakhiya** | Tax during Raksha Bandhan and Janeu ceremonies |
| **Mijhari** | Tax on labourers and Jagaris |
| **Tika Bhent** | Levied on marriages and auspicious occasions |
| **Maro Kar** | Tax on a person who died without a son |
| **Sonya Phagun** | Tax during festivals |

> **EXAM TIP:** The "Chhatisi" (36 taxes) system is a very high-yield topic. Remember at least 6-7 key taxes with their specific functions for both Prelims MCQs and Mains answers.

## 3.6 CHAND SOCIETY & CULTURE

### Social Structure

- Formally rigidified by Rudra Chand's **Traivarnik Dharmanirnay**
- Brahmins divided into strict sub-castes (Chauthani, Tithani, Pitaliye)
- **Nayak (Katwal) Caste:** Born from relations between Chand soldiers and Doti women during Bharti Chand's 12-year war

### Art & Architecture

| Category | Achievements |
|---|---|
| **Stone Carving** | Baleshwar Temple Group (Champawat) — masterpiece of Chand art |
| **Water Conservation** | Integration of **Naulas** (covered step-wells/springs) into temple complexes; e.g., **Ek Hathiya Ka Naula** (Champawat) |
| **Temple Architecture** | **Hathiya Dewal Temple** (Thal, Pithoragarh) — modeled after Ellora |
| **Temple Renovation** | Lakshmi Chand renovated **Bagnath Temple** at Bageshwar |

### Fort Architecture

| Fort | Builder | Location |
|---|---|---|
| Rajbunga Fort | Somchand | Champawat |
| Lalmundi Fort (Fort Moira) | Balo Kalyan Chand | Almora |
| Malla Mahal | Rudra Chand | Almora |
| Khagmara Fort | — | Almora |

## 3.7 DECLINE & FALL OF THE CHAND DYNASTY

### Causes of Decline (Multi-Factor)

| Factor | Details |
|---|---|
| **Weak Later Rulers** | Dynasty crippled by incompetent successors |
| **Internal Conspiracies** | Court factions fighting for power |
| **External Invasions** | Rohilla attacks weakened the state |
| **The Fatal Betrayal** | Diwan **Harshdev Joshi** ('Chanakya of Kumaon' / 'Earl of Warwick') invited Gorkhas to attack |
| **Final Battle** | Battle of **Hawalabagh** (1790 CE) |
| **Last King** | **Mahendra Chand** — defeated by Gorkhas |

> **KEY INSIGHT:** Harshdev Joshi's manipulation is the single most important human factor in the fall of both the Chand (1790) and Parmar (1804) dynasties. He invited and assisted the Gorkhas in both invasions.

## PART B: THE GORKHA RULE IN UTTARAKHAND (1790–1815)

> *Historically termed 'Gorkhyali' — this period is remembered as a dark, tyrannical, and atrocities-based military administration.*

---

## 3.8 THE GORKHA INVASION — TIMELINE

| Year | Event | Key Details |
|---|---|---|
| **1790** | **Gorkhas conquer Kumaon** | Led by Hastidal Chautariya, Amar Singh Thapa, Jagjit Pandey; defeated last Chand King Mahendra Chand at **Hawalabag field** |
| **1791** | First attack on Garhwal | Battle of Langurgarh — settled for annual tribute of Rs 25,000 from Pradyumna Shah |
| **1803** | Devastating earthquake (9.0 magnitude) | September 1, 1803 — destroyed Garhwal's infrastructure |

### Duration of Gorkha Rule

| Region | Duration |
|---|---|
| Kumaon | **25 years** (1790–1815) |
| Garhwal | **10.5 years** (1804–1815) |

## 3.10 THE BRUTAL JUSTICE SYSTEM — DIVYA PARIKSHA (ORDEALS)

> *The Gorkhas had no fixed, codified laws. Justice was arbitrary and based on extreme physical ordeals (Dip).*

**First Court:** Established in Almora, headed by a **Vichari** (judge)

### Types of Ordeals (Dip)

| Ordeal Name | Method |
|---|---|
| **Goladip** | Accused carries a **red-hot iron rod** in bare hands for a fixed distance |
| **Tarajudip** | Accused weighed at night on a scale; if lighter next morning = innocent; if heavier = guilty |
| **Kadhai Dip** | Accused forces hands into a cauldron of **boiling oil** |

### Punishment System

| Crime | Punishment |
|---|---|
| **Treason** | Death penalty |
| **Murder** | Hanging from a tree |
| **General crimes** | Mutilation — cutting off hands or noses |
| **Brahmin offenders** | Death penalty EXEMPT; punished by exile or cutting off **shikha** (tuft of hair) |

> **KEY FACT:** The caste-based judicial inequality (Brahmins exempt from death) reinforced social hierarchy even in the penal system.

### Complete List of Gorkha Taxes

| Tax Name | Description |
|---|---|
| **Pungdi Kar** | Primary land tax |
| **Kushahi Kar** | Specific tax on Brahmins for land acquisition |
| **Manga Kar** | Tax collected specifically during wartime |
| **Tika Bhent Kar** | Levied on marriages and auspicious occasions |
| **Mijhari Kar** | Levied on Jagaris and Brahmins |
| **Maro Kar** | Tax on a person who died without leaving a son |
| **Timari Kar** | Tax given directly to Gorkha Faujdars and Subedars |
| **Sonya Phagun Kar** | Tax levied during festivals |
| **Rakhiya** | Tax during religious ceremonies |

> **EXAM TIP:** Compare Gorkha taxes with Chand "Chhatisi" — both exploitative but Gorkha taxes were more arbitrary and linked to life events (death, marriage, festivals).

## 3.12 SOCIAL & CULTURAL IMPACT OF GORKHA RULE

### New Social Categories Imposed by Gorkhas

| Designation | Community |
|---|---|
| **Kami** | Local craftsmen |
| **Sunuwar** | Goldsmiths |
| **Nau** | Barbers |
| **Kathua** | **Slaves** (formalization of slave class) |

### Cultural Destruction

| Impact | Details |
|---|---|
| **Fear & Superstition** | Divya Pariksha replaced rational justice with terror |
| **Festival Taxation** | Cultural celebrations suffocated by Sonya Phagun Kar and Tika Bhent Kar |
| **Forced Labour** | Begar system intensified beyond Chand-era limits |
| **Caste Reinforcement** | Gorkha penal code deepened social inequality |

### The Most Profound Impact: Paving the Way for British Acceptance

> The Gorkha tyranny was so severe that when the British took control in 1815, their administration appeared **"liberal and reformist"** in comparison. The traumatized population welcomed British rule without immediate mass resistance — this psychological conditioning is the most significant long-term consequence of Gorkhyali.

## 3.13 THE ANGLO-GORKHA WAR & LIBERATION (1814–1815)

### Cause
- Gorkha expansionism clashed with British East India Company interests
- British Governor-General declared war in 1814

### The Battle of Nalapani (Khalanga Fort, Dehradun)

| Parameter | Details |
|---|---|
| **Location** | Nalapani (Khalanga) Fort, Dehradun |
| **British Commander** | Major General **Gillespie** |
| **Gorkha Defender** | Captain **Balbhadra Thapa** |
| **Gorkha Force** | Just **500 soldiers** |
| **Gillespie's Fate** | **Killed in battle** (October 31, 1814) |
| **How British Won** | By cutting off the fort's drinking water supply |
| **Legacy** | British built **Khalanga War Memorial** in Dehradun — honouring BOTH Gillespie AND Balbhadra Thapa |

> **KEY FACT:** The Khalanga War Memorial is unique in military history — it honours both the victors AND the vanquished, testament to the incredible bravery of the 500 Gorkha defenders.

### Treaty of Sugauli

| Parameter | Details |
|---|---|
| **Signed** | Between British and Nepal |
| **Ratified by Nepal** | May 4, 1816 |
| **Result** | Uttarakhand liberated from Gorkha rule |
| **Post-War Division** | Eastern Garhwal (Pauri) → British retained; Western Garhwal → returned to Sudarshan Shah (Tehri State) |

### COMPARATIVE ANALYSIS — KEY INSIGHTS FOR MAINS ANSWERS

**1. Political Consolidation:**
- Katyuris: First to unify the region under one banner
- Parmars: Ajaypal unified 52 Garhs → created 'Garhwal' identity
- Chands: Freed Kumaon from Nepal → centralized at Almora
- Gorkhas: Destroyed both identities; imposed foreign military rule

**2. Economic Systems — Progressive to Regressive:**
```
KATYURI (basic agrarian) → PARMAR (1/3 tax + land grants) → CHAND (1/6 tax + 36 taxes) → GORKHA (arbitrary extortion)
```

**3. Justice Evolution:**
```
KATYURI (King supreme) → PARMAR (Panchayat + Diwan) → CHAND (Two formal courts) → GORKHA (Ordeals/torture)
```
*The Chand system was the most advanced; the Gorkha system was a complete regression.*

**4. Cultural Contribution:**
- Katyuri = Temples (Jageshwar, Dwarahat)
- Parmar = Painting (Garhwal School)
- Chand = Stone carving + Water architecture (Naulas)
- Gorkha = Nil (cultural destruction)
