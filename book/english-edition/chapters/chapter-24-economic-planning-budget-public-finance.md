# CHAPTER 24

# Economic Planning, Budget & Public Finance

## 24.1 Overview: Fiscal Evolution Since Statehood

Uttarakhand's fiscal journey since 2000 represents a transformation from a revenue-deficit, grant-dependent hill state to a revenue-surplus economy with growing self-reliance. At the time of its formation in 2000-01, the state had a budget size of approximately Rs 5,000 crore, own tax revenue of just Rs 233 crore, and was running a revenue deficit. By 2026-27 (Budget Estimates), the budget size has grown to Rs 1,11,703 crore (approximately 22 times), own tax revenue has risen to Rs 25,913 crore (approximately 51 times, with the 2024-25 figure at Rs 11,862 crore), and the state has achieved a structural shift to a revenue surplus of Rs 2,536 crore. The fiscal deficit stands at 2.94 per cent of GSDP, well within the FRBM limit. Capital expenditure has risen from approximately Rs 1,000 crore to Rs 14,763 crore (a record), and the number of GST dealers has reached 2,79,043, with 2,22,461 new registrations since 2017. The Budget crossed Rs 1 lakh crore for the first time in 2026-27.

## 24.2 Budget 2026-27: Macro Fiscal Framework

### Total Size and Structure

The total expenditure proposed in the 2026-27 budget is Rs 1,11,703.21 crore, against total receipts of Rs 1,10,143.13 crore. Revenue receipts amount to Rs 67,525.77 crore, while capital receipts stand at Rs 42,617.35 crore. On the expenditure side, revenue expenditure is Rs 64,989.44 crore and capital expenditure is Rs 46,713.77 crore.

### GSDP Projection

The Gross State Domestic Product for 2026-27 at current prices is projected at Rs 4,27,716 crore, representing 12 per cent growth over the 2025-26 estimate of Rs 3,81,889 crore. The real growth rate was 7.23 per cent in 2025-26 and is projected at 8.2 per cent for 2026-27.

## 24.3 Revenue Receipts: Sources of Income

### Tax Revenue (Largest Source)

Total tax revenue amounts to Rs 43,327.43 crore, which includes both the state's own tax revenue and its share of central taxes. The state's own tax revenue stands at Rs 25,912.86 crore, which is the core metric of fiscal self-reliance. The state's share in central taxes under the 16th Finance Commission devolution is Rs 17,414.57 crore.

The state's revenue comes from, in descending order: own tax revenue (largest), state share in central taxes, public debt (borrowings), and grants-in-aid from the Central Government.

## 24.5 The "Committed Expenditure" Crisis: Critical Analysis

### The Core Problem

| Component | Amount (Rs Cr) | % of Revenue Receipts |
|---|---|---|
| Salaries | ~22,105 | 33% |
| Pensions | ~11,137 | 16% |
| Interest Payments | ~7,929 | 12% |
| Total Committed | ~41,172 | 61% |

With 61 per cent of revenue receipts consumed by non-discretionary, recurring expenses, the state has only 39 per cent fiscal space for actual development, infrastructure, and welfare.

### Analytical Implications

The compressed development space limits the state's ability to scale health, education, and ecological infrastructure. The rising pension burden, driven by early retirement and a large post-2000 recruitment wave, is creating a pension explosion. Debt servicing (Rs 28,161 crore in repayment plus Rs 7,929 crore in interest) constitutes a massive annual outflow. By national comparison, the Central Government allocates 4.4 per cent of GDP to capital expenditure; Uttarakhand's capital expenditure-to-revenue ratio is better but remains constrained by the committed load.

### Way Forward

The state must restructure pension frameworks toward contribution-based models, freeze non-essential department expansions, utilize multi-tasking administrative cadres, and channel the revenue surplus (Rs 2,536 crore) into liquid investment reserves.

## 24.6 Fiscal Deficit, Revenue Surplus and FRBM Compliance

### Key Fiscal Indicators (2026-27)

| Indicator | Value | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue Surplus | Rs 2,536.33 Crore (0.6% of GSDP) | Positive — no revenue deficit |
| Fiscal Deficit | Rs 12,579.70 Crore | — |
| Fiscal Deficit (% of GSDP) | 2.94% | Within FRBM limit of 3% |
| Primary Deficit | ~1.61% | — |
| Debt-to-GSDP Ratio | 24.23% | Healthy (national ~50%) |
| Opening Cash Balance | Rs 753.57 Cr (positive) | — |
| Closing Cash Balance | Rs 18.48 Cr (positive) | — |

### FRBM Act (Fiscal Responsibility and Budget Management)

The FRBM Act was enacted in Uttarakhand in 2005. It sets a fiscal deficit cap of 3 per cent of GSDP, which the state currently meets at 2.94 per cent. The revenue target is zero revenue deficit, which has been achieved with a surplus of Rs 2,536 crore. The debt target requires a declining debt-to-GSDP trajectory.

### Fiscal Health Assessment

The state's strengths include its revenue-surplus status (unlike most Indian states), a low debt-to-GSDP ratio of 24.23 per cent, the tripling of capital expenditure in six years, and FRBM compliance. However, weaknesses persist: 53 per cent of revenue comes from central transfers (indicating dependency), debt repayment obligations are rising (Rs 28,161 crore this year), cash balance reserves have been depleted (100 per cent drawdown in recent cycles), and the fiscal deficit is trending upward (2.94 per cent, approaching the 3 per cent ceiling, which may be breached in stress years).

## 24.7 State Taxes and Taxation Reforms

### Tax Revenue Growth: 51 Times Since 2001

Own tax revenue grew from Rs 233 crore in 2000-01 to Rs 11,862 crore in 2024-25, representing approximately 51 times growth.

### GST Implementation

GST was launched on 1 July 2017. Since its implementation, 2,22,461 new entities have been registered, bringing the total number of GST dealers to 2,79,043. SGST collection reached Rs 7,301.12 crore in 2024-25 (up to December), while total GST collection including IGST and compensation reached Rs 15,617.74 crore.

### Taxation Innovations

The state has introduced several innovations to improve tax compliance and revenue collection. The Bill Lao Inam Pao (BLIPUK App) is a gamified tax compliance initiative where consumers upload bills for prizes including cars and scooters. A Digital Forensic Lab has been established in Dehradun in collaboration with NFSU Gandhinagar to investigate financial irregularities. Artificial intelligence has been integrated for departmental efficiency enhancement. The IFMS Portal Utility allows taxpayers to view deposit details without visiting the treasury. Paperless Registry enables registered documents to be delivered via email or WhatsApp. The e-Nakal system provides verified copies of deeds on mobile or computer from home. The Swabhoomi App facilitates geo-tagging of properties and integrates urban, water, and energy portals with revenue records. The Vyapari Durghatna Bima scheme provides Rs 10 lakh for trader accidental death and has been extended to November 2026. The MDTSS (Mining Digital Transformation and Surveillance System) uses drones, CCTV, and digital gates for mining oversight. A new mining policy introduces transparent online auctions through e-Auction.

### Excise Department

The excise revenue target for 2025-26 is Rs 5,060 crore, against a collection of Rs 4,357.68 crore in 2024-25. Excise is the second largest own-revenue source after GST.

## 24.8 Economic Planning and State Annual Plans

### Planning Framework

The NITI Aayog replaced the Planning Commission in 2015 and plays an advisory role. The State Planning Commission has been merged into the SETU Aayog (Strategic Economic Transformation Unit). State annual plans now operate through annual fiscal statements under the FRBM framework. The state is aligned with the Viksit Bharat 2047 national target, positioning the third decade since statehood as "Uttarakhand's Decade."

### Key Planning Concepts

The SANKALP Model guides planning with its seven pillars: Samagra Vikas (integrated development), Atmanirbharta (self-reliance), Navachar (innovation), Kaushal (skill), Avasarachna (infrastructure), Lok Sahbhagita (people's participation), and Paardershita (transparency). The state follows a Saturation Approach targeting 100 per cent coverage in key areas such as furniture in all schools, e-office in all districts, and heliports in all districts. There has been a shift toward outcome-based budgeting with measurable outcome indicators. The PM Gati Shakti framework, implemented through the Unnati Portal, provides inter-departmental coordination for infrastructure and prevents duplication.

### Finance Commission and Fiscal Transfers

The 16th Finance Commission is ongoing under Chairman Arvind Panagariya. Expected central assistance is approximately Rs 24,000 crore within the Finance Commission frame. A key concern is that competitive urban-challenge funding may disadvantage smaller hill ULBs. Revenue-deficit, sector-specific, and state-specific grants have been discontinued, which increases the importance of disaster grants and local-body grants for Uttarakhand. The 6th State Finance Commission is chaired by N. Ravishankar.

## 24.9 Decentralized Planning: Panchayati Raj and ULBs

### Panchayati Raj Institutions (PRIs)

The budget allocation for Panchayati Raj in 2026-27 is Rs 1,491 crore. The state has 7,227 Gram Panchayats, 95 Kshetra Panchayats (block level), 13 Zila Panchayats, and 662 Nyaya Panchayats. Women's reservation stands at 50 per cent in PRIs and Van Panchayats. The Jan-Jan Ki Sarkar, Jan-Jan Ke Dwar initiative has conducted over 660 camps at the Nyaya Panchayat level, with more than 5.33 lakh citizens participating and over 33,000 grievances resolved on the spot.

### Urban Local Bodies (ULBs)

The budget allocation for ULBs in 2026-27 is Rs 1,814 crore. The total number of ULBs has risen to 108, up from 63 in 2001. Municipal Corporations have increased from 1 to 11, while there are 35 Nagar Palika Parishads and 62 Nagar Panchayats. Development Authorities have grown from 5 to 14.

### Critical Issues in Decentralized Planning

Most ULBs have near-zero own-tax revenue and are entirely grant-dependent. The 16th Finance Commission's competitive and market-linked urban challenge funds may disadvantage smaller hill ULBs. Hill panchayats lack technical staff for Detailed Project Report preparation and project execution. CAG has flagged Planning, Execution, Verification, and Delivery failures at the local level. Of the 11,217 Van Panchayats that exist, many are non-functional due to migration.

### Way Forward (Decentralized Planning)

The path forward includes strengthening GIS-based property mapping for ULB own-revenue generation, exploring municipal bonds for creditworthy hill towns, capacity building via SIRD and PRIA for panchayat-level DPR preparation, linking the Devbhoomi Parivar ID for proactive benefit delivery at the gram level, and implementing outcome-based monitoring via the CM Dashboard and Vidya Samiksha Kendra model.

## 24.11 Structural Issues: Critical Analysis

### Issue 1: Excessive Committed Expenditure (Fiscal Rigidity)

Salaries (Rs 22,105 crore) plus pensions (Rs 11,137 crore) plus interest (Rs 7,929 crore) total Rs 41,172 crore, locking 61 per cent of revenue receipts. This compresses fiscal space for asset creation, health, ecology, and disaster resilience. Any revenue shortfall immediately threatens development spending.

### Issue 2: Fiscal Deficit Trajectory

The fiscal deficit at 2.94 per cent (2026-27) is close to the 3 per cent FRBM ceiling. The previous year revised estimate was 3.62 per cent. The debt repayment mandate of Rs 28,161 crore adds further pressure. In a disaster year (cloudburst or earthquake), emergency borrowing could breach the FRBM limit, requiring special dispensation.

### Issue 3: Depleting Cash Reserves and Central Dependency

State Finance Audit shows 100 per cent drawdown in the Cash Balance Investment Account. Fifty-three per cent of revenue comes from central devolution and grants. This leaves limited independent liquidity buffers for internal shocks and makes the state vulnerable to any delay or reduction in central transfers.

### Issue 4: The Hill-Plain Development Divide

Capital expenditure prioritizes transit projects in plain-adjacent zones. Hill districts face 40 per cent post-harvest loss due to lack of cold-chain infrastructure, a low Credit-Deposit Ratio of 25 per cent, and continued ghost-village formation. Fiscal allocations do not adequately compensate for the "Himalayan Distance Penalty" in implementation costs.

## 24.12 Way Forward: Strategic Solutions

| Solution | Implementation |
|---|---|
| Restructure Pension Frameworks | Shift toward contribution-based models; cap legacy pension growth |
| Monetize Green Assets | Carbon credits + Green Tourism Cess + GEP-linked climate adaptation fund |
| Decentralized Cold-Chain Hubs | Solar-powered cold storage in mountain clusters; curb 40% post-harvest loss |
| Rebuild Cash Reserves | Channel fixed percentage of revenue surplus into liquid investment accounts (target: 5% of revenue receipts) |
| Strengthen ULB Own-Revenue | GIS property mapping, self-certification, municipal bonds, Land Pooling Regulations 2025 |
| Hill-Specific CapEx Formula | Weight infrastructure allocations by terrain difficulty (Category A/B/C/D) not just population |
| Expenditure Controls | Freeze non-essential department expansion; multi-tasking cadres |
| Digital Revenue Enhancement | MDTSS for mining; BLIPUK for consumer-driven tax compliance; e-auction |
