# CHAPTER 22

# Industrial Development, MSME & Startup Ecosystem

## 22.1 OVERVIEW: UTTARAKHAND'S INDUSTRIAL TRANSITION

The industrial sector in Uttarakhand has transitioned from a small public-sector base to a robust, **private-sector-led manufacturing hub**. The Secondary Sector now contributes **39.95%** to the GSDP (2025-26 estimate), with manufacturing alone accounting for **26.24%**.

At state formation in 2000-01, there were only 14,163 registered MSME units; by 2025, this number has grown to 79,394–94,595 — roughly a six-fold increase. MSME employment has expanded approximately eleven-fold, from about 40,000 to 4,56,605–4,63,478. Large industries have grown from 39 to 107. Exports have risen from negligible levels to ₹14,952 Crore (a 400% increase since 2011-12). The state now hosts 1,100–1,750 recognized startups, holds 29 GI Tags, and operates 7 SIDCUL-developed Integrated Industrial Estates.

## 22.2 SIDCUL: THE INDUSTRIAL BACKBONE

### About SIDCUL

The State Infrastructure & Industrial Development Corporation of Uttarakhand Ltd (SIDCUL) was established in 2002 as a Government of Uttarakhand Enterprise with an authorized share capital of ₹50 Crore. Its objective is the overall industrial development of the state and creation of necessary infrastructure — either directly or through Special Purpose Vehicles. SIDCUL serves as the nodal agency for industrial development in the state.

### The Special Federal Package (2003)

The industrial boom was catalyzed by a **10-year Central excise tax holiday combined with income tax exemptions** (2003–2013) that attracted massive corporate investment to the plains. This converted the Haridwar-Pantnagar-Selaqui belt into India's fastest-growing manufacturing corridor for:
- **Automobiles** (Tata Motors, Ashok Leyland, Mahindra, Hero MotoCorp)
- **FMCG** (HUL, Dabur, Nestlé, Patanjali)
- **Pharmaceuticals** (Cipla, Dr. Reddy's, Ranbaxy)

### MSME Policy Provisions (2015 / 2023 Update)

The MSME Policy employs a geographical categorization system, with districts mapped into Categories A, B, C, and D (where A represents the steepest, most remote areas that receive the highest incentives). Key provisions include: DPR Assistance that reimburses 75% of total expenditure for Detailed Project Report formulation; Capital Goods Subsidy of 20%–50% on plant, machinery, and workshop buildings (maximum ₹4 Crore); Stamp Duty reimbursement of 50%–100%; Electricity Duty waiver of 100% for loads up to 500 KW; Interest Subsidy of 2%–4% annually on corporate loans; and Single Window Clearance through investuttarakhand.com, which achieved "Top Achiever" status in BRAP 2024.

### Institutional Support & Branding

The Uttarakhand Handloom & Handicraft Development Council (UHHDC) provides artisan training, while the Uttarakhand Khadi and Village Industries Board (UKVIB) promotes cottage sector employment. The Himadri brand markets UK handicrafts in other states, and the House of Himalayas serves as the flagship umbrella brand for SHG products including millets, spices, honey, Aipan, and Pichhora. NIFT/NID collaboration is modernizing traditional designs for contemporary global markets. The Uttarakhand Oon Yojana (wool scheme) operates through 20 centres producing Shawls, Pankhi, Tweed, Thulmas, and Blankets. State Craft Awards offer cash prizes of ₹15,000 at the state level and ₹6,000 at the district level. Organic Woollen Growth Centres established in 2019 have imported Merino sheep for high-quality organic wool production.

### GI Tags (29 Total)

Key GI-tagged products include: Aipan, Ringal craft, Badri Cow Ghee, Berinag Tea, Malta, Bedu, Almora Copper, Munsyari Rajma, and local copper products. A 30–40% value increase has been reported after GI certification, while 400% export growth is linked to the broader branding strategy.

## 22.6 INVESTMENT SCENARIO — MoUs, FDI & GROUNDING

### Global Investors Summit (GIS)

The Global Investors Summit 2023, themed "Peace to Prosperity," resulted in 1,779 MoUs signed with a total value of ₹3.5–3.56 Lakh Crore. Investment grounded amounts to approximately ₹17,743 Crore immediately, with ₹40,000–1,00,000 Crore in the pipeline. These investments have created over 4.6 Lakh job opportunities. Through the Single Window system, 15,917 Common Application Forms (CAFs) have been approved with a proposed value of ₹76,540 Crore.

### Foreign Direct Investment (FDI)

Tourism FDI is permitted at 100% under the automatic route. Germany has signed an MoU through the Innovation Hub RheinMain partnership, while Iceland has entered a geothermal energy agreement with Verkis Consulting Engineers (2025). Key FDI sectors include green energy, ropeways, logistics parks, and wellness tourism.

### Investment Festival 2025

A Strategic Advisory Committee has been formed, with export-policy references and incubation centres actively functioning. The state achieved "Top Achievers" category in BRAP 2024.

## 22.7 STARTUP ECOSYSTEM — THE "INNOVATION VAULT"

The state currently hosts 1,100–1,750 recognized startups, growing from zero at state formation to a national contender. The Uttarakhand Start-up Policy 2023 targets 1,000 new tech/innovation startups in five years. A ₹200 Crore Venture Capital Fund (growth-stage; LP model via SEBI-registered AIFs) supports the ecosystem. Patent filing costs are reimbursed at 100% (up to ₹1–5 Lakh), and a monthly sustenance allowance is provided for early-stage founders.

### Incubation & Infrastructure

The Hub & Spoke Model features a massive incubation centre at IT Park, Dehradun, with at least one incubator per district. The Devbhoomi Udhyamita Yojana transforms college campuses into Innovation & Start-up Labs. Rani Bagh HMT has been proposed as a dedicated IT Hub. An IT start-up hub has been established in collaboration with STPI. Host institutions include IIT Roorkee, UPES Dehradun, and Pantnagar University.

### SHG-to-Enterprise Transition

Startup culture is envisioned as a pathway to convert SHGs into formal enterprises — linking the 68,000+ women's SHGs with incubation support, branding through House of Himalayas, and export market access.

## 22.8 PROBLEMS OF INDUSTRIALIZATION — CRITICAL ANALYSIS

### Structural Issues

The **Geographical Industrial Divide** means over 80% of industries are concentrated in 3 plain districts, with 10 hill districts largely excluded. The **"Plains Paradox"** sees Haridwar and US Nagar generating high GDP but ranking lowest on the SDG Index (13th and 9th respectively) due to civic strain from rapid industrialization. **Ecological and Seismic Fragility** is a critical concern with 90% of the state falling in Seismic Zones IV and V, where blasting and heavy engineering weakens the Main Central Thrust zones.

The **Logistical "Distance Penalty"** arises from the absence of rail in high hills, winding single-lane roads, and maintenance costs 3–4 times higher than on the plains. The **Capital Flight (CD Ratio Crisis)** sees hill savings funding loans in the plains — Bageshwar's CD Ratio is just 24.66% and Almora's is 26.97%. **Brain Drain** leads the productive age group to migrate to plain-area industrial clusters.

The **PEVD Paradox** has been flagged by CAG, noting poorly drafted DPRs leading to mid-way project abandonment. **Environmental Clearance Delays** caused by forest diversion requirements and stringent EIA norms result in project timeline slippage. The **Employability Gap** emerges from traditional education failing to match modern tech industry demands in AI, IoT, and Robotics. **Human-Wildlife Conflict** disrupts industrial and agricultural activities in hill-fringe areas. **Traditional Craft Decline** stems from Chinese competition and labour migration, weakening Almora's copperware and carpet sectors.

## 22.9 MEGA INDUSTRIAL & INVESTMENT POLICY 2025 — PROVISIONS

### Classification of Enterprises

| Category | Capital Investment (excl. land) | Minimum Employment |
|---|---|---|
| Large | ₹50–200 Crore | 50 employees |
| Ultra Large | ₹200–500 Crore | 150 employees |
| Mega | ₹500–1,000 Crore | 300 employees |
| Ultra Mega | Above ₹1,000 Crore | 500 employees |

### Key Provisions

The policy offers a Capital Subsidy of up to 20% (maximum ₹400 Crore) for Ultra Mega investments, with an additional 1–2% capital subsidy as a Hill Incentive for industries in Category A and B mountain districts. Stamp Duty is reimbursed at 100% for new units. Excise Exemption of 100% is available for 10 years, along with Corporate Tax Exemption of up to 30% for new units.

Interest Subvention for 3 years is structured by category: Category A receives 3% (max ₹4 Lakh/year), Category B receives 3% (max ₹5 Lakh/year), and Category C receives 3% (max ₹6 Lakh/year). Priority sectors include food processing, wine making, medicinal herbs, and Pirul products. Centralized Single Window clearance operates via investuttarakhand.com with a 3–7 year execution timeline. The policy also provides Flatted Factories comprising 43,000 sq m of plug-and-play space in Haridwar, Selakui, and US Nagar, along with worker housing through rent-based accommodation via PPP in Haridwar, Kashipur, and Pantnagar.

## 22.10 WAY FORWARD — THE SANTULAN INDUSTRIAL STRATEGY

The state's industrial strategy going forward emphasizes several complementary approaches. **Hill Incentives** provide financial compensation for corporations willing to operate in mountainous terrain. The **"High-Value, Low-Volume"** approach focuses on MAPs, AYUSH, IT, Drones, and AI to bypass topographical constraints. **Aroma Valleys** cultivating Lemongrass and Damask Rose offer ideal hill-terrain solutions since these crops naturally resist wildlife.

**Monetizing Ecology** through GEP and the ₹10,000 Crore "Green Bonus" for carbon-sink services represents a new revenue paradigm. **Ropeway Freight** under Parvatmala enables swift evacuation of high-value goods from slopes. **Digital Financial Inclusion** through 100% PACS computerization ensures credit reaches remote blocks. **Functional Consolidation** through FPOs and cooperative models provides scale in processing and marketing.

**Skill Upgradation** follows the Hub & Spoke Model integrating ITIs, Polytechnics, and Higher Education with courses in AI, IoT, and Robotics. The **Pirul Strategy** involves pine needle collection at ₹10/kg, converting it to biomass energy while preventing forest fires. **MDTSS** (Mining Digital Transformation & Surveillance System) deploys drones and CCTV to combat illegal mining. The **Silver Economy** concept envisions converting ghost villages into Senior Living Communities with youth employed as Care Sahayaks.
