# CHAPTER 5

# The Tehri Princely State (1815–1949)

## 5.1 FOUNDATION OF TEHRI STATE

After the British defeated the Gorkhas in 1815, the conquered territory was divided using the Alaknanda River as the boundary. The region east of the Alaknanda was incorporated into the Kumaon Commissionerate under direct British administration, while the territory west of the Alaknanda (excluding Dehradun and Rawain) was returned to the Parmar dynasty. Sudarshan Shah, son of the martyred Pradyumna Shah, founded the Tehri Princely State on December 28, 1815. He established his capital at the confluence of the Bhagirathi and Bhilangana rivers. The name 'Tehri' is derived from 'Trihari,' meaning a place that washes away three types of sins — those of thought (Mansa), speech (Vacha), and deed (Karmana). To secure his dynasty under British paramountcy, Sudarshan Shah signed a treaty with the British on March 4, 1820.

## 5.3 ADMINISTRATIVE STRUCTURE

### The King's Title and Status

The king of Tehri bore the exalted title of **Bolanda Badrish** (Speaking Badrinath), reflecting the people's reverence for the monarch as an incarnation of God. His authority was supreme and absolute, functioning as a feudal monarchy under British paramountcy.

### Central Council (Council of Ministers)

The central administration was headed by the **Mukhtaar** (Chief Minister), supported by the **Wazir** or **Diwan** (Council of Ministers handling policy and military affairs), the **Bakshi** (Messenger/Dispatcher), and the **Daftari** (in-charge of Royal Orders).

### Territorial Division

The state was divided into administrative tiers. At the top was the King (Bolanda Badrish), below whom the territory was organized into **Thanos** (headed by Thanedars), which were further divided into **Parganas or Mandals** (headed by Faujdars), then into **Pattis** (headed by Kanungos), and finally into **Villages** (managed by Patwaris and Pradhans).

### Judicial System

The King served as the supreme and final judicial authority. Tehri's legal system was formalized through the **Narendra Hindu Law**, compiled in 1918 by **Pandit Harikrishna Raturi** (published 1917). This represented the first formal codification of Hindu personal law in a princely state of the region. Raturi also authored *Garhwal ka Itihas* (History of Garhwal), a foundational historical text.

## 5.4 ECONOMY AND TAXATION

### Land Ownership

All land in the state was ultimately owned by the King. The land classification system continued from the Parmar era and included several types of grants. **Vishnupriti** lands were tax-free donations to Brahmins or temples. **Raut** lands were awarded to soldiers for bravery. **Jagir** lands were given to state officials in lieu of salary.

### Major Taxes

The primary agricultural tax was the **Tihar**, which claimed one-third of total production. The **Sirti** served as the main cash income source for the state. **Bhumikar** was a land tax paid in cash. **Punituti** (also called Paud Tuti) was an import-export tax on goods crossing state borders. The most despised levy was **Bara-Begar**, a deeply exploitative system of forced unpaid labour exacted by officials.

### Trade System

Commerce operated largely through the **Dhakari System**, a barter arrangement in which villagers exchanged ghee, oil, and spices for cloth, salt, and jaggery. Trans-Himalayan trade flourished through traders from the Niti and Mana valleys who actively traded with Tibet. The **Muafidars** (revenue-free grantees) formed part of the oppressive bureaucratic class that contributed to popular discontent.

## 5.5 SOCIETY, RELIGION AND CULTURE

### Social Structure

Tehri's society was characterized by a deep divide between the oppressive monarchical bureaucracy (including muafidars) and the suffering peasantry. Forced labour (Begar) was a major source of social tension, and rural society remained dominated by traditional caste hierarchies.

### Religious Patronage

Kirti Shah organized the **Sarvdharma Sammelan** (Interfaith Conference) in 1902 and sponsored Swami Ram Tirth's attendance at an international religious conference in Japan. The state's key temples included Surkanda Devi, Chandrabadni, and Kunjapuri. The Sem Mukhem Nagraja Temple signifies the deep roots of Naga worship in the region.

### Art and Literature

The **Garhwal School of Painting** was kept alive during this period, with prominent artists **Chaitu and Manku** carrying forward the tradition. Sudarshan Shah himself was a distinguished poet, producing the 7-volume *Sabhasar Granth* under the pen name 'Surat Kavi.' Harikrishna Raturi contributed both the Narendra Hindu Law (1918) and *Garhwal ka Itihas* to the literary heritage of the state.

### Growth of Vernacular Journalism

The **Garhwali** newspaper was first published from Dehradun in 1905 with **Girija Dutt Naithani** as its first editor. It served as the mouthpiece of the Garhwal Union and focused on social reforms. The **Pratap Printing Press**, established by Pratap Shah in the 1880s, enabled local publishing for the first time.

## 5.7 THE TILARI / RAWAIN MASSACRE (May 30, 1930)

The Tilari Massacre stands as the most significant act of state violence in Tehri's history, often called the **"Jallianwala Bagh of Uttarakhand."** The immediate trigger was a set of new forest laws introduced in 1927–28 under Narendra Shah that severely restricted traditional grazing rights, fodder collection, fuelwood gathering, and community festivals. In response, the people formed an **'Azad Panchayat'** and gathered for an unarmed protest meeting at the Tiladi ground on the banks of the Yamuna River.

On May 30, 1930, Tehri's Diwan **Chakradhar Juyal** ordered indiscriminate firing on the unarmed crowd, resulting in a massacre of farmers. For this act of brutality, Chakradhar Juyal earned the epithet **"General Dyer of Tehri."** May 30 has been observed as **Martyr's Day** ever since.

## 5.8 TEHRI RAJYA PRAJA MANDAL AND SRIDEV SUMAN

### Praja Mandal

The **Tehri Rajya Praja Mandal** was founded on January 23, 1939, in Dehradun by **Sridev Suman** and his associates. Its objective was to demand a responsible, democratic government under the King's patronage, drawing inspiration from Gandhian methods of non-violent protest.

### Sridev Suman — The Supreme Martyr

Sridev Suman was a staunch admirer of Gandhi and an adherent of non-violence. After being arrested for his activism against the Tehri monarchy, he undertook an **84-day fast-unto-death** in Tehri jail and was martyred on **July 25, 1944**, at the age of 28. His death catalyzed mass rebellions, including the Saklana Revolt (1947) and Kirtinagar Revolt (1948). His legacy endures through Sridev Suman Uttarakhand University, and he is considered the greatest martyr of Uttarakhand's princely state era.

## 5.9 THE FINAL REVOLTS AND MERGER

### Saklana Revolt (1947)

The Saklana Revolt was a peasant uprising led by **Nagendra Saklani**, a Communist Party of India leader, supported by Dada Daulat Ram. Nagendra Saklani was killed on **January 11, 1948**, while defending the liberated Kirtinagar division from royal forces. The revolt highlighted the CPI's role in the Tehri liberation movement.

### Kirtinagar Revolt (January 1948)

Revolutionaries seized the Kirtinagar division in January 1948. **Moluram** and others were martyred when the King's forces attempted to retake the area. The immense public pressure generated by these revolts forced the monarchy to concede.

### The Merger

Under immense public and political pressure, the last king **Manvendra Shah** signed the merger agreement on **May 18, 1948**. The Instrument of Accession declared: "The Maharaja of Tehri-Garhwal hereby cedes to the Dominion Government full and exclusive authority..." On **August 1, 1949**, the Tehri State was legally dissolved and merged into the United Provinces (Uttar Pradesh) as an independent district. Tehri was one of the last princely states to merge with India — over two years after independence.
