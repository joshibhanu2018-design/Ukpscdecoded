## 28.1 CORE HEALTH INDICATORS (IMR, MMR, TFR)

Uttarakhand has achieved significant public health progress, dropping its mortality markers below the national average and stabilizing its demographic profile. The 80% decline in MMR since 2000 is cited as the state's greatest socio-economic victory.

### Key Analysis

| Achievement | Details |
|---|---|
| **IMR Reduction** | Near 30% reduction from NFHS-5; now well below national baseline (20 vs. 25) |
| **MMR Decline** | 80% decline since 2000 (398 → 91); driven by surge in institutional deliveries and expanded infrastructure |
| **TFR Stabilization** | Below national sub-replacement level (2.1); consolidated demographic transition across hill and plain areas |
| **Institutional Deliveries** | Tripled from 30% to 93.86%; key driver of mortality decline |
| **Immunization** | Doubled from 47% to 96%; near-universal coverage achieved |

> **KEY FACT BOX: The "Himalayan Healthcare Revolution"**
>
> The 80% decline in MMR (from 398 to 91) is driven by three factors:
> 1. **Khushiyon Ki Sawari** — free ambulance service for pregnant women
> 2. **HEMS** — Heli-Emergency Medical Service bypassing terrain during "Golden Hour"
> 3. **93.86% institutional deliveries** — near-universal hospital-based births
>
> Uttarakhand now has an IMR **below** the national average (20 vs. 25), a remarkable achievement for a mountainous state.

## 28.3 THE ANAEMIA GAP AND BIOMARKER MODIFICATIONS

### The NFHS-6 Anaemia Data Deletion

| Parameter | Details |
|---|---|
| **Decision** | Ministry of Health and Family Welfare dropped all traditional anaemia tracking metrics from NFHS-6 fact sheets |
| **Rationale** | Blood-iron assessments shifted to the specialized **Diet and Biomarkers Survey in India (DABS-I)** |
| **Impact** | Direct comparison impossible; policymakers must rely on NFHS-5 baseline |

### Historical Reference (NFHS-5 Baseline)

| Category | Anaemia Prevalence (NFHS-5) |
|---|---|
| **Children (under 5)** | 58.8% clinically anaemic |
| **Women of Reproductive Age (15-49)** | 42.6% to 53% anaemic |
| **Status** | Flagged as a public health crisis |

### Government Response to Anaemia

| Initiative | Details |
|---|---|
| **Fortified Rice** | Enriched with Iron, Folic Acid, and Vitamin B-12; distributed via PDS |
| **Millet Integration** | 1 kg free Mandua (finger millet) monthly to PHH cardholders; high in iron and calcium |
| **Salt Nutrition Scheme** | Iodized salt at ₹8/kg to 14 lakh cardholders |
| **Aanchal Amrit Yojana** | Fortified milk (Vitamin A & D) to children in Anganwadis |
| **Mission Poshan 2.0** | Real-time monitoring of 7.33 lakh beneficiaries via Poshan Tracker app |

> **KEY FACT BOX: The Anaemia Crisis**
>
> While NFHS-6 dropped anaemia data (shifted to DABS-I), the NFHS-5 figures reveal a severe crisis: **58.8%** of children under 5 and over **42.6%** of women are anaemic. The state is combating this through bio-fortification (fortified rice + millet + iodized salt) rather than just caloric welfare.

## 28.5 MODERNIZATION AND EMERGENCY LOGISTICS

The government is aggressively shifting toward technology-led solutions to bypass the "Golden Hour" geographic penalty.

### Emergency and Logistic Initiatives

| Initiative | Details |
|---|---|
| **Heli-Emergency Medical Service (HEMS)** | Uttarakhand is the **first state in India** to launch helicopter-based emergency services from AIIMS Rishikesh; ensures air-lifts during "Golden Hour" from remote hills |
| **Drone Medical Service** | AIIMS Rishikesh pioneers regular drone delivery of blood components, anti-venoms, and medicines to remote/cutoff zones |
| **e-Sanjeevani (Telemedicine)** | Network of 1,500 'spokes'; conducted over **15.15 lakh** virtual consultations; connects rural patients to city specialists |
| **Mobile Medical Units (MMUs)** | 5 units deployed in Haridwar, US Nagar, and Tehri Garhwal for doorstep primary care |
| **108 Jeevan Vahini** | ALS and BLS ambulances stationed at rugged bottlenecks; free critical patient transport |
| **Khushiyon Ki Sawari** | Fleet of ambulances providing free transport for pregnant women; 40,000+ beneficiaries |
| **Doli Seva** | Financial support for transporting patients on manual stretchers (litters) in roadless areas |
| **Tele-MANAS** | Mental health tele-counseling cells operationalized in Srinagar and Almora medical colleges |

> **KEY FACT BOX: The "Air-Bridge Strategy"**
>
> Uttarakhand uses three tiers to bypass terrain:
> 1. **HEMS** (Helicopters) — for critical trauma and maternity emergencies
> 2. **Drones** (DaaS) — for blood components and medicines to cutoff zones
> 3. **e-Sanjeevani** (Telemedicine) — 15.15 lakh consultations connecting villages to city specialists
>
> This "Phygital" model ensures healthcare reaches patients even when roads are blocked by landslides or monsoon damage.

### B. Ayushman Bharat — Atal Ayushman Uttarakhand Yojana (AAUY)

Uttarakhand was among the earliest states to expand the national mission into a universal state-wide program.

| Parameter | Details |
|---|---|
| **Coverage** | Universal — covers **entire state** (unlike national AB which covers selected groups) |
| **Cards Issued** | ~60 lakh Ayushman "Golden Cards" |
| **Insurance Amount** | ₹5 Lakh per year per family for cashless treatment |
| **Budget** | ₹600 crore allocated in latest cycle |
| **Model Shift** | From trust-based model to **insurance-based, audit-heavy model** |
| **Purpose of Shift** | Reduce inflated billing by private hospitals; standardize claim procedures; improve financial discipline |

### Analysis: Issues with the Model Shift

| Issue | Details |
|---|---|
| **Paperwork Burden** | Insurance model introduces administrative complexity |
| **Claim Rejections** | Concerns over delayed payments and rejections to hospitals |
| **Fiscal Friction** | Transition period causing temporary disruptions in service delivery |

> **EXAM TIP:** Remember the distinction: **National Ayushman Bharat** covers selected BPL/vulnerable groups; **Atal Ayushman Uttarakhand Yojana (AAUY)** provides **universal** coverage to ALL permanent residents of the state. Budget: ₹600 crore. Cards: ~60 lakh.

## 28.11 CRITICAL ISSUES IN THE HEALTH SECTOR

Despite statistical growth, the "Himalayan Distance Penalty" creates deep-seated implementation gaps.

### Master Issues Table

| Issue | Details |
|---|---|
| **"Golden Hour" Geographic Penalty** | Transport time to tertiary centres exceeds critical "Golden Hour" in high Himalayas (Pithoragarh, Chamoli); leads to preventable fatalities during trauma/maternity emergencies |
| **"Referral Center" Syndrome (PEVD Failure)** | PHCs/CHCs have buildings but lack specialist doctors (surgeons, gynecologists) and functional diagnostics (X-ray/Ultrasound); reduced to mere transit points |
| **Hidden Nutritional Crisis** | 42.6–50% women anaemic; 27% children stunted; "nutritional poverty" in remote districts despite overall health ranks |
| **Aging Population Burden** | Youth out-migration (Palaayan) leaves elderly in "ghost villages" with chronic geriatric needs; standard NHM centres not equipped |
| **Personnel Shortage** | Chronic shortage of specialists in hill districts; heavy reliance on plains medical hubs; doctor ratio still only 23 per lakh |
| **Fiscal Friction (Insurance Model)** | Shift to insurance-based Ayushman introduces paperwork burden; claim rejections and payment delays to hospitals |
| **Dietary Quality** | Only 15.3% of children (6-23 months) receive adequate diet nationally; impacts stunting in remote blocks |
| **Adult Double Burden** | Rising obesity, diabetes, hypertension in urban plains while child undernutrition persists in hills |
| **Infrastructure Gaps** | Many sub-centres lack basic facilities; long distances between facilities in mountain terrain |

## 28.12 WAY FORWARD — STRATEGIC SOLUTIONS

The state is adopting a **"Phygital" (Physical + Digital)** approach to overcome geographic limitations.

### Strategic Solution Framework

| Solution | Mechanism | Impact |
|---|---|---|
| **Drone Medical Services (DaaS)** | Scale AIIMS Rishikesh model for regular drone delivery of blood, anti-venoms, emergency medicines | Bypasses road blockages; reaches cutoff zones in minutes |
| **HEMS Expansion** | Scale helicopter emergency to all districts; not just AIIMS Rishikesh | Eliminates "Golden Hour" penalty for remote areas |
| **Hub-and-Spoke Telemedicine** | Expand e-Sanjeevani (15.15 lakh consultations); connect village "spokes" with city specialist "hubs" | Specialist care without patient travel |
| **Hill-Specific Service Norms** | Replace national population-based thresholds with **accessibility-based norms** (distance + terrain difficulty) | Health facility viability measured by geography, not just population |
| **Caregiver Skill Mission** | Train local youth as "Care Sahayaks" for elderly in "ghost villages" | Circular economy of care; prevents further migration |
| **First Medicity (Haridwar)** | Super-specialty hospital + nursing/paramedical colleges | Permanent specialized tertiary capacity |
| **Organ and Milk Banks** | Established in all government medical colleges | Specialized neonatal and transplant services |
| **"You Quote, We Pay"** | Specialists bid own salary for remote postings (up to ₹6 lakh/month) | Overcomes chronic specialist shortage |
| **Preventive NCD Screening** | Door-to-door ASHA worker screening for 17.50 lakh individuals | Early detection of "silent killers" (diabetes, hypertension, cancer) |
| **Bio-Fortification via PDS** | Fortified rice + free millet + subsidized iodized salt | Combat anaemia and stunting through food system, not just medical system |

### The "Phygital" Healthcare Model

```
PHYSICAL LAYER                          DIGITAL LAYER
├── 1,914 Sub-Centres                   ├── e-Sanjeevani (15.15 lakh consultations)
├── 524 PHCs + 81 CHCs                  ├── Poshan Tracker (7.33 lakh beneficiaries)
├── 1,554 Ayushman Arogya Mandirs       ├── Tele-MANAS (mental health)
├── 9 Medical Colleges                  ├── 104 National Health Helpline
├── HEMS + Drone Services               ├── e-PoS (9,063 automated shops)
└── Mobile Medical Units                └── e-KYC (eliminated ghost beneficiaries)
```

> **KEY FACT BOX: The Three Phases of Uttarakhand Healthcare**
>
> 1. **Phase 1 (2000–2013):** Basic access and infrastructure creation (Sub-Centres, PHCs)
> 2. **Phase 2 (2013–2020):** Universal coverage and insurance (Ayushman Bharat, NHM upgrades, 93% institutional deliveries)
> 3. **Phase 3 (2020–Present):** "Phygital" Revolution — HEMS, Drones, Telemedicine, Bio-fortification, Hill-specific norms
>
> The state aims to achieve a **"Zero Preventable Death"** target by combining air-bridge logistics with accessibility-based facility planning.
