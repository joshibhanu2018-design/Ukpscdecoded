# Health in Uttarakhand

## 28.1 CORE HEALTH INDICATORS (IMR, MMR, TFR)

Uttarakhand has achieved significant public health progress, dropping its mortality markers below the national average and stabilizing its demographic profile. The 80% decline in MMR since 2000 is cited as the state's greatest socio-economic victory, driven principally by the Khushiyon Ki Sawari free ambulance service for pregnant women, the Heli-Emergency Medical Service (HEMS) bypassing terrain during the "Golden Hour," and 93.86% institutional deliveries ensuring near-universal hospital-based births. Uttarakhand now has an IMR below the national average (20 vs. 25), a remarkable achievement for a mountainous state.

### Key Analysis

The IMR has seen a near 30% reduction from NFHS-5 and now stands well below the national baseline at 20 versus 25. The MMR has declined by 80% since 2000, falling from 398 to 91, driven by the surge in institutional deliveries and expanded infrastructure. The TFR has stabilized below the national sub-replacement level of 2.1, consolidating the demographic transition across both hill and plain areas. Institutional deliveries tripled from 30% to 93.86%, serving as the key driver of mortality decline, while immunization coverage doubled from 47% to 96%, achieving near-universal coverage.

Uttarakhand now outperforms national averages in all four indicators.

## 28.3 THE ANAEMIA GAP AND BIOMARKER MODIFICATIONS

### The NFHS-6 Anaemia Data Deletion

The Ministry of Health and Family Welfare dropped all traditional anaemia tracking metrics from NFHS-6 fact sheets. Blood-iron assessments were shifted to the specialized Diet and Biomarkers Survey in India (DABS-I), making direct comparison impossible and requiring policymakers to rely on the NFHS-5 baseline.


### Historical Reference (NFHS-5 Baseline)

According to NFHS-5, 58.8% of children under 5 were clinically anaemic, while 42.6% to 53% of women of reproductive age (15-49) were anaemic — figures flagged as a public health crisis.

### Government Response to Anaemia

The state is combating anaemia through bio-fortification rather than just caloric welfare. Key initiatives include the distribution of fortified rice enriched with Iron, Folic Acid, and Vitamin B-12 via the PDS; millet integration providing 1 kg of free Mandua (finger millet) monthly to PHH cardholders for its high iron and calcium content; a Salt Nutrition Scheme offering iodized salt at ₹8/kg to 14 lakh cardholders; the Aanchal Amrit Yojana providing fortified milk (Vitamin A and D) to children in Anganwadis; and Mission Poshan 2.0 enabling real-time monitoring of 7.33 lakh beneficiaries via the Poshan Tracker app.

## 28.5 MODERNIZATION AND EMERGENCY LOGISTICS

The government is aggressively shifting toward technology-led solutions to bypass the "Golden Hour" geographic penalty. Uttarakhand uses a three-tiered "Air-Bridge Strategy" to overcome terrain: HEMS (helicopters) for critical trauma and maternity emergencies, drones for blood components and medicines to cutoff zones, and e-Sanjeevani (telemedicine) providing 15.15 lakh consultations connecting villages to city specialists. This "Phygital" model ensures healthcare reaches patients even when roads are blocked by landslides or monsoon damage.

### Emergency and Logistic Initiatives

| Initiative | Details |

## 28.6 NATIONAL HEALTH MISSION (NHM) AND AYUSHMAN BHARAT

### A. National Health Mission (NHM)

NHM serves as the operational backbone for rural healthcare delivery, focusing on infrastructure upgrades and maternal-child safety. Under NHM, 1,554 Ayushman Arogya Mandirs have been established out of a target of 2,160, providing comprehensive primary care. Infrastructure includes 1,914 Sub-Centres, 524 PHCs (Type A), and 81 CHCs. Institutional deliveries have reached 93.86% (up from 30% in 2000) and the MMR has declined by 80% since state formation. NCD screening through door-to-door visits targets 17.50 lakh individuals for diabetes, hypertension, and cancer. The innovative "You Quote, We Pay" programme allows specialist doctors to bid their own salary (up to ₹6 lakh/month) to serve in remote districts.

### B. Ayushman Bharat — Atal Ayushman Uttarakhand Yojana (AAUY)

Uttarakhand was among the earliest states to expand the national Ayushman Bharat mission into a universal state-wide program. Unlike the national scheme which covers selected BPL/vulnerable groups, AAUY provides universal coverage to all permanent residents of the state. Approximately 60 lakh Ayushman "Golden Cards" have been issued, providing ₹5 lakh per year per family for cashless treatment. The budget allocation stands at ₹600 crore in the latest cycle. The scheme has shifted from a trust-based model to an insurance-based, audit-heavy model to reduce inflated billing by private hospitals, standardize claim procedures, and improve financial discipline.

However, the model shift has introduced challenges including increased paperwork burden from the insurance model's administrative complexity, concerns over delayed payments and claim rejections to hospitals, and temporary disruptions in service delivery during the transition period.

### Nutritional Bio-Fortification Innovations

The PDS now actively combats malnutrition through fortified rice enriched with Iron, Folic Acid, and Vitamin B-12; millet integration providing 1 kg of free local Mandua (finger millet) monthly to PHH cardholders to boost iron and calcium intake; and a Salt Nutrition Scheme supplying iodized salt at subsidized ₹8/kg to approximately 14 lakh cardholders.

### Technological Automation

The state has achieved 100% automation of its 9,063 Fair Price Shops through e-PoS systems. Uttarakhand ranks 6th in India for foodgrain distribution under the One Nation One Ration Card (ONORC) scheme. Mandatory e-KYC enforcement led to the cancellation of approximately 50,000 fraudulent "ghost beneficiary" ration cards.

### Social Protection Coverage

According to MoSPI rankings, Uttarakhand ranks among the top 4 states in India for social welfare outreach, with 77.2% of the population covered by at least one social protection or food security scheme. However, a dietary quality gap persists: only 15.3% of children aged 6–23 months receive an adequate diet nationally, directly impacting pocket-level stunting in remote blocks of Chamoli and Uttarkashi.

### Mahalaxmi Kit — Eligibility Details

The Mahalaxmi Kit targets pregnant women registered at Anganwadi Centres whose family income is below ₹6,000/month and who are non-income tax payers. It covers the first two girl children, with a special rule for female twins. The Mother's Kit contains almonds, dates, sanitary pads, mustard oil, towels, warm shawl, bedsheets, socks, and a nail cutter. The Baby's Kit includes cotton and warm clothing, baby blanket, rubber sheets, diapers, baby oil/powder/cream, soap, and a vaccination card.

### Vatsalya Yojana — Key Features

The Mukhyamantri Vatsalya Yojana provides ₹3,000 monthly DBT until age 21, completely free school and college education, monthly zero-cost ration tracking, and legal protection of ancestral property (with the District Magistrate given authority to freeze ancestral property from sale until the child turns adult). Most distinctively, it provides 5% horizontal reservation in government jobs for registered orphans — making Uttarakhand the first state to enact orphan employment reservation.

## 28.10 THE ADULT "DOUBLE BURDEN" — NCD TRANSITION

While child undernutrition is improving, NFHS-6 data exposes a rising lifestyle disease curve among adults. Obesity, diabetes, and hypertension are rising across urban plains (Dehradun, Haridwar), as the disease burden shifts from infectious diseases to Non-Communicable Diseases (NCDs). The government is responding with door-to-door NCD screening via ASHA workers targeting 17.50 lakh individuals for diabetes, hypertension, and cancer, along with the Nephro Dialysis Project providing district-level subsidized dialysis for CKD patients and the Cardiac Care Project establishing telemetry-linked coronary care units in sub-district hospitals.

A significant regional disparity exists: plain districts like Haridwar lag in health and sanitation (SDG Rank 13th) due to rapid urbanization, while hill districts such as Rudraprayag and Chamoli surge to top SDG rankings due to better maternal health outcomes.


## 28.11 CRITICAL ISSUES IN THE HEALTH SECTOR

Despite statistical growth, the "Himalayan Distance Penalty" creates deep-seated implementation gaps.

### Master Issues Table

| Issue | Details |
|---|---|
| **"Golden Hour" Geographic Penalty** | Transport time to tertiary centres exceeds critical "Golden Hour" in high Himalayas (Pithoragarh, Chamoli); leads to preventable fatalities during trauma/maternity emergencies |
| **"Referral Center" Syndrome (PEVD Failure)** | PHCs/CHCs have buildings but lack specialist doctors (surgeons, gynecologists) and functional diagnostics (X-ray/Ultrasound); reduced to mere transit points |
| **Hidden Nutritional Crisis** | 42.6–50% women anaemic; 27% children stunted; "nutritional poverty" in remote districts despite overall health ranks |
| **Aging Population Burden** | Youth out-migration (Palaayan) leaves elderly in "ghost villages" with chronic geriatric needs; standard NHM centres not equipped |
| **Personnel Shortage** | Chronic shortage of specialists in hill districts; heavy reliance on plains medical hubs; doctor ratio still only 23 per lakh |
| **Fiscal Friction (Insurance Model)** | Shift to insurance-based Ayushman introduces paperwork burden; claim rejections and payment delays to hospitals |
| **Dietary Quality** | Only 15.3% of children (6-23 months) receive adequate diet nationally; impacts stunting in remote blocks |
| **Adult Double Burden** | Rising obesity, diabetes, hypertension in urban plains while child undernutrition persists in hills |
| **Infrastructure Gaps** | Many sub-centres lack basic facilities; long distances between facilities in mountain terrain |

## 28.12 WAY FORWARD — STRATEGIC SOLUTIONS

The state is adopting a "Phygital" (Physical + Digital) approach to overcome geographic limitations.

### Strategic Solution Framework

| Solution | Mechanism | Impact |
|---|---|---|
| **Drone Medical Services (DaaS)** | Scale AIIMS Rishikesh model for regular drone delivery of blood, anti-venoms, emergency medicines | Bypasses road blockages; reaches cutoff zones in minutes |
| **HEMS Expansion** | Scale helicopter emergency to all districts; not just AIIMS Rishikesh | Eliminates "Golden Hour" penalty for remote areas |
| **Hub-and-Spoke Telemedicine** | Expand e-Sanjeevani (15.15 lakh consultations); connect village "spokes" with city specialist "hubs" | Specialist care without patient travel |
| **Hill-Specific Service Norms** | Replace national population-based thresholds with accessibility-based norms (distance + terrain difficulty) | Health facility viability measured by geography, not just population |
| **Caregiver Skill Mission** | Train local youth as "Care Sahayaks" for elderly in "ghost villages" | Circular economy of care; prevents further migration |
| **First Medicity (Haridwar)** | Super-specialty hospital + nursing/paramedical colleges | Permanent specialized tertiary capacity |
| **Organ and Milk Banks** | Established in all government medical colleges | Specialized neonatal and transplant services |
| **"You Quote, We Pay"** | Specialists bid own salary for remote postings (up to ₹6 lakh/month) | Overcomes chronic specialist shortage |
| **Preventive NCD Screening** | Door-to-door ASHA worker screening for 17.50 lakh individuals | Early detection of "silent killers" (diabetes, hypertension, cancer) |
| **Bio-Fortification via PDS** | Fortified rice + free millet + subsidized iodized salt | Combat anaemia and stunting through food system, not just medical system |


### The "Phygital" Healthcare Model

The Phygital Healthcare Model operates on two complementary layers. The Physical Layer comprises 1,914 Sub-Centres, 524 PHCs and 81 CHCs, 1,554 Ayushman Arogya Mandirs, 9 Medical Colleges, HEMS and Drone Services, and Mobile Medical Units. The Digital Layer encompasses e-Sanjeevani (15.15 lakh consultations), the Poshan Tracker (7.33 lakh beneficiaries), Tele-MANAS for mental health, the 104 National Health Helpline, e-PoS for 9,063 automated shops, and e-KYC that eliminated ghost beneficiaries.

Uttarakhand's healthcare transformation has progressed through three phases. Phase 1 (2000–2013) focused on basic access and infrastructure creation through Sub-Centres and PHCs. Phase 2 (2013–2020) achieved universal coverage and insurance through Ayushman Bharat, NHM upgrades, and 93% institutional deliveries. Phase 3 (2020–Present) represents the "Phygital" Revolution with HEMS, Drones, Telemedicine, Bio-fortification, and Hill-specific norms. The state aims to achieve a "Zero Preventable Death" target by combining air-bridge logistics with accessibility-based facility planning.
