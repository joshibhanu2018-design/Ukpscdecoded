## PART A: RELIGIOUS PLACES & TEMPLES

## 10.1 THE CHOTA CHAR DHAM CIRCUIT

The Chota Char Dham forms the core of Uttarakhand's religious tourism, comprising four sacred shrines arranged in increasing order of altitude:

| Dham | District | Deity | Key Features |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Gangotri** | Uttarkashi | Goddess Ganga | Source of the Ganges |
| **Yamunotri** | Uttarkashi | Goddess Yamuna | Source of the Yamuna |
| **Badrinath** | Chamoli | Lord Vishnu | Conical style; head of Panch Badri |
| **Kedarnath** | Rudraprayag | Lord Shiva | Katyuri style; one of 12 Jyotirlingas; head of Panch Kedar |

### Winter Seats (When Dhams Close)

During winter months when the high-altitude shrines close, worship shifts to designated winter seats. Badrinath's winter seat is Narsingh Temple in Joshimath. Kedarnath and Madmaheshwar shift to Onkar Eshwar Temple in Ukhimath. Yamunotri moves to Sameshwar Temple in Kharsali. Gangotri shifts to Mukhimath (Mukhba) in Mukhba village. Tungnath's winter seat is in Makkumath, Rudraprayag.

## 10.4 ANCIENT TEMPLE CLUSTERS — ASI RECOGNIZED MONUMENTS

Uttarakhand has 44 Monuments of National Importance recognized by the Archaeological Survey of India.

### Jageshwar Dham (Almora)

Jageshwar Dham is located on the banks of Jata Ganga in a dense deodar forest. It contains over 100 temples, making it the largest temple complex in the state, dating from the 7th to 14th centuries. Key shrines include the Dandeshwar Temple, Kuber Temple (god of wealth), and the Nau Durga (Nanda Devi) Temple. The architecture follows the Nagara and Shikhara styles.

### Dwarahat Group (Almora) — "Dwarika of the Himalayas" / "Khajuraho of Kumaon"

The Dwarahat temple complex comprises eight main groups. The Gujardeo Temple is the most artistic, showcasing the finest Katyuri architecture. The Kutumbari Temple is the "lost" temple whose components were used by locals. Other groups include the Badrinath Group, Kacheri Group, Maniyan Group, Ratandeo Shrines, and Mrityunjaya. The style represents indigenous Kumaoni architectural tradition.


### Katarmal Sun Temple (Almora)

Built in the 12th century, the Katarmal Sun Temple is the second largest Sun Temple in India (after Konark). Its main idol faces east, a unique feature. The temple serves as historical proof of Shaka dominance and the spread of Sun worship in Kumaon.

### Baijnath Group (Bageshwar)

Located at the confluence of the Gomti and Garud Ganga rivers, the Baijnath group was built by King Bhudev of the Katyuri dynasty. It is famous as the marriage place of Lord Shiva and Parvati. The Baghnath Temple within this complex is the only ancient Shiva temple in North India that faces south.

### Baleshwar Temples (Champawat)

The Baleshwar Temples are distinguished by exquisite stone carvings reflecting the Chand dynasty influence. A unique feature is the integrated Naula (covered spring), an example of traditional water conservation architecture.

### Lakha Mandal (Dehradun)

Associated with the Pandavas of the Mahabharata, Lakha Mandal is known as the "Storehouse of idols." A unique feature is the Shiva Lingam believed to reflect images of those who pour water on it.

### Naga Temples (Ancient Serpent Worship)

Ancient serpent worship is evidenced by several Naga temples across the state. Beninag (Binag) in Pithoragarh is the most famous. Other Naga temples include Dhaulnag, Kalinag, Basukinag, and Sem Mukhem (14th century, Tehri).

## 10.7 GOVERNMENT INITIATIVES — TEMPLE & PILGRIMAGE MANAGEMENT

The Manaskhand Mandir Mala Mission is developing 48 temples in Kumaon, while the existing Kedarkhand circuit covers infrastructure in Garhwal. Trijuginarayan is being aggressively branded as a Global Wedding Destination based on the mythological Shiva-Parvati marriage and its eternal Agnikund. A phone ban has been implemented in Char Dham temples to maintain sanctity. Operation Kalanemi protects pilgrims from "fake sadhus" and anti-social elements. Religious corridors are planned along the Ganga and Sharda rivers.

The 2026 Char Dham Yatra theme is "Atithi Devo Bhava," with enhanced registration and facilities. The Haridwar Master Plan 2040 addresses sanitation and traffic management for peak events with crowds exceeding 30 lakh. The Kumbh budget allocation stands at Rs 1,027 crore for infrastructure. In national tableaux competitions, the Manaskhand tableau won first place and the Kedarkhand tableau won third place.

The Adi Kailash Yatra has attracted approximately 36,000 tourists following the PM's visit, with a marathon organized, village viewpoints planned, and linkage to winter tourism. A Cultural Heritage Building is proposed at Kalapani. The Nanda Devi Yatra has been postponed to 2027, with concerns about Kumaon participation.


## PART B: FAIRS & FESTIVALS

## 10.8 SOCIO-ECONOMIC & CULTURAL SIGNIFICANCE

Fairs and festivals in Uttarakhand serve multiple functions. They are primary platforms for the preservation of identity, sustaining endangered oral traditions (Jagar), dialects, and folk arts. They function as economic hubs — historical trade fairs (Jauljibi, Gauchar) were essential for barter and commerce and now promote ODOP and handicrafts. They foster social cohesion, resolving enmities (such as the Gagli Yuddh Utsav in Jaunsar) and promoting inter-faith harmony (Piran Kaliyar). Contemporary challenges include ecological degradation at high-altitude sites and commercialization threatening authenticity.

## 10.9 GLOBALLY RECOGNIZED FESTIVALS

### Ramman (Ritual Theater of Chamoli)

Ramman is celebrated annually in April-May (Baisakh) at the Saloor-Dungra villages in Chamoli district. Recognized as a UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity in 2009, it is a ritual theater performed for the village deity Bhumiyal Devta, featuring mask dances, folk theater, and Ramayana narratives.

### Kumbh Mela (The Global Spiritual Gathering)

The Kumbh Mela takes place in Haridwar every 12 years, when Jupiter enters Aquarius and the Sun enters Aries. It was included in the UNESCO World Heritage List in 2017. Crowds exceed 30 lakhs at peak, necessitating infrastructure investments under the Haridwar Master Plan 2040 and Rs 1,027 crore in budget allocation.

### Nanda Devi Raj Jat Yatra (The Himalayan Mahakumbh)

The Nanda Devi Raj Jat Yatra is the world's longest religious pilgrimage — 280 km on foot over 19-20 days. Celebrated every 12 years, the most recent yatra took place in 2014, with the next planned for 2027. It starts from Nauti/Kansuwa (Chamoli) and ends at Homkund. The procession is uniquely led by a sacred four-horned male sheep called Medha.


## 10.10 DEVI DHURA BAGWAL (THE STONE WAR)

The Devi Dhura Bagwal takes place at the Varahi Devi Temple in Devidhura, Champawat, on Raksha Bandhan. It is a traditional "stone war" fought between four clans: Chamyal, Valik, Gahadwal, and Limgadiya. Participants are called Dyoke. Since 2013, the event has been modified to use flowers and fruits instead of stones to prevent injuries.

## 10.11 HARELA (FESTIVAL OF GREENERY)

Harela is celebrated on the first day of the Shravan month (monsoon). It marks the beginning of the harvest season. Residents plant barley and wheat in pots days in advance and offer the grown greenery to deities. The festival symbolizes prosperity and renewal and is closely linked to environmental conservation.

## 10.13 CURRENT AFFAIRS — PILGRIMAGE & SACRED GEOGRAPHY (2025-26)

The Adi Kailash Yatra has attracted approximately 36,000 tourists following the Prime Minister's visit, with a marathon organized, village viewpoints planned, and linkage established with winter tourism. The Nanda Devi Raj Jat has been postponed to 2027, with concerns raised about Kumaon participation. The Kumbh and Ardh Kumbh remain major cultural-religious anchors for the state. A phone ban has been implemented in Char Dham temples as a discipline measure.

Trijugi Narayan is being developed as a Global Wedding Destination based on its eternal Agnikund. The Manaskhand Mission targets 48 temples in Kumaon for development. Sacred corridors are planned along the Ganga and Sharda rivers. The Gaura Devi Centenary marks the 100th birth anniversary of the legendary environmentalist. Basanti Bisht has received Padma recognition for Jagar singing. The budget session in Gairsain carries symbolism of hill identity and balanced regionality.
