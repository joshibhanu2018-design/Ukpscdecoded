## 4.1 ARRIVAL & TERRITORIAL DIVISION (1815)

Following the defeat of the Gorkhas and the **Treaty of Sugauli (1815)**, the British East India Company took control of the region.

### Division of Territory

| Division | Territory | Administration |
|---|---|---|
| **British Kumaon & Garhwal** | East of Alaknanda River | Direct British rule; included in **Kumaon Commissionerate** |
| **Tehri Riyasat** (Princely State) | West of Alaknanda (excluding Dehradun & Rawain) | Handed to Parmar King **Sudarshan Shah** |

> **KEY FACT:** The Alaknanda River served as the natural boundary between British territory and the Tehri Princely State.

### Character of Early British Rule (1815–1857)

The initial period was characterized as a time of **subdued governance, peace, and dynamism** — a stark contrast to the brutal Gorkha era, which made British administration appear liberal and reformist to the traumatized local population.

## 4.2 MAJOR BRITISH COMMISSIONERS — CHRONOLOGICAL TABLE

| Commissioner | Tenure | Key Title / Epithet | Major Contributions |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Edward Gardner** | 1815–1816 | First Commissioner of British Kumaon | Appointed May 3, 1815; initiated first land settlement in Kumaon; started postal service between Almora and Srinagar |
| **G.W. Traill** | 1816–1835 | First "real" Commissioner; **Father of Revenue Police System** | "Na Vakeel, Na Daleel, Na Appeal"; created 9 Patwari posts (1819); Double Lock System (1824); Mule Army (1822); Assi Sala Settlement (1823); Almora Jail (1816); Pauri Jail (1821); Thapla land reservation (1826) |
| **J.H. Batten** | 1848–1856 | — | Shifted HQ from Almora to **Nainital** (Oct 1855); 8th Settlement (Bees Sala); Upper Ganges Canal completed (1854) |
| **Sir Henry Ramsay** | 1856–1884 | **'Uncrowned King of Kumaon'** | Golden Age of British rule; codified Revenue Police (1874); abolished forest contract system (1858); managed 1857 revolt; Ramsay Collegiate School (1871); Beckett's scientific settlement |
| **K.L. Mehta** | 1947–48 | First & only **Indian Commissioner** | Last Commissioner of Kumaon Commissionerate |

## 4.3 COMMISSIONER G.W. TRAILL — DETAILED PROFILE

> *The man who built the administrative DNA of British Uttarakhand*

| Achievement | Details |
|---|---|
| **Judicial Policy** | Iron-fist rule: *"Na Vakeel, Na Daleel, Na Appeal"* (No lawyer, no argument, no appeal) |
| **Revenue Police (1819)** | Created 9 posts of **Patwari** — responsible for revenue collection, land records, AND police powers |
| **Treasury Security (1824)** | Introduced **Double Lock System** — one key with Collector, one with Treasurer |
| **Anti-Begar Reform (1822)** | Established **'Mule Army' (Khachchar Sena)** to transport goods — to reduce exploitative Coolie Begar |
| **Forest Policy (1826)** | Reserved **'Thapla' land** for government use; banned felling of Sal trees — first foundation of government-controlled forests |
| **Postal Service** | Implemented postal service across Uttarakhand (1816) |
| **Infrastructure** | Almora Jail (1816); Pauri Jail (1821) |
| **Land Settlement** | Conducted Assi Sala Settlement (1823) — first boundary demarcation |

---

## 4.4 COMMISSIONER SIR HENRY RAMSAY — DETAILED PROFILE

> *The 'Uncrowned King of Kumaon' — 28-year tenure (longest) — Golden Age of British rule*

| Achievement | Details |
|---|---|
| **Managing 1857 Revolt** | Declared martial law in Kumaon; used loyalty of local zamindars; kept revolt's impact extremely low in the region |
| **Forest Management (1858)** | Abolished destructive contractor system; initiated scientific management — tree marking, fire protection, plantation |
| **Revenue Police (1874)** | Formally expanded and codified the system (currently covers ~60% of Uttarakhand) |
| **Education** | Established **Ramsay Collegiate School** in Almora (1871) — Kumaon's first inter college |
| **Tenure** | 1856–1884 (28 years — unprecedented length) |

## 4.5 LAND REVENUE SETTLEMENTS (11 Total — 3 Critical)

### Land Ownership Structure

| Class | Role |
|---|---|
| **Thaat** | The land itself |
| **Thaatwan** | Primary landowner |
| **Khaikar** | Farmers cultivating under the Thaatwan |
| **Sirtan** | Tenant farmers paying annual rent |

### The Three Revolutionary Settlements

| Settlement | Year | Conducted By | Also Called | Key Innovation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **5th (Assi Sala)** | 1823 | G.W. Traill | Panchsala (5-year); occurred in Samvat 1880 | **First time boundaries** of land, forests, and water precisely demarcated within village limits |
| **8th (Bees Sala)** | 1840 (Garhwal) / 1844 (Kumaon) | J.H. Batten | Twenty-year settlement | Introduced **Khasra survey method** for land measurement |
| **9th (Beckett's)** | 1863–1873 | Beckett (during Ramsay's reign) | First scientific settlement | **First purely scientific settlement**; classified land into 5 categories |

### Beckett's Five-Category Land Classification

| Category | Description |
|---|---|
| **Talaun / Talaon** | Permanently irrigated land (highest quality) |
| **Upraun Avval** | Dry terraced high land — First grade |
| **Upraun Doyam** | Irrigated land dependent on rain/rivers |
| **Kantili / Kateel** | Inferior terraced land |
| **Ijrain** | Barren / Non-terraced stony land (lowest quality) |

> **EXAM TIP:** Remember this as: **T-U-U-K-I** (Talaun → Upraun Avval → Upraun Doyam → Kantili → Ijrain) — from best to worst quality.

## 4.6 THE REVENUE POLICE SYSTEM

| Feature | Details |
|---|---|
| **Created By** | G.W. Traill (1819) |
| **Expanded & Codified By** | Henry Ramsay (1874) |
| **Why Unique** | Patwaris had **dual powers** — revenue collection + police functions |
| **Reason** | Low crime rate in hills made separate police uneconomical |
| **Current Coverage** | Operates in **~60% of Uttarakhand** today |
| **Original Posts** | 9 Patwari posts created |

> **KEY INSIGHT:** This system is still studied as a unique administrative innovation — combining revenue and policing in one official. No other Indian state has this system at this scale.

## 4.7 BRITISH FOREST MANAGEMENT — FOUR PHASES

---

### PHASE 1: Early Exploitation & Contract System (1815–1858)

| Year | Event | Details |
|---|---|---|
| **1818** | Kath-Bamboo Mahals | Traill leased Shivalik and Bhabar forests to contractors |
| **1826** | Thapla Land Reservation | Traill reserved 'Thapla' land for government; banned Sal tree felling — **first government-controlled forests** |
| **1853** | Railway Boom | Demand for wooden sleepers skyrocketed |
| **1853–61** | **'Pahari Wilson'** (Frederick Wilson) | British timber merchant; ruthlessly cut forests; floated timber down Alaknanda & Bhagirathi rivers |
| **1855–1861** | — | Forest destruction reached **absolute peak** in Uttarakhand |

### PHASE 2: Scientific Management & Restrictive Laws (1858–1911)

| Year | Event | Details |
|---|---|---|
| **1858** | Ramsay's Intervention | Abolished contractor system; initiated scientific management (tree marking, fire protection, plantation) |
| **1864** | Forest Department Created | With German expert **Dietrich Brandis** (first Inspector General of Forests) |
| **1865** | **First Forest Act** | Passed under Brandis's advice |
| **1878** | First Forest School | Established in Dehradun on Brandis's advice |
| **1884** | Renamed | Imperial Forest School |
| **1893** | Benap Notification | All **Benap (unmeasured) land** declared as protected forests |
| **1894** | **First Forest Policy** | Hunting of wild animals and fishing made strictly illegal |
| **1906** | FRI | Forest School upgraded to **Imperial Forest Research Institute** |
| **1911–1917** | Stiffe & Nelson Settlement | Additional **3,311 sq. miles** declared reserved forests — **completely stripped local population of ancestral rights** |

### PHASE 3: Forest Rebellions — Public Backlash (1904–1930)

| Event | Year | Details |
|---|---|---|
| **Khas Patti / Kunjdi Van Movement** | 1904–1906 | Villagers revolted against Conservator Keshavanand for reserving forests around Chandrabadni temple (Tehri) |
| **1921 Forest Fires** | 1921 | Widespread protest — reserved forests set on fire |
| **First Woman Arrested** | 1921 | **Durga Devi of Someshwar** — imprisoned for 1 month for burning Thakalodi forests |
| **First Man Arrested from Kumaon** | 1921 | **Mohan Singh Mehta** |
| **Tiladi/Rawain Massacre** | May 30, 1930 | Tehri king's Diwan **Chakradhar Juyal** ordered firing on unarmed farmers at Tiladi ground (banks of Yamuna); called **"Jallianwala Bagh of Uttarakhand"**; May 30 = Martyr's Day |

### PHASE 4: Resolution & Forest Panchayats (1921–1931)

| Event | Year | Details |
|---|---|---|
| **Forest Grievances Committee** | April 13, 1921 | Chaired by Kumaon Commissioner **Percy Wyndham** |
| **Recommendations** | — | Villagers' suffering due to rigid "scientific forestry"; return rights; lift bans on bamboo/grazing; allow forest rights up to half mile from village |
| **Forest Panchayat Act** | **1931** | **Van Panchayats** implemented; forests divided into Reserved, Protected, and Village forests |

> **KEY FACT:** The Van Panchayat system (1931) uniquely empowers local communities in Uttarakhand to manage forests — this system continues to this day and is studied globally as a model of community forest governance.

## 4.8 ECONOMY UNDER BRITISH RULE

### Revenue Sources

| Source | Details |
|---|---|
| **Primary** | Land Revenue (through 11 settlements) |
| **Commercial** | Forest timber, Tea plantations, Canal irrigation |
| **Labour** | Coolie Begar (exploitative unpaid forced labour) |

### Key Economic Developments

| Year | Development | Details |
|---|---|---|
| **1822** | Mule Army (Khachchar Sena) | By Traill — to reduce Coolie Begar |
| **1843** | First Tea Plantations | Captain **Huddleston** established first commercial tea gardens in **Pauri and Gadoliya** |
| **1854** | Upper Ganges Canal | Completed during Batten's tenure; father of project: **Colonel Sir P.T. Cautley** |
| **1883** | Tarai Improvement Fund | For agricultural upliftment in the plains |
| **Ongoing** | Coolie Begar System | Forced unpaid labour for transporting British goods — became major cause of resentment and eventually a powerful movement |

### Key Educational Institutions (Chronological)

| Year | Institution | Location | Significance |
|---|---|---|---|

| **1871** | Ramsay Collegiate School | Almora | Kumaon's first inter college (by Henry Ramsay) |
