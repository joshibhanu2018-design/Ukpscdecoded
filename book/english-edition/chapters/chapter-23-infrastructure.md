## 23.1 BANKING & FINANCIAL INFRASTRUCTURE

### Branch Network (As of September 30, 2025)

As of September 2025, Uttarakhand has a total of 2,622 bank branches, of which 1,206 (46%) are rural, 626 (24%) are semi-urban, and 793 (30%) are urban. The population per branch is approximately 4,035, significantly better than the national average of 11,271. The state has 2,524 ATMs, with urban areas having the highest concentration at 1,147. Dehradun leads with 651 branches, while Rudraprayag has the fewest at 56.

### Institutional Breakdown

|---|---|---|

| Private Sector Banks | ~511 | — |
| Regional Rural Banks | 289 | — |
| District Cooperative Banks | 10 (through 327 branches) | — |

### Total Banking Business

Total banking volume reached ₹3,75,378 Crore as of September 2025, registering 7.72% growth. Priority Sector Lending stands at ₹64,230 Crore, with Agriculture Advances at ₹15,910 Crore and MSME Advances at ₹35,274 Crore. There are 5.77 Lakh active Kisan Credit Cards, with 1,74,005 new cards issued in 2024-25.

### Cooperative Banking — The PACS Revolution

The state has 671 multipurpose Primary Agricultural Credit Societies (PACS), all of which have been computerized — making Uttarakhand the first state in India to achieve 100% PACS computerization. Of these, 310 PACS function as Jan Suvidha Kendras (e-governance hubs) and 23 PACS provide generic medicines as Jan Aushadhi Kendras. The cooperative system offers 0% interest loans of up to ₹1 Lakh for individuals and ₹5 Lakh for SHGs. Total deposits in the cooperative sector amount to ₹15,65,145 Lakh.

### Role of NABARD

| Initiative | Impact |
|---|---|
| RIDF | 15,984 km roads built; 2.45 Lakh hectares irrigated |
| SHG-Bank Linkage | 55,444 SHGs linked; ₹1,238.55 Crore credit |
| FIDF | Concessional finance for fisheries/trout infrastructure |

### Financial Inclusion

Under PM Jan-Dhan Yojana (PMJDY), 39.53 Lakh accounts have been opened with deposits of ₹3,062.64 Crore and 25.78 Lakh RuPay cards issued. PM Suraksha Bima Yojana (PMSBY) has enrolled 49.71 Lakh people for accidental insurance, while PM Jeevan Jyoti Bima Yojana (PMJJBY) covers 18.14 Lakh for life insurance. Atal Pension Yojana (APY) has 9.30 Lakh subscribers. The RBI Regional Office in Dehradun was established on June 30, 2006.

### The CD Ratio Paradox

The overall Credit-Deposit Ratio stands at 54.67% (including RIDF). The highest ratio is in US Nagar at 119.42%, while the lowest is in Bageshwar at just 24.66%, followed by Almora at 26.97% and Pauri at 27.47%.

The CD Ratio asymmetry reveals a structural "Capital Flight" phenomenon — savings deposited by hill residents are diverted by banks to fund loans in the plains or outside the state. This starves hill districts of entrepreneurial investment, fuels Palaayan (outmigration), and creates a vicious cycle where lack of credit leads to lack of enterprise, which leads to lack of youth, which further reduces demand for credit.

### Way Forward (Banking)

- **Digital Banking Units (DBUs):** Paperless units in every block
- **Bank-on-Wheels:** Mobile ATMs for disaster-affected and remote areas
- **Green Credit Framework:** Linking finance to GEP goals
- **District-specific credit targets:** Mandating hill-capital reinvestment into horticulture, trout, and MAPs
- **100% PACS computerization** already achieved — next step: CBS integration

## 23.2 ENERGY & POWER INFRASTRUCTURE

### The Energy Paradox

Uttarakhand possesses a hydroelectric potential of approximately 25,000 MW but has an installed capacity of only about 4,045 MW. Total generation stands at 16,500 MU (4.3 times the 2001 level), while total consumption has reached 17,192 MU (6 times the 2001 level) — meaning the state consumes more than it generates. Household electrification has achieved 100% coverage, up from 60% in 2000-01. Solar capacity stands at 861–1,027 MW. The state's power transmission infrastructure is exemplary: PTCUL records transmission losses of just 1.02%, system availability of 99.72%, and AT&C losses reduced from 44.82% to 14.57%. PTCUL holds a Joint 1st national ranking alongside Gujarat and Haryana, with an A++ credit rating.

### Energy Issues and Way Forward

Generation remains below consumption (16,500 MU vs 17,192 MU), requiring grid imports during peak demand driven by the industrial corridor. Ecological fragility poses challenges since 90% of the state lies in Seismic Zones IV-V, where blasting fractures aquifers and causes springs to dry. The E-Flow Debate around 20-30% minimum flow mandates reduces project capacity in lean season. Siltation from glacial melt and landslides reduces turbine lifespan. The way forward includes Micro-Hydel, Solar, and Pumped Storage Plants (PSP) as "Green Batteries"; Cumulative Environmental Impact Assessment for entire river basins; and smart meters to reduce the 14.57% AT&C losses further.

### Technical & Vocational

The state has 153 government ITIs (including 16 exclusively for women) and 72 Polytechnics (a 4.5-fold increase). Smart classes have been installed in 1,585 government schools. Under PM SHRI, 192 secondary and 34 primary schools have been designated as "Ideal Schools." ITDA-CALC operates 50 digital literacy training centres. Corporate ITI partners include Hyundai, Maruti, Samsung, Tata STRIVE, and Siemens.

### Education Issues and Way Forward

Ghost teachers and absenteeism in remote hills are being addressed through biometric and CCTV attendance systems. Migration has left hill schools with single-digit enrolment, prompting the PM SHRI Cluster Model approach. While 1,585 smart classes help bridge the digital divide across geography, digital literacy remains low among the elderly. The employability gap from outdated ITI courses is being addressed by pivoting to AI/IoT/Robotics through corporate CSR. Madrasa education reform has transformed the Madrasa Board into the State Minority Education Authority (July 2026) with STEM and NCERT mandated. The way forward includes the Hub & Spoke Model, Vidya Samiksha Kendra for real-time data, and NEP 2020 implementation (Uttarakhand being the first state to prepare for it).

### Health Issues and Way Forward

The Himalayan Distance Penalty means the nearest specialist can be a 10-hour drive away in upper hills — e-Sanjeevani telemedicine is addressing this gap. The state now faces an NCD shift, battling lifestyle diseases (diabetes, hypertension, cancer) similar to developed nations. PHC norms based on plains population thresholds need revision to hill-specific accessibility norms. NFHS data reveals persistent nutritional gaps with 27% stunting and 50% anaemia despite scheme coverage. The way forward includes fortified rice and dal in PDS, HPV vaccine rollout, HEMS expansion, Medicity Haridwar, and an AI Mission for health.

## 23.5 COMMUNICATION & TELECOM INFRASTRUCTURE

### Connectivity Data

Approximately 95% of villages have mobile connectivity, with 1,108 mobile towers installed under DBN projects covering Uttarakhand villages. All 13 districts now have 5G coverage (with Airtel confirming all-district coverage), while nationally 5.18 Lakh 5G BTSs have been deployed as of March 2026. BharatNet Phase 3 for the Uttarakhand circle has been contracted at ₹2,647.12 Crore to NCC Ltd, comprising ₹1,543 Crore CapEx and ₹1,104 Crore OpEx.

### Digital Governance Stack

### Communication Issues and Way Forward

While 95% coverage exists, quality and speed in remote valleys remains poor, and tower maintenance in landslide zones is costly. The digital literacy gap means elderly populations in ghost villages cannot leverage digital services. Cyber vulnerability has been highlighted by state data centre attacks and a dark-web bomb threat to Nainital Court, underscoring the need for CERT-UTK strengthening. The way forward includes BharatNet Phase 3 (₹2,647 Crore), Digital Banking Units in every block, and the Uttarakhand AI Mission 2025 launched at Raj Bhavan, adapted to hilly conditions.

## 23.6 WATER SUPPLY, SANITATION & SEWERAGE

### Jal Jeevan Mission (Rural Water)

Under the Jal Jeevan Mission, rural household coverage has reached 97.86% (14,17,379 out of 14,48,425 households), providing 55 Litres Per Capita Per Day. School coverage is 100% across 19,123 schools, and Anganwadi coverage is 100% across 16,439 centres. The Jal-Sakhi initiative engages women SHGs in collecting water bills with a 7.5% incentive (₹16.87 Lakh disbursed).

### Urban Sewerage

The state has 67 operational Sewage Treatment Plants with a capacity of 420.83 MLD, currently treating 294.89 MLD. An additional 47 STPs are under construction adding 253.92 MLD capacity. A wastewater reuse pilot in Dehradun channels treated STP water for parks, dividers, and washing.

### Sanitation (Swachh Bharat Mission)

| Parameter | Data |
|---|---|
| ODF Plus Villages | 15,049 |
| ODF Model Villages | 14,892 |
| Plastic Waste Compactors | 81 (across rural blocks) |
| Legacy Waste Clearance | 14 projects; 17.51 Lakh MT (Dehradun, Haridwar, Kashipur, Haldwani) |

### Spring Rejuvenation (SARRA)

The Spring and River Rejuvenation Authority (SARRA) has been allocated ₹125 Crore under the theme "Dhara Mera, Naula Mera, Gaon Mera, Prayas Mera." The Bhagirath App enables citizens to report endangered water sources. The target is to revive approximately 6,000 drying springs, streams, and naulas. CAMPA support of ₹5 Crore each has been directed to Shipra (Nainital) and Western Nayar (Pauri) rivers.

### Water Issues and Way Forward

Over 50% of springs are showing reduced flow due to deforestation and climate change. While JJM has achieved 97.86% Functional Household Tap Connections, long-term maintenance in landslide-prone terrain remains the real challenge. TERI has flagged coliform/bacterial load in spring water with post-monsoon mineral deficiency. The way forward includes SARRA and the Bhagirath App, community-managed decentralized systems, circular water economy through STP reuse, and Amrit Sarovar (1,322 built).

## 23.7 HOUSING INFRASTRUCTURE

### Rural Housing (PMAY-Gramin)

Under PMAY-Gramin, financial assistance of ₹1.30 Lakh per house is provided for hill states (higher than ₹1.20 Lakh for plains). The Awas Plus Waiting List targets 68,530 families for saturation, with the goal of 100% coverage of eligible rural households. PMGSY targets 13,920 km of rural roads for habitations with 250+ population.

### Urban Housing (PMAY-Urban)

Under Beneficiary Led Construction (BLC), 25,190 houses have been sanctioned, while the Affordable Housing Partnership (AHP) has sanctioned 15,960 houses.

### Urban Development

| Parameter | Data |
|---|---|
| ULBs | 108 (up from 63 in 2001) |
| Municipal Corporations | 11 (up from 1) |
| Development Authorities | 14 (up from 5) |
| Smart City (Dehradun) | ₹1,000 Crore; Command Centre; electric buses |
| AMRUT 2.0 | Expanded to 68 municipalities |
| UIRUDP (ADB) | $118.48 million for Dehradun + Nainital climate-resilient water/sewerage |

### Housing Issues and Way Forward

Urbanization at 36.77% is outpacing infrastructure services, leading to traffic, sewerage, and waste crises. ULBs suffer from near-zero own-tax revenue and remain grant-dependent, limiting their agility. Hill housing vulnerability in Seismic Zone IV-V requires earthquake-resistant design at higher cost. The way forward includes Land Pooling Regulations 2025, GIS-based property mapping, self-certification for building approval, and ADB-funded climate-resilient networks.

### SHG/Social Infrastructure Issues and Way Forward

Most SHGs remain in the savings and micro-credit phase and need market linkage and processing support to transition into formal enterprises. Lakhpati Didi sustainability is a concern since the income threshold of ₹1 Lakh/year is eroded by inflation, requiring value-chain deepening. The Gender Budget allocation of ₹19,692 Crore is large, but implementation quality at the tehsil level remains the real test. Migration prevention targeting 474 ghost villages depends on local enterprise creation rather than welfare alone. The way forward involves SHG-to-Enterprise transition via startup incubation, House of Himalayas brand scaling, Cooperative-FPO linkage, digital financial literacy for women, and the Pravasi Panchayat model.
