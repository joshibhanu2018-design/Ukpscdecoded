# CHAPTER 6

# National Movement, Freedom Fighters & Growth of Journalism in Uttarakhand

## PART A: UTTARAKHAND IN THE NATIONAL MOVEMENT

## 6.1 THE REVOLT OF 1857: THE EARLY SPARKS

### Regional Divide in 1857

The Revolt of 1857 had an uneven impact across Uttarakhand. In **Kumaon**, the impact was significantly higher — Commissioner Henry Ramsay had to impose strict martial law to maintain control. In **Garhwal**, however, the impact remained low because local zamindars such as Padam Singh and Shivram Singh remained loyal to the British and actively prevented revolutionaries from entering the hills.

### Key Events of 1857

On September 17, 1857, an army led by Khan Bahadur Khan of Rohilkhand briefly captured **Haldwani**. However, the British recaptured Haldwani the very next day, on September 18, 1857.

### Kalu Singh Mehra — The First Freedom Fighter of Uttarakhand

Kalu Singh Mehra, from village Bisung (Visud) in Champawat, holds the distinction of being the first freedom fighter of Uttarakhand. He was the first to organize an armed rebellion against the British in the region. To unite the people of Kumaon, he formed a secret organization called **'Krantiveer'**, drawing support from followers including Anand Singh Faltyal and Bishan Singh Karayat.

## 6.2 SWADESHI MOVEMENT (1905) AND EARLY POLITICAL AWAKENING

The partition of Bengal in 1905 sparked political awakening in Uttarakhand. A massive protest meeting was held at **Nanda Devi in Almora** against the partition. In 1906, **Hariram Tripathi** translated *"Vande Mataram"* into the Kumaoni dialect, an act the British considered highly seditious. At the 1905 Banaras Session of the Congress, a group of youth from Uttarakhand — including G.B. Pant, Hargovind Pant, Badri Datt Pandey, and Mukundi Lal — attended and were heavily influenced by **Gopal Krishna Gokhale**.

## 6.3 HOME RULE MOVEMENT (1914) AND KUMAON PARISHAD (1916)

### Home Rule League in Uttarakhand

The Home Rule League was launched in Uttarakhand in 1914, explicitly aligned with **Bal Gangadhar Tilak** rather than Annie Besant. Badri Datt Pandey famously declared: "We have not established the Home Rule League of the deceitful old woman Basanti (Annie Besant), rather we have established the league of Lokmanya Tilak." He also said that seeing Tilak four times felt like completing his "Char Dham Yatra." In 1918, **Swami Vicharanand Saraswati** established the Dehradun branch of the League.

### Formation of Kumaon Parishad (1916)

The **Kumaon Parishad** was founded in 1916 by G.B. Pant, Badri Datt Pandey, Hargovind Pant, and others. Its primary goal was to find solutions to local socio-economic problems — particularly forest rights and begar (forced labour) — while simultaneously integrating Uttarakhand into national political goals. The Parishad became the political backbone of the region and merged with the Indian National Congress in **1926**.

## 6.4 NON-COOPERATION MOVEMENT (1920–1922) AND THE COOLIE BEGAR END

### Gandhi's Early Visits

Mahatma Gandhi first visited Uttarakhand in **April 1915**, arriving in Haridwar during the Kumbh Mela and also visiting Rishikesh and Swargashram. He returned in **1916** to deliver a lecture at Gurukul Kangri University in Haridwar and also visited Dehradun.

### The Coolie Begar System

Three types of forced labour existed under British rule in the hills. **Coolie Begar** meant working without pay, primarily carrying luggage of British officials. **Coolie Utaar** required providing free coolies for officers. **Coolie Barda Yash** demanded providing free rations to officials.

### THE BLOODLESS REVOLUTION — January 13-14, 1921

The abolition of the Coolie Begar system stands as one of the most significant achievements of the freedom movement in Uttarakhand. On January 13-14, 1921, approximately 40,000 agitators gathered at the **Uttarayani Fair in Bageshwar** under the leadership of **Badri Datt Pandey**, Hargovind Pant, and Chiranjilal. After taking a sacred pledge at the **Bagnath Temple**, the people threw the official Begar registers into the **Saryu River** and vowed never to provide forced labour again. Mahatma Gandhi hailed this as a **"bloodless revolution,"** and Badri Dtt Pandey earned the title **"Kumaon Kesari."** The British officially ended the practice by 1923.

### First Arrests

**Mohan Singh Mehta** holds the distinction of being the first politician from Uttarakhand to go to jail during the Non-Cooperation Movement.

## 6.5 CIVIL DISOBEDIENCE MOVEMENT AND SALT SATYAGRAHA (1930)

### Dandi March Participants from Uttarakhand

Three individuals from Uttarakhand marched alongside Gandhi during the 1930 Salt Satyagraha: **Jyotiram Kandpal**, **Bhairav Datt Joshi**, and **Gorakhveer Khadak Bahadur**.

### Women's Vanguard — Flag Hoisting (May 25, 1930)

On May 25, 1930, **Bishni Devi Shah** — the first woman freedom fighter from Uttarakhand to go to jail — led a group of women including Kunti Verma, Mangla Devi, and Revati to hoist the Indian tricolour on the **Almora Municipality building**. This act of defiance marked a watershed moment for women's participation in the freedom movement in the region.

### THE PESHAWAR INCIDENT (April 23, 1930)

The Peshawar Incident represents a watershed moment in Indian military history. On April 23, 1930, soldiers of the **2/18 Royal Garhwal Rifles** under the leadership of **Veer Chandra Singh Garhwali** refused the order of British Commander Ricket to fire upon unarmed Afghan freedom fighters at Peshawar's **Kissakhani Bazaar**. The Afghan protesters were followers of Frontier Gandhi — **Khan Abdul Ghaffar Khan**. For this act of defiance, Garhwali was sentenced to over **11 years** in prison. His defence was led by **Barrister Mukundi Lal** from Uttarakhand. The national impact was immense — **Motilal Nehru** announced the observance of **'Garhwal Day'** across the entire country.

## 6.6 QUIT INDIA MOVEMENT (1942)

### Underground Resistance

When the Quit India Movement was launched, top leaders from the region — Hargovind Pant and Badri Datt Pandey — were immediately arrested under "Operation Zero Hour." Hargovind Pant was the first leader from the region arrested during the 1942 movement. Underground resistance continued through **Madan Mohan Upadhyay**, who operated underground radio stations and distributed revolutionary pamphlets. **Sarala Behn** (born Katherine Heilman), who had established Lakshmi Ashram in Kausani, was declared by the British as the **"most dangerous person of Almora."**

### Major Rebellions and Firing Incidents

| Incident | Date | Details |
|---|---|---|
| **Deghat Firing Case** | Aug 19, 1942 | Harikrishna Upreti and Heeramani Badola martyred in police firing |

| **Gujdu Movement** | 1942 | Led by Ram Prasad Nautiyal; earned title "Bardoli of Garhwal" |

## 6.7 CONTRIBUTION TO AZAD HIND FAUJ (INA)

Uttarakhand made a remarkable contribution to the Indian National Army, providing approximately **12% of total INA soldiers** (around 2,500 soldiers). **Major Dev Singh Danu** served as commander of Netaji's bodyguard battalion, while **Chandra Singh Negi** commanded the Officers Training School in Singapore. Subhas Chandra Bose himself had established a secret organization in Dehradun, holding public meetings at **Tagore Villa** to plan the bomb attack on Lord Hardinge.

### Institutions Inspired by Gandhi's Visits

Two significant institutions emerged from Gandhi's influence on the region. The **Gandhi Ashram at Chanoda (Almora)** was established in 1937 by **Shanti Lal Trivedi** and became a major nerve centre for revolutionary activities until the British forcefully locked it down in 1942. The **Lakshmi Ashram in Kausani** was founded in 1941 by **Sarla Behn** (Katherine Heilman) to prepare local women for the freedom struggle.

## 6.11 SOCIAL REFORM MOVEMENTS

### The Dola Palki Movement (1930s)

Under the prevailing social order, lower-caste artisans (Shilpkars/Dalits) were strictly prohibited from sitting in a Dola or Palki (palanquin) during weddings and were forced to walk. Around 1930, **Jayanand Bharti**, an Arya Samaj follower, led a sustained campaign that successfully secured the right for Dalit grooms to use palanquins. This represented a massive blow for social equality in the hills.

### Shilpkar (Artisan) Reform Movement

The reform of the Shilpkar (artisan) community progressed through several milestones. In 1905, **Hari Prasad Tamta** formed the Tamta Sudhar Sabha. By 1911, his efforts led to the official recognition of the term **'Shilpkar'** for Dalits, replacing derogatory designations. In 1913, **Khusiram Arya** founded the Shilpkar Sudharini Sabha to combat child marriage and alcoholism among Dalits.

### Naik Social Reform Movement (1919-1929)

The Naik caste faced a particularly oppressive tradition in which girls were forced into prostitution. The Naik Reform Committee was formed in 1919 to address this injustice. Its sustained campaign resulted in the British government passing the **Naik Balika Raksha Kanun** (Naik Girl Protection Act) in **1929**, which strictly prohibited this practice.

## PART C: GROWTH OF JOURNALISM IN UTTARAKHAND

## 6.13 FIRST PHASE: FOUNDATION AND COLONIAL ERA (1842–1870)

The history of journalism in Uttarakhand begins in 1842 with **The Hills**, published by **John MacKinnon** from Mussoorie. This was the first newspaper of both Uttarakhand and Northern India, printed in English. MacKinnon also established the region's first printing press. In 1845/1850, **John Lang** launched **Mephistolite** from Mussoorie — a distinctly anti-imperialist English newspaper that opposed British policies. Lang was also the lawyer for Rani Lakshmibai. The most significant milestone of this phase was the publication of **Samay Vinod** in 1868 by **Jaydatt Joshi** from Jaspur — the first Hindi and Urdu language newspaper from Uttarakhand.

## 6.14 SECOND PHASE: NATIONALIST AWAKENING AND PROTEST (1871–1940)

This period represents the golden era of vernacular journalism in Uttarakhand, as newspapers shifted decisively toward protest against British imperialism.

| Newspaper | Year | Editor/Publisher | Significance |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Almora Akhbar** | 1871 | Buddhi Ballabh Pant (Almora) | "First brick of journalism in Uttarakhand" |
| **Almora Akhbar** (under Pandey) | 1913 | Badri Dutt Pandey became editor | Scathing attacks on British; exposed illegal murder by DC Lomash (1918); banned |
| **Garhwal Samachar** | 1902 | Girija Dutt Naithani (Lansdowne) | Hindi monthly |
| **Garhwali** | 1905 | Vishambhar Dutt Chandola & Girija Dutt Naithani (Dehradun) | Published by Garhwal Union; Chandola = "Father of Hindi Journalism in Garhwal" |

## 6.15 THIRD PHASE: MASS STRUGGLE AND SOCIAL REFORMS (1941–1947)

The final pre-independence phase of journalism was dominated by **Yugvani**, published from 1941/1947 by **Bhagwati Prasad Panthri** and **Gopeshwar Kothiyal** from Dehradun. This newspaper served as the platform for the Tehri Praja Mandal struggle, and historians have placed it at par with **Lenin's newspaper 'Iskra'** for its role in Tehri's people's revolution.

### Post-Independence Milestone

In 1953, **Vishnu Dutt Uniyal** launched **Parvatiya** from Nainital — the **first Hindi daily newspaper of the state**.
