## 6.1 THE REVOLT OF 1857: THE EARLY SPARKS


### Regional Divide in 1857

| Region | Impact | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| **Kumaon** | Significantly higher impact | Commissioner Henry Ramsay had to impose strict martial law |
| **Garhwal** | Low impact | Local zamindars (Padam Singh, Shivram Singh) remained loyal to British; prevented revolutionaries from entering hills |

### Key Events of 1857

| Event | Details |
|---|---|
| **Sept 17, 1857** | Army led by Khan Bahadur Khan of Rohilkhand briefly captured **Haldwani** |
| **Sept 18, 1857** | British recaptured Haldwani the next day |

### Kalu Singh Mehra — The First Freedom Fighter of Uttarakhand

| Parameter | Details |
|---|---|
| **Village** | Bisung (Visud), Champawat |
| **Title** | First freedom fighter of Uttarakhand |
| **Action** | First to organize armed rebellion against British in the region |
| **Organization** | Formed secret organization called **'Krantiveer'** to unite people of Kumaon |
| **Supporters** | Anand Singh Faltyal, Bishan Singh Karayat |

## 6.2 SWADESHI MOVEMENT (1905) & EARLY POLITICAL AWAKENING

### Key Events

## 6.3 HOME RULE MOVEMENT (1914) & KUMAON PARISHAD (1916)

### Home Rule League in Uttarakhand

| Parameter | Details |
|---|---|
| **Launched in Uttarakhand** | 1914 |
| **Aligned with** | Bal Gangadhar Tilak (NOT Annie Besant) |
| **Badri Datt Pandey's famous quote** | "We have not established the Home Rule League of the deceitful old woman Basanti (Annie Besant), rather we have established the league of Lokmanya Tilak" |
| **Another quote by Pandey** | Seeing Tilak four times felt like completing his "Char Dham Yatra" |
| **Dehradun branch (1918)** | Established by Swami Vicharanand Saraswati |

### Formation of Kumaon Parishad (1916)

| Parameter | Details |
|---|---|
| **Founded by** | G.B. Pant, Badri Datt Pandey, Hargovind Pant, and others |
| **Primary Goal** | Find solutions to local socio-economic problems (forest rights, begar) while integrating Uttarakhand into national political goals |
| **Achievement** | Became the political backbone of the region |
| **Merged with** | Indian National Congress in **1926** |

## 6.4 NON-COOPERATION MOVEMENT (1920–1922) & THE COOLIE BEGAR END

### Gandhi's Early Visits

| Visit | Year | Details |
|---|---|---|
| **Haridwar** | April 1915 | During Kumbh Mela; also visited Rishikesh and Swargashram |
| **Haridwar** | 1916 | Lecture at Gurukul Kangri University; also visited Dehradun |

### The Coolie Begar System (Background)

Three types of forced labour existed:
| Type | Description |
|---|---|
| **Coolie Begar** | Working without pay (carrying luggage of British officials) |
| **Coolie Utaar** | Providing free coolies for officers |
| **Coolie Barda Yash** | Providing free rations to officials |

### THE BLOODLESS REVOLUTION — January 13-14, 1921

| Parameter | Details |
|---|---|
| **Date** | January 13-14, 1921 |
| **Location** | **Uttarayani Fair, Bageshwar** |
| **Leaders** | **Badri Datt Pandey**, Hargovind Pant, Chiranjilal |
| **Number of Agitators** | ~40,000 |
| **Sacred Pledge Taken At** | Bagnath Temple |
| **The Action** | Threw official Begar registers into the **Saryu River** |
| **Vow** | Never to provide forced labour again |
| **Gandhi's Praise** | Hailed this as a **"bloodless revolution"** |
| **Title Earned** | Badri Datt Pandey → **"Kumaon Kesari"** |
| **British Response** | Officially ended the practice by 1923 |

### First Arrests

| Person | Distinction |
|---|---|
| **Mohan Singh Mehta** | First politician from Uttarakhand to go to jail during Non-Cooperation Movement |

### THE PESHAWAR INCIDENT (April 23, 1930)

> **A watershed moment in Indian military history**

| Parameter | Details |
|---|---|
| **Date** | April 23, 1930 |
| **Regiment** | 2/18 Royal Garhwal Rifles |
| **Hero** | **Veer Chandra Singh Garhwali** |
| **British Commander** | Ricket |
| **Refused Order** | To fire upon unarmed Afghan freedom fighters |
| **Location** | Peshawar's **Kissakhani Bazaar** |
| **Afghan Leader** | Frontier Gandhi — **Khan Abdul Ghaffar Khan** |
| **Punishment** | Garhwali sentenced to over **11 years** in prison |
| **Defence Lawyer** | **Barrister Mukundi Lal** (from Uttarakhand) |
| **National Impact** | Motilal Nehru announced observance of **'Garhwal Day'** across entire country |

## 6.6 QUIT INDIA MOVEMENT (1942)

### Underground Resistance

| Feature | Details |
|---|---|
| **Top Leaders Arrested** | Hargovind Pant, Badri Datt Pandey (under "Operation Zero Hour") |
| **Underground Work** | Madan Mohan Upadhyay operated underground radio stations; distributed revolutionary pamphlets |
| **Sarala Behn** | Katherine Heilman; established Lakshmi Ashram (Kausani); British declared her **"most dangerous person of Almora"** |
| **Hargovind Pant** | First leader from the region arrested during 1942 Quit India |

### Major Rebellions and Firing Incidents

| Incident | Date | Details |
|---|---|---|
| **Deghat Firing Case** | Aug 19, 1942 | **Harikrishna Upreti** and **Heeramani Badola** martyred in police firing |
| **Salam Revolt** | Aug 25, 1942 | **Pratap Singh** = "Bhagat Singh of Salam"; **Ram Singh Azad** = "Lion of Salam" |
| **Sult Firing Incident** | Sept 5, 1942 | British officer **Johnson** ordered firing in Khumaan (Sult); brothers **Ganga Ram and Khimdev** martyred; Gandhi called this region **"Bardoli of Kumaon"** |
| **Gujdu Movement** | 1942 | Led by **Ram Prasad Nautiyal**; earned title **"Bardoli of Garhwal"** |

## 6.11 SOCIAL REFORM MOVEMENTS

### The Dola Palki Movement (1930s)

| Parameter | Details |
|---|---|
| **Issue** | Lower-caste artisans (Shilpkars/Dalits) strictly prohibited from sitting in Dola/Palki (palanquin) during weddings; forced to walk |
| **Leader** | **Jayanand Bharti** (Arya Samaj follower) |
| **Period** | Around 1930 |
| **Outcome** | Successfully secured right for Dalit grooms to use palanquins; massive blow for social equality |

### Shilpkar (Artisan) Reform Movement

| Event | Year | Leader | Outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tamta Sudhar Sabha formed | 1905 | **Hari Prasad Tamta** | — |
| Term **'Shilpkar'** officially recognized for Dalits | 1911 | Hari Prasad Tamta | Respectable term replacing derogatory ones |
| Shilpkar Sudharini Sabha formed | 1913 | **Khusiram Arya** | End child marriage and alcoholism among Dalits |

### Naik Social Reform Movement (1919-1929)

| Parameter | Details |
|---|---|
| **Issue** | Naik caste girls traditionally forced into prostitution |
| **Committee Formed** | Naik Reform Committee (1919) |
| **Outcome** | British government passed **Naik Balika Raksha Kanun** (Naik Girl Protection Act) in **1929** to strictly prohibit this practice |

## 6.13 FIRST PHASE: FOUNDATION & COLONIAL ERA (1842–1870)

| Newspaper | Year | Editor | Location | Significance |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **The Hills** | 1842 | **John MacKinnon** | Mussoorie | **First newspaper of Uttarakhand & Northern India**; English; MacKinnon established region's first printing press |
| **Mephistolite** | 1845/1850 | **John Lang** | Mussoorie | English; distinctly **anti-imperialist**; opposed British policies; Lang was lawyer for Rani Lakshmibai |
| **Samay Vinod** | 1868 | **Jaydatt Joshi** | Jaspur | **First Hindi and Urdu language newspaper from Uttarakhand**; monumental milestone |

---

## 6.14 SECOND PHASE: NATIONALIST AWAKENING & PROTEST (1871–1940)

> **The "Golden Era" of vernacular journalism — newspapers shifted to protest against British imperialism**

| Newspaper | Year | Editor/Publisher | Location | Significance |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Almora Akhbar** | 1871 | **Buddhi Ballabh Pant** (Almora Debating Club) | Almora | **"First brick of journalism in Uttarakhand"** |
| *— Under Badri Dutt Pandey* | 1913 | Badri Dutt Pandey became editor | — | Launched scathing attacks on British; 1918: exposed illegal murder by DC Lomash; British imposed massive fine & **banned** the newspaper |
| **Garhwal Samachar** | 1902 | **Girija Dutt Naithani** | Lansdowne | Hindi monthly |
| **Garhwali** | 1905 | **Vishambhar Dutt Chandola** (Father of Hindi Journalism in Garhwal) & Girija Dutt Naithani | Dehradun | Published by Garhwal Union; emphasized regional news, local writers, social evils; covered forest movements & patwari terror; Tehri State tried to shut it down |
| **Shakti** | 1918 | **Badri Dutt Pandey** | Almora (Deshbhakt Press) | Launched on **Vijayadashami** immediately after Almora Akhbar's ban; continued nationalist struggle |
| **Tarun Kumaon** | 1922 | **Barrister Mukandi Lal** | Lansdowne | Hindi weekly; named on lines of Mazzini's *'Young Italy'* |
| **Swadhin Praja** | 1930 | **Victor Mohan Joshi** | Almora | Boldly termed execution of Bhagat Singh as **"hooliganism of the government"** |
| **Samta** | 1934 | **Hari Prasad Tamta** | Almora | Dedicated to voice of poor and Dalit society; 1935: edited by **Lakshmi Devi Tamta** — **first Dalit woman journalist of the state** |
| **Karmabhoomi** | 1939 | **Bhairav Dutt Dhulia** & **Bhakt Darshan** | Lansdowne | Prominent nationalist publication |
