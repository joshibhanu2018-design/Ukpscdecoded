## PART A: NATIONAL PARKS & WILDLIFE

## 19.1 PROTECTED AREA NETWORK — OVERVIEW

Uttarakhand's protected area network covers approximately 12% of the state's geographical area, comprising 6 National Parks, 7 Wildlife Sanctuaries, 4 Conservation Reserves, and 1 Biosphere Reserve. The state hosts remarkable biodiversity: 20% of India's total fauna, 102 mammal species (25% of India), 600 bird species (50% of India), and 4,700 species of flowering plants (25% of India's angiosperms). The tiger population stands at 560 (3rd in India), with Corbett holding the highest global density at 260 tigers in 2023. The state has 2,026 elephants and 2,927 leopards. In the Great Backyard Bird Count 2025, Uttarakhand ranked 2nd in India with 446 species recorded.

## 19.5 HUMAN-WILDLIFE CONFLICT (HWC) — DETAILED ANALYSIS

### Causes

Habitat fragmentation from linear infrastructure (highways, rail) cuts migration routes. High tiger density in Corbett pushes leopards to human fringes due to prey base depletion. Invasive species (Lantana and Parthenium) destroy the understory, reducing herbivore fodder and driving predators into villages. Dry spells combined with Pirul fuel trigger fires that destroy habitat, pushing wildlife toward settlements. The Kanha-Corbett corridor has been compromised by corridor breaking.

### Multi-Dimensional Impact

Crop depredation by monkeys and wild boar is the primary reason for farm abandonment (Palayan Trigger), with over 80% crop loss recorded in Rudraprayag and Chamoli. The feminization of risk is acute, as women encounter predators during firewood and fodder collection. Wildlife movement hampers school access in remote blocks. Human-wildlife conflict drives the abandonment of terraced farms, creating ghost villages.


### Conflict Species

Leopards (Guldar) are the most frequent attackers, particularly in Almora, Pauri, and Tehri, often entering habitations. Elephants encroach on Shivalik Elephant Reserve corridors. Monkeys and wild boar, declared "Vermin" in high-conflict tehsils, are the main agricultural destroyers.

## PART B: TOURISM

## 19.6 MACRO TOURISM STATUS

Tourism contributes approximately 13.5–15.4% to the state's GSDP. Total arrivals in 2023 reached 7 crore (with a target of 8.4 crore in 2024), including approximately 5.94 crore domestic and 1.2 million international visitors. The Char Dham and Hemkund circuit recorded 51.04 lakh pilgrims in 2025 (Kedarnath 17.68 lakh, Badrinath 16.60 lakh, Gangotri 7.58 lakh, Yamunotri 6.44 lakh, Hemkund 2.74 lakh). Tourism supports approximately 2 million jobs (direct and indirect) with a target of USD 10 billion contribution by 2030. There are 5,751 registered homestays in the state.

## 19.9 TOURISM — ISSUES & CHALLENGES

The carrying capacity crisis is the defining challenge: 7 crore tourists overwhelm a state with just 1 crore population, and Mussoorie and Nainital have exceeded their structural limits, prompting NGT-mandated audits. Ecological fragility manifests in construction on paraglacial slopes (as in the Joshimath crisis) and the concretization of Naulas. High-altitude eutrophication threatens lakes like Hemkund and Roopkund, with plastic pollution along pilgrimage routes. Extreme seasonality concentrates tourism in summer and Char Dham months, creating job insecurity and resource hollowing. Remote areas like Pithoragarh and Munsiyari suffer from a distance penalty of under-connectivity. Hotel construction blocks spring recharge zones, causing hydrological choking and summer water shortages.

## 19.11 WAY FORWARD — THE "SANTULAN" STRATEGY

The way forward demands a shift from volume to value through regenerative tourism that contributes to preservation. Spatial diversification through the Manaskhand circuit (Kumaon) and Ghaam Tapo (winter) can spread tourism across all 13 districts and 12 months. Eco-friendly transit prioritizing ropeways over road widening, complemented by electric heli-taxis for last-mile connectivity, reduces environmental impact. Tech-driven management using AI predictive analytics enables crowd control and carrying-capacity audits. Multi-modal hubs connect rail and road base camps to ropeways for eco-sensitive final miles. Community-led "Farm-to-Fork" experiences link organic produce (House of Himalayas) with homestays. GEP integration ties tourism revenue to ecological health metrics.
