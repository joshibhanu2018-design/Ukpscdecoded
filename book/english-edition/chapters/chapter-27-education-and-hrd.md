# Education & Human Resource Development in Uttarakhand

## 27.1 ROLE OF EDUCATION IN HUMAN RESOURCE DEVELOPMENT

Education is the state's "human capital multiplier" and a cornerstone of Uttarakhand's HRD strategy. It is one of the three core pillars of the Human Development Index (HDI) used to quantify human capabilities.

### Strategic Functions of Education in Uttarakhand

Education serves as a socio-economic elevator, shifting the rural demographic from low-paying subsistence agriculture to tertiary industries such as tourism, hospitality, and public services. It plays a vital role in reversing the "Money-Order" economy by fostering localized entrepreneurship and mitigating severe youth migration (Palayan). Educated female populations directly improve IMR and maternal health markers, delivering health and demographic dividends. The CM Skill Upgradation and Global Employment Scheme prepares youth for international opportunities, including Japanese language training for caregivers. The Devbhoomi Udyamita Yojana converts college campuses into Startup and Innovation Labs, building an innovation ecosystem. Under Samagra Shiksha, vocational education has been approved for 544 new schools in Agriculture, Automotive, and IT with NSQF Level-3 and Level-4 certificates.

### HDI Performance Metrics

The Mean Years of Schooling (MYS) stands at 7.5 years, ranging from 6.3 in Champawat to 8.6 in Dehradun. The Expected Years of Schooling (EYS) is 11.2 years, indicating that the average child is expected to complete secondary level. The state's budgetary commitment to education is ₹12,466 crore (16.7% of total expenditure), higher than the national state average of 15%, with the departmental allocation for 2025-26 at ₹12,660.60 crore for Education, Sports and Culture.


### The Literacy Paradox

While Uttarakhand boasts a high overall literacy rate of 78.82% (well above the national average), there is a significant gender gap of approximately 17% (Male: ~87%, Female: ~70%). More critically, high educational attainment in the hills has historically failed to restrict out-migration because the traditional curriculum does not teach youth how to monetize their local geography — leading to a pool of "educated but unemployable" youth. The state is now pivoting toward the SANTULAN (Balanced Development) model where education and skill development are intended to curb migration and enhance global employability.

## 27.3 EDUCATION SYSTEM PROBLEMS AND CHALLENGES

The "Himalayan distance penalty" and mass out-migration (Palayan) have created deep structural bottlenecks that make Uttarakhand's education challenges fundamentally different from plain-area states.

### Master Problem Table

| Problem | Nature | Scale / Data |
|---|---|---|
| **Ghost Teachers** | Teachers appointed to remote schools live in plains; sub-let duties to unqualified locals | Severe governance failure; "No work-No pay" policy introduced |
| **Single-Digit Enrollments** | Mass migration emptied hill primary schools | 826+ schools permanently closed over 5 years; 2,655 schools run on single teacher |
| **Single Teacher Schools** | Critical shortage of educators in remote areas | 2,655 primary schools; 28,000+ students pedagogically vulnerable |
| **Stalled School Mergers** | "Cluster Schools" policy announced but infrastructure within 5–10 km stalled | Terrain difficulties and administrative delays |
| **Human-Wildlife Conflict** | Wildlife attacks disrupt children's schooling in rural areas | Critical need for transport support and closer schooling |

| **Contractualization** | Increasing contractual teachers and Class IV workers | Impacts job security and institutional stability |

| **Infrastructure Deficits** | Many schools lack safe drinking water, functional girls' toilets, boundary walls | "PEVD Paradox" (Planning-Execution-Verification-Delivery failure) |
| **Learning Poverty** | Rote-based curriculum; students not trained in local value chains | Despite high literacy, employability remains low |
| **Emerging Social Threats** | Rising drug/alcohol usage among youth | Threatens to derail demographic dividend |
| **Budget Surrenders** | Departments return unspent allocations while infrastructure gaps persist | ₹2,366 crore returned by departments |

### A. The "Ghost Teacher" Phenomenon

The "Ghost Teacher" phenomenon involves teachers appointed to remote hill schools who live in the plains and sub-let their duties to unqualified locals for a fraction of their salary, severely compromising education quality in remote areas. Tech-driven reforms now require geo-fenced, biometric attendance via the Apni Sarkar portal, with salary linked to physical presence at school coordinates. The "No work-No pay" policy makes salary strictly conditional on verified attendance. CCTV-based attendance verification has been introduced, while the Shailesh Matiyani Awards provide honors, cash prizes, and 2-year service extensions for outstanding educators.


### B. Single-Digit Enrollments and School Closures (The Palayan Effect)

Mass out-migration (Palayan) from hill districts has emptied primary schools, resulting in 826+ government primary schools being permanently closed over five years, with hundreds more reporting single-digit attendance. This creates a vicious cycle: school closure forces remaining families to migrate, causing further depopulation. The proposed solution is the PM SHRI "Cluster" Model — establishing one highly equipped "School of Excellence" at the Nyaya Panchayat level with smart transport, rather than maintaining multiple unviable schools.

### C. The "PEVD Paradox" in Infrastructure

The PEVD Paradox refers to systemic failures in Planning, Execution, Verification, and Delivery. The result is budget surrenders (₹2,366 crore returned by departments) while remote schools lack functional restrooms, safe drinking water, or boundary walls. The solution being implemented is SETU Aayog mandatory "Outcome Appraisals" before releasing subsequent fund tranches.

## 27.4 UNIVERSALIZATION AND PROFESSIONALIZATION

### A. Universalization of Elementary Education

Primary GER stands at 104% with baseline literacy at approximately 78.8%, supported by the Samagra Shiksha framework. However, universal access is physically hampered by landslide-prone terrain, long walking commutes for children, and decaying primary buildings. The Palayan impact has dropped student numbers to single digits in thousands of hill primary schools. The PM SHRI "Cluster" Model addresses this by identifying 192 secondary and 34 primary schools for upgrade into "Ideal Schools" (Adarsh Vidyalaya) serving multiple surrounding hamlets. Additionally, 1,585 smart classes and virtual studios ensure remote students receive identical quality lectures as urban hubs.

### B. Professionalization and Industry-Readiness

The state is pivoting from rote-based learning to a knowledge-based economy to solve the "unemployable graduate" problem. Key initiatives include the ITI Revamp partnering with Hyundai, Maruti Suzuki, and Samsung to upgrade labs and align curriculums with modern machinery; the Uttarakhand AI Mission 2025 integrating Robotics, IoT, and Cloud Computing into polytechnics; the Devbhoomi Udyamita Yojana converting college campuses into Startup and Innovation Labs with 12,000+ students trained; and vocational integration in 544 schools with NSQF Level-3 and Level-4 certificates in Agriculture, IT, and Automotive sectors.

Uttarakhand faces a unique situation where macro-statistical success (GER 104%, literacy 78.82%) masks deep structural gaps in rural accessibility, professional employability, and social equity. The state is transitioning toward a technology-led "SANTULAN" (Balanced Development) model to address these persistent issues.

Secondary dropout is driven by safety concerns during long foot travel, domestic burdens, and substandard sanitary facilities.

## 27.6 SC/ST/OBC AND MINORITY EDUCATION

### Challenges

Scheduled Castes and Tribes face spatial disadvantage affecting remote tribal groups including Jaunsaris, Tharus, and Bhotiyas. OBC communities encounter financial barriers to higher education in hill districts. Minorities face the challenge that traditional madrasas operated without modern STEM curriculum, leaving students isolated from the mainstream.

### Government Initiatives

| Initiative | Details |
|---|---|
| **Minority Education Act 2025** | Abolishes Madrasa Board effective July 1, 2026; all institutions must affiliate with Uttarakhand Board of School Education; mandates STEM + NCERT curriculum |
| **State Minority Education Authority** | Replaces Madrasa Board; ensures NEP-2020 aligned curriculum |
| **Dual-Shift System** | Morning shift: modern NCERT curriculum (Science, Maths, English, Computers); Evening: religious education and Constitutional values |
| **PM-JANMAN** | Saturation coverage of educational infrastructure for PVTGs (Raji, Buksa) |
| **Ekalavya Model Residential Schools** | High-quality, free education specifically for tribal youth |
| **Pre/Post-Matric Scholarships** | Specialized scholarships for SC/ST/OBC/Minority students |
| **Free Coaching Centers** | For competitive exams for low-income families |
| **Free Textbooks & Uniforms** | For all girls; financial incentives for SC/ST and BPL boys |

The Minority Education Act 2025 is a landmark reform — the first of its kind in India. Effective July 1, 2026, it mandates all madrasas to affiliate with the State Board (not the independent Madrasa Board), teach STEM subjects (Science, Maths, English, Computers) in the morning shift, align with the NEP 2020 curriculum framework, and continue religious and Constitutional values education in the evening shift. This integrates minority students into the mainstream educational and employment ecosystem.

## 27.7 TEACHER TRANSFERS AND ACCOUNTABILITY

The key issues in teacher governance include the "Ghost Teacher" problem (teachers in remote posts sub-letting duties while living in the plains), single teacher schools (2,655 schools with 28,000+ students affected), and increasing contractualization that undermines long-term institutional stability.

Reforms include geo-fenced biometric attendance mandatory via the Apni Sarkar portal with salary linked to verified GPS presence, the "No Work-No Pay" policy making salary strictly conditional on physical presence at school coordinates, CCTV monitoring for visual verification of attendance, the Shailesh Matiyani Awards (cash prizes, honors, and 2-year service extensions for outstanding educators), and the demand to replace plain-area population thresholds with accessibility-based norms for hills.


## 27.8 RIGHT TO EDUCATION (RTE) ACT IMPLEMENTATION

The RTE Act mandates 25% reservation in all private unaided schools for Economically Weaker Sections and disadvantaged groups, enforced through a computerized lottery system to prevent elite bias and ensure transparency. Compliance hurdles include the difficulty of mapping "neighborhood tracking limits" due to sparse habitations in mountainous areas and long delays in government per-child monetary reimbursements to private schools. Basic entitlements include mid-day meals, free textbooks, and uniform allowances to eliminate out-of-pocket costs. The Bal Vatika innovation integrates pre-primary education (ages 3–6) into the formal system, bridging the Anganwadi–Class 1 gap. For migratory communities such as Van Gujjars, mobile school units ensure zero dropouts during transhumance. NIPUN Bharat aims for universal foundational literacy and numeracy for ages 3–9 by 2026-27.

## 27.9 SAMAGRA SHIKSHA AND FOUNDATIONAL EDUCATION

### Overview

Samagra Shiksha is an integrated holistic education mission that merged the earlier Sarva Shiksha Abhiyan (SSA, launched in 2001 for universalization of elementary education for ages 6–14) and RMSA under a single umbrella since 2018, now covering secondary education goals as well. The budget for 2025-26 stands at ₹988.13 crore (State Plan), within a broader allocation of ₹12,660.60 crore for Education, Sports and Culture. Vocational coverage includes 544 new schools and 548 laboratories approved for NSQF Level-3 and Level-4 certificates in Agriculture, IT, Automotive, and Beauty and Wellness sectors.

### PM SHRI "Cluster" Model

The PM SHRI Cluster Model merges unviable low-enrollment schools into "Schools of Excellence." Some 192 secondary and 34 primary schools have been identified for upgrade into "PM SHRI Ideal Schools" (Adarsh Vidyalaya), featuring high-quality residential education, smart transport, modern ICT, science labs, and early entrepreneurial training. These function at the Nyaya Panchayat level, serving multiple surrounding village schools.

### Vidya Samiksha Kendra

The Vidya Samiksha Kendra is a central command center linked to the national NDEAR architecture. It uses real-time data to track school performance, attendance, and outcomes, marking a shift from input-based to outcome-based learning systems.

Some 4,457 Anganwadi centres have been converted into Bal Vatika classrooms co-located near primary schools. The programme is state-run and zero-cost (equivalent to private nursery programs). SCERT-designed specialized training books prepare Anganwadi workers, and three interactive student modules cover Health (Swasthya), Dialogue (Samvad), and Creation (Srijan), with the goal of achieving developmental parity before Grade 1 entry.

## 27.13 ROLE OF KEY INSTITUTIONS

### A. University Grants Commission (UGC)

The UGC regulates general higher education curriculum and issues infrastructure development funds. It provides NEP 2020 oversight, with Uttarakhand being the first state to implement NEP in higher education under UGC mandates. It maintains quality across remote regional hubs and directly vets standards of state universities.

### B. All India Council for Technical Education (AICTE)

AICTE monitors, accredits, and regulates engineering, pharmacy, hotel management, and business institutes. Its flagship schemes include the Pragati scholarship for female tech students and Saksham for differently-abled scholars. It enforces NEAT (National Educational Alliance for Technology) and industrial internships, ensuring hill students acquire job-ready capabilities and lowering regional dropout rates.

### C. State Universities — Regional HRD Anchors

Uttarakhand Technical University (UTU) serves as the central affiliating body for technical, management, and pharmacy courses, expanding tech training to remote hills via constituent centers such as THDC Institute in New Tehri and Nanhi Pari Seemant Engineering Institute in Pithoragarh. G.B. Pant University (GBPUAT) is the cradle of India's Green Revolution, training in high-yield hill agriculture, horticulture, and sustainable mountain ecosystem management. Sri Dev Suman and Kumaun Universities provide massive regional clusters of accessible UG/PG education, reducing costs for families. Uttarakhand Open University (UOU) delivers distance learning for employed youths and women in inaccessible villages.

### D. Industrial Training Institutes (ITIs)

ITIs serve as the vocational supply line bridging secondary schooling and technical fields. They combat migration through short-term trade certifications (electrician, fitter, welder, motor mechanic, eco-tourism hospitality) and provide direct manpower supply for SIDCUL industrial pockets in Haridwar, Pantnagar, and Selakui. Industry-led corporate upgrades from Hyundai, Maruti, and Samsung support smart manufacturing training. The 16 ITIs exclusive for women address gender equity, while the CM Skill Upgradation Scheme trains Japanese-speaking caregivers for Japan placements under the global placement initiative.

### E. Uttarakhand State Council for Science and Technology (UCOST)

UCOST operates in a triple role. As a Research Funder, it finances research on landslide mitigation, spring-shed conservation, and alpine bio-prospecting. As a Science Popularizer, it operates the Regional Science Centre at Vigyan Dham in Dehradun, the Science City under construction (₹173 crore), the Science Centre in Champawat, "Labs on Wheels" mobile science laboratories deployed across all 95 development blocks, and the state's first science community radio station. As an Innovation Catalyst, it provides patent awareness workshops, Intellectual Property Rights assistance, Entrepreneurship Development Programmes, the "Adarsh Champawat" Model with Technology Resource Centres and Women Technology Centres for vocational training, and ITDA-CALC with 50 centers providing digital literacy and computer training to rural youth.

### B. Flagship Skilling Schemes

| Scheme | Details |
|---|---|
| **Mukhyamantri Kaushal Unnayan & Vaishvik Rojgar Yojana** | Global mobility catalyst; trains ANM/GNM graduates, hospitality professionals, elder-care workers; subsidizes 20% of foreign language training (Japanese, German); 75% interest subsidy on education loans up to ₹1 Lakh for visa/travel |

| **ITDA-CALC** | 50 centers providing digital literacy to rural youth to prevent migration |

### C. The Orange Economy (Creative and Cultural Skilling)

Uttarakhand is pivoting toward the Orange Economy — an economic sector built on intellectual property, cultural heritage, arts, crafts, and creative expression.

| Component | Details |
|---|---|
| **Heritage & Craft Preservation** | Standardized job roles for traditional wood carving (Aipan art), temple replicas, organic ringal-bamboo weaving; converting artisans into commercial entrepreneurs |
| **"House of Himalayas" Brand** | Premium umbrella brand marketing products from 68,000 Women SHGs (Aipan art, Ringal craft, organic millets) |
| **GI Tag Leadership** | Legal protection for 29 products (Munsiyari Rajma, Almora Copperware, etc.); boosted market value by 30–40% |
| **One District Two Products (ODTP)** | Each district identifies 2 local specialties for global promotion (e.g., Almora: Tweed + Bal Mithai) |
| **Digital Content Hub** | Youth upskilled in digital film production, drone piloting, travel vlogging for modern tourism workforce |
| **Wellness & Yoga Infrastructure** | Skill certification for Yoga instructors, Ayurvedic spa therapists, naturopathy assistants; Rishikesh as structured training node |


### D. The Silver Economy and Caregiver Mission

With an aging population in hill districts due to youth out-migration, the state has developed a Caregiver Skill Mission that trains youth as Care Sahayaks to support elderly homestays and wellness retreats. This turns the demographic challenge into a high-value service asset, with the state planning to train 1.5 lakh Care Sahayaks.

### E. Systemic Issues in Skilling

| Issue | Details |
|---|---|
| **Spatial Disconnect** | Training infrastructure skewed toward plains; mountain youth must travel long distances for advanced ITIs |
| **Weak Industry-Academia Linkages** | HEIs produce graduates with skill gaps in communication, digital problem-solving, adaptive execution |
| **Mismatched Aspirations** | Skilling offers low-wage assembly positions; mountain youth refuse due to high urban living costs vs. low savings |
| **Ecological Disruption** | Skills tied to traditional sectors (horticulture, spring-fed farming) face climate risks |
| **Skills Paradox** | High literacy (78.82%) but low employability due to rote-based education |
| **Ex-Servicemen Mismatch** | 1.39 lakh ex-servicemen; radar technicians deployed as basic security guards |
| **Gender Gap** | Rural female LFPR high (52.6%) but urban female LFPR drops to 24.4% |

## 27.17 THE WAY FORWARD — STRATEGIC RECOMMENDATIONS

### The SANTULAN Model (Balanced Development)

The state is pursuing "SANTULAN" to convert "Geography-led problems into Technology-led solutions."

| Recommendation | Rationale |
|---|---|
| **Curriculum Contextualization** | Revamp traditional syllabus to teach monetization of local geography — Himalayan eco-tourism, carbon auditing, disaster resilience |
| **PM SHRI Cluster Model (Full Transition)** | Residential "Schools of Excellence" at Nyaya Panchayat level with smart bus services; eliminates need to migrate for schooling |
| **Hub-and-Spoke Training** | Apex centers in Dehradun/Haldwani as "hubs" feeding mobile training labs ("Spokes") to remote blocks |
| **Mandatory Apprenticeships** | Credit-linked industrial apprenticeships in B.A./B.Sc./B.Com tracks under NEP 2020 |
| **Transition to "Weightless" Industries** | Low-volume, high-value sectors: IT, AYUSH, Aromatic plants (Aroma Valleys) |
| **Green Skill Development** | Train youth as "Carbon Auditors" and "Eco-mapping Technicians" to monetize GEP |
| **Orange & Green Economy Expansion** | Tie vocational training to high-value mountain products, climate-smart agriculture, eco-tourism homestays, clean-energy maintenance |
| **Localized Incubation** | District-level start-up incubators + soft loans for ITI graduates to become job creators |
| **Silver Economy Integration** | Link skill development to aging hill population; train certified geriatric caregivers |
| **Hill-Specific Service Norms** | Replace plain-area population thresholds with accessibility-based norms for mountains |
| **AI & Future Tech** | Uttarakhand AI Mission 2025; train youth in prompt engineering and modern tech roles |
| **Eco-Forestry & Spring Restoration** | Community programs to revive Naulas/Dharas; maintain subsurface moisture balance |
| **SETU Aayog Outcome Appraisals** | Mandatory outcome verification before releasing education fund tranches |


### The VIGYAN Framework

| Principle | Application |
|---|---|
| **V** — Value-based | Curriculum integrates ethics, environmental stewardship, Himalayan heritage |
| **I** — Innovation-driven | Startup labs, AI mission, Orange Economy |
| **G** — Governance-first | Biometric attendance, SETU Aayog appraisals, Vidya Samiksha Kendra |
| **Y** — Youth-centric | Global employment schemes, sports scholarships, localized incubators |
| **A** — Accountability | "No work-No pay," outcome-based funding, PGI-D tracking |
| **N** — Nature-smart | Green skills, carbon auditing, eco-tourism, spring rejuvenation |

Uttarakhand's education transformation can be understood through three phases. Phase 1 (2000–2013) focused on access and enrollment through SSA and universalization efforts. Phase 2 (2013–2020) prioritized quality and governance through RTE, RMSA, and digital classrooms. Phase 3 (2020–Present) emphasizes outcomes and employability through NEP 2020, the AI Mission, the Orange Economy, and the SANTULAN model. The state aims to transform its human capital from a "migration force" into a skilled "Himalayan growth force" for Viksit Bharat @ 2047.
