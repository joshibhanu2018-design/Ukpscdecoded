# Folk Culture of Uttarakhand: Dances, Songs, Instruments, Singers, Costumes, Food, Dialects, Paintings & Crafts

## PART A: FOLK DANCES OF UTTARAKHAND

Folk music and dance constitute the heartbeat of Uttarakhand's culture — a profound manifestation of regional identity that has been sustained across generations in the hills and valleys of the state.

## 9.1 MARTIAL AND WAR-STYLE DANCES

| Dance | Region/Tribe | Details |
|---|---|---|
| **Chholia Dance** | Kumaon | War-style dance with sword and shield; characteristic of wedding processions; vigorous movements |
| **Saron Dance** | Garhwal | Garhwal's equivalent of martial dance; with swords and shields to rhythm of drums |
| **Pauna (Pona) Dance** | Bhotiya tribe | Similar to Saron style; performed during weddings and auspicious occasions; often holding a handkerchief |

## 9.2 RELIGIOUS AND RITUALISTIC DANCES

| Dance | Region | Details |
|---|---|---|
| **Ramman** | Saloor-Dungra villages, Chamoli | UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage (2009); ritual theater for deity Bhumiyal Devta; mask dances; Ramayana narratives; celebrated annually in April-May (Baisakh) |
| **Pandav Nritya** | Kedar Valley, Garhwal | Religious dance-drama narrating Mahabharata story; involves singing, dancing, recitation |
| **Jaggar (Jagad) Dance** | Garhwal & Kumaon | Major dance to invoke deities; involves Jagarya (singer), Oji (instrumentalist), Paswa (medium upon whom deity descends) |
| **Ran Bhoot (Devta Ghirana)** | — | Organized to appease and worship souls of those martyred on battlefield |
| **Bagadwal Dance** | Central Garhwal | Performed to pray for happiness and prosperity |


## 9.3 ORNAMENTAL AND SOCIAL DANCES

| Dance | Region | Details |
|---|---|---|
| **Chonfla (Chaufla)** | Both regions | Ornamental dance comparable to India's Bihu and Garba; men and women dance in circle without musical instruments, relying on collective singing |
| **Jhora (Kumaon) / Chanchari (Garhwal)** | Kumaon & Garhwal | Ornamental circle dances; participants place hands on each other's shoulders; lead singer plays Hudki in centre; Kumaon: marks arrival of spring; Garhwal: performed on moonlit nights |
| **Thadiya Dance** | Both regions | Performed in courtyard (Thad) when newly married girls return to parental home; traditionally from Basant Panchami to Bikhoti Sankranti |
| **Jhumailo Dance** | Garhwal | Performed by women (often newly married); reflects beauty and pain of woman's heart; memories of maternal home |
| **Chhopati Dance** | Rawain Jaunpur, Tehri | Popular ornamental dance specific to this region |
| **Tandi Dance** | Garhwal | Songs mention contemporary events and deeds of famous figures |

## 9.4 TRIBAL AND REGIONAL SPECIFIC DANCES

### Tharu Tribe Dances

The main folk dance of the Tharu community is Lahchaari. The Khichdi Dance is a special dance performed by men and women together during their 8-day Holi celebrations.

### Jaunsari Tribe Dances

The major dance of the Jaunsari tribe is Harul, for which playing of the Ramtula (Ransingha) instrument is compulsory. Budiyaat Dance is performed on auspicious occasions such as births and marriages.

### Bhotiya Tribe Dances

The Bhotiya tribe performs Dhurang and Dhuring as their specific folk dances reflecting tribal culture and traditional practices. Pauna is their war dance, performed during weddings. The Jaad Bhotiya sub-tribe performs the Shant Dance, a devotional dance.

### Raji Tribe Dances

The Raji tribe performs Ringadans, a circular dance similar to the Thadiya style.


## 9.5 MISCELLANEOUS AND ACROBATIC DANCES

| Dance | Details |
|---|---|
| **Langvir Nritya** | Unique acrobatic dance from Garhwal; men balance on top of tall bamboo pole and perform gymnastics to rhythm of Dhol, Damana, and trumpets; popular in Tehri Garhwal |
| **Ghughuti Dance** | Entertaining dance performed by young boys and girls |
| **Bhaila Dance** | Performed on Diwali by lighting a pine-wood torch called a bhaila |
| **Mask Dances (Mukhot)** | Performed in Sour Valley (Pithoragarh) during Hiljatra festival; main attraction: Lakhiya Bhoot |
| **Hiranchittal Dance** | Mask dance organized during Ahun festival in Askot Patti, Kumaon |

## PART B: FOLK SONGS OF UTTARAKHAND

## 9.6 SEASONAL SONGS (RITUGEET)

Seasonal songs form a vital part of Uttarakhand's oral tradition, marking the rhythm of the agricultural calendar and natural cycles. Phag or Hori songs celebrate the festival of Holi with agricultural themes and the arrival of spring. Basanti songs are associated with the spring festival in Kumaon. Baramasa songs mark seasonal transitions and celebrate the entire cycle of the year. Chaumasa songs reflect the rainy season and the hope of meeting a beloved. Harela and Phuldei songs are dedicated to specific agricultural and spring festivals.

## 9.7 AGRICULTURAL SONGS (KRISHI GEET)

Hurkiya Baul (Hudki Bol) is a narrative-driven song tradition in Kumaon performed during the Kharif (paddy) sowing season. These songs recount local heroic tales to inspire labourers and emphasize the significance of farming. Gudail songs are traditional songs sung specifically during manual agricultural work.

## 9.8 LOVE AND ROMANCE SONGS (PRANAY GEET)

Jhumailo offers a glimpse into the pain of a woman's heart and her beauty, and is popular in Garhwal. Bajuband reflects pastoral life, love, and sacrifice of shepherds, particularly in the Ranwai area of Tehri. Chhopati is an entertaining question-and-answer song where men and women of Jaunsar sit in a circle. Nyoli and Bhagnaul are feeling-based (anubhuti pradhan) songs from Kumaon; Bhagnaul is sung by a lover in memory of the beloved during fairs.


## 9.9 SONGS OF SEPARATION AND MEMORY (VIRAHA GEET)

Khuded is a poignant song sung by young married women in Garhwal remembering their maternal home (Naihar/Maayke). Laman songs are traditional songs expressing the sorrow of separation from loved ones.

## 9.10 RELIGIOUS AND RITUALISTIC SONGS

Jagar songs are powerful compositions used to praise and invoke gods, local deities, and mythological figures. They involve the Jagaria (singer) and lead to the Dangriya (medium) entering divine possession. Sanskar or Mangal Geet are auspicious songs performed during births, naming ceremonies, and marriages. Kulachar or Birudavali are caste-specific songs sung by the Auji and Baddi communities on festive occasions at the host's house.

## 9.11 DIDACTIC AND ENTERTAINING SONGS

Chhura (Choora) songs are didactic compositions by older shepherds to impart traditional wisdom to the younger generation. Panwade or Bhadau are heroic folk ballads narrating exploits of legendary figures like Tilu Rauteli and Jeetu Bagdwal. Bhanta-Santa songs are specifically categorized as entertaining, humorous, or satirical compositions.

## 9.12 TRIBAL-SPECIFIC FOLK SONGS

The Bhotiya tribe has its own distinct song traditions including Tuvera, Bajyu, and Timli. The Jaunsari tribe is known for Chhopti, their question-and-answer songs. The iconic song "Bedu Pako," first recorded in the 1970s, remains the most globally recognized Garhwali song representing the state's culture.

## PART C: FOLK INSTRUMENTS OF UTTARAKHAND

## 9.13 MEMBRANOPHONE (LEATHER) INSTRUMENTS

The Dhol and Damau (Damoun) are the most popular and central instruments, played together on nearly all auspicious occasions. The Dhol was declared the State Instrument of Uttarakhand in 2015. The Hudka (Hudki) is a small hand-held drum and the primary instrument of the Bhotiya tribe, essential for Hudki Bol performances and the Jhora circle dance. Other instruments in this category include the Nagada (kettledrum), Tabla, Dafli, Daunr (Donr), and Dhatima.


## 9.14 AEROPHONE (WIND) INSTRUMENTS

The Ransingha (Ramtula) and Turhi were originally martial/war instruments but are now ceremonial. The Ramtula is compulsory during the Harul dance of the Jaunsari tribe. The Bhankora is a long trumpet-like instrument played specifically during Dev-Poojan (religious worship) in Garhwal. The Mochhang (Jew's Harp) is a small instrument traditionally played by shepherds while tending flocks. Other wind instruments include the Nagphani, Shankh (conch shell), Algoja (flute), and Mashakbeen (bagpipe, of European origin).

## 9.15 IDIOPHONE (METAL) INSTRUMENTS

Metal instruments include the Ghanta (bell), Thali (plate) — frequently played alongside the Daur drum during Jagar ceremonies to invoke local deities — Ghunghroo (anklet bells), Chimta (tongs), Manjira (cymbals), and Kartal.

## 9.16 CHORDOPHONE (STRING) INSTRUMENTS

String instruments include the Sarangi, Iktara (a one-stringed instrument), and Veena.

## 9.17 KEY FACTS ABOUT INSTRUMENTS & CULTURAL CONTEXT

The traditional expertise of playing Dhol and Damau rests primarily with the Auji community. The Dhol Sagar is an ancient traditional treatise detailing complex rhythms and techniques of the Dhol, authored by Keshav Das Anuragi. Uttam Das is the state's only surviving expert of this ancient knowledge. In Jagar performances, the Oji (instrumentalist) plays Hudka or Thali while the Jagarya (singer) narrates tales.

### Specialized Artists & Performers

Uttam Das is the only surviving expert of the Dhol Sagar (ancient rhythm treatise). Deva Dhami is known as the "Chholiya Man of Uttarakhand," preserving the martial war-dance of Kumaon. Narayan Singh Thapa (Dhami) is distinguished in cinematography and documentary filmmaking, famous for *Dibrugarh Ki Baadh*.

### Rediscovery of Paintings

Barrister Mukundi Lal presented Molaram's work to the world through his foundational book *Garhwal Paintings*. Other key academic works include *Rajput Painting* (Kumar Swami), *Madhya Himalaya ki Kala* (Dr. Yashwant Singh Katoch), and *Kumaon Chitrakala* (Dr. Yashodhar Mathpal).


## PART E: TRADITIONAL COSTUMES

## 9.20 GENERAL REGIONAL ATTIRE

In Garhwal, women traditionally wear a Ghagra with a Dupatta. In Kumaon, the distinctive cultural symbol for women is the red dupatta with lace called Pichhaura. Modern dress increasingly includes salwar-kurta. Men wear kurta and pyjama, with handwoven woolen shawls in winter. Shepherds wear specific clothing called Dorva in high-altitude Bugyals.

## 9.21 TRIBAL COSTUMES (DETAILED)

### Bhotiya Tribe (High-Altitude Adaptation)

The Bhotiya tribe has the most specialized woolen attire, adapted to extreme cold. Men wear the Ranga (a knee-length woolen coat), Chungthi (woolen pajamas), traditional caps known variously as Chungthi, Chukal, Gaiju, or Khagchasi, and traditional woolen shoes called Baankhe. Women wear the Chyumala (a full-sleeved robe), Chyun (an inner half-sleeved dress), Chyukala (a cap-like head covering), and Jyukhya (a waist belt).

### Jaunsari Tribe

In winter, Jaunsari men wear woolen coats with woolen pajamas (Diguba) and woolen caps (Jhagoli). Women wear woolen ghagras with kurtas and a large headscarf called Dhaant. In summer, men shift to lighter cotton versions while women wear kurti with pajamas (Jhaga), cotton ghagras, and cholis.

### Tharu Tribe

Tharu men wear dhoti, anga (short jacket), kurta, and a cap or safa (turban), with a distinguishing long braid. Women wear vibrant lehengas with cholis, embroidered kurtas, and a black dupatta (odhni). Tattooing is common among both genders.

### Boksa & Raji Tribes

Boksa men wear dhoti, kurta, sadri (jacket), and turban, while women wear lehenga, choli, and odhni. Raji men wear dhoti, angarkha, and turban, with women similarly dressed in lehenga, choli, and odhni.


## PART F: FOOD HABITS & CUISINE

## 9.22 STAPLE GRAINS & THE "MILLET MISSION"

Mandua (Finger Millet) is the quintessential winter staple, consumed as Mandua ki Roti. The State Millet Policy (Shree Anna) has an allocation of Rs 134.188 crore. Jhangora (Barnyard Millet) is used to make Jhangore ki Kheer, a popular sweet and festive dessert. Other traditional grains include Ramdana, Kauni, and Cheena.

## 9.23 TRADITIONAL DISHES & PULSES

Bhatt (Black Soybean) has a distinctive flavour and is most famously used in Chudkani. Chainsoo is a high-protein dish made from roasted black gram dal. Gahat (Horse Gram) accounts for approximately 19% of total area under pulses in the state. Kafuli (Kafli) is a green leafy preparation. Other supplementary traditional dishes include Fanu, Chaisu, Gathoni, and Thechwani.

## 9.24 INDIGENOUS HERBS & CONDIMENTS

Jamboo and Kala Jira are native mountain herbs used for tempering. Bhang ki Chutney is a distinctive sour condiment made from roasted hemp seeds and cumin. Pahadi Namak is mountain salt blended with herbs and spices.

## 9.25 ICONIC REGIONAL SWEETS

Bal Mithai is the signature sweet of Almora, which is nicknamed "Bal Mithai's house." It is made from roasted khoya coated with white sugar balls. Singauri is a Kumaoni specialty made from khoya and wrapped in Malu leaf.

## 9.26 TRIBAL FOOD & BEVERAGES

The Tharu tribe's traditional drink is Jaad, a rice liquor, and their staple diet consists of fish and rice. The Bhotiya tribe's primary alcoholic drink is Jya; they also consume Chhang. The Boksa tribe's staple diet likewise consists of fish and rice.


## 9.27 MODERN ECONOMIC BRANDING

The "House of Himalaya" is the umbrella brand promoting products such as Badri cow ghee, Munsiyari rajma, red rice, mountain salt, and Bedu. GI Tags have been secured for Badri Cow Ghee, Bedu (Himalayan fig), Berinag Tea, and Malta. Under the ODOP (One District One Product) scheme, notable products include Bal Mithai (Almora), Mandua biscuits (Bageshwar), Honey (Haridwar), Copper (Bageshwar), Iron (Champawat), and Woollen carpet (Pithoragarh). The Aanchal Cafe Plus has been launched in Dehradun as a local dairy brand and quality food outlet. The Uttarakhand Matsuri (2026) in Tokyo, Japan provides international exposure to the state's cuisine and heritage. The festivals of Ghee (Olgiya) Sankranti on the first day of Bhado and Makar Sankranti (Ghughutiya), where children are fed Ghughut (deep-fried flour and jaggery sweet), remain important cultural touchpoints for food traditions.

## PART G: DIALECTS OF UTTARAKHAND

## 9.28 LINGUISTIC CLASSIFICATION

Uttarakhand's languages belong to the Indo-Aryan family, originating from Khas (Shaurseni Apbhransh). Hindi is the first official language, with Sanskrit declared the second official language in 2010. The linguistic landscape forms a "socio-cultural mosaic" shaped by centuries of migration.

### Kumaoni Language

First scientifically studied by Ganga Dutt Upreti, Kumaoni has four major dialect groups. Eastern dialects include Kumayyon (Kali Kumaon), Soryali (Sor region), Sirali, and Askoti. Western dialects include Khasparniya (Almora/Baramandal), Chogarkhiya, Gangoi, Pakshai, Danpuriya, and Phalda Koti. The northern dialect is Johari (Johar region), and the southern dialect is Rachbhaisi (Kathgodam, Haldwani).

### Garhwali Language

The written roots of Garhwali can be traced to *Gorakhwani* by King Sudarshan Shah. Northern/tribal dialects include Marchha-Tolchha (Niti and Mana valleys), Taknori, and Bangani. Central and southern dialects include Shrinagarya (Srinagar area), Nagpurya (Nagpur Pargana), Dasholya, Salani, Chaundkoti, and Lobya. Western dialects include Jaunsari (Jaunsar-Bawar) and Ranwalti (Yamuna Valley).


### Tribal & Specialized Dialects

The Bhotiya (Jad) sub-tribe speaks Romba, which has Tibeto-Burman roots. Other Bhotiya groups speak six distinct dialects from the Himalayan Tibetan-Burmese family. The Raji (Vanrawat) tribe speaks Munda, an ancient language with Munda and Tibetan-Sanskrit influences. The Tharu speak a blend of Pahari, Awadhi, and Nepali. The Boksa tribe speaks Rachbhainsi, Bhavari, and Kumaiya.

### Linguistic Preservation Initiatives

The Uttarakhand Boli Bhasha Sansthan (Dialect Language Institute) has been established in Gauchar. The Adarsh Sanskrit Gram Yojana aims to create one model Sanskrit-speaking village in each district, and a Sanskrit University has been proposed in Jageshwar. Some dialects contain Mon-Khmer words, indicating ancient migrations from south China through Himalayan corridors. The primary challenge remains language loss as youth shift to Hindi and English.

## 9.30 THE GARHWAL SCHOOL OF PAINTING (16th–19th Century)

The Garhwal School is a prominent branch of the Pahari style, heavily influenced by Kangra art. Its origins date to 1658, when Mughal prince Suleiman Shikoh sought refuge in the region and brought court painters Tunwar Shyamdas and Hardas to the court of Prithvipati Shah. The school reached its peak under Mola Ram Tomar (1743-1833) and achieved maturity under the patronage of King Abhivira Chand.

Mola Ram's famous paintings include Chandramukhi, Mayankmukhi, Nayika, Dampati, and Utkanthita. He studied under guru Rai Singh in Srinagar, founded the Manmath Panth religious sect, and wrote the literary work *Garh Rajvansh* in Brij Bhasha.


## 9.31 TRADITIONAL FOLK & RITUALISTIC PAINTINGS

Aipan (Alpana) is a highly significant Kumaoni art form featuring geometric and religious motifs painted on walls, floors, and cloth during festivals. It is being revived by Meenakshi Khaati, known as the "Aipen Girl of Kumaon." Other traditional styles include Thapa, Po, Gajo, and Jyunti, which depict deities and floral patterns.

## 9.32 MODERN PAINTING LEGACY & MUSEUMS

Key institutions preserving the painting heritage include the Maula Ram Painting Museum in Srinagar (Pauri Garhwal), which houses the Garhwal School legacy, and the Folk Culture Museum in Bhimtal established by Yashodhar Mathpal. Other institutions include the Uday Shankar Dance and Drama Academy and the Uttarakhand Institute of Folk Arts in Almora. A notable recent discovery in 2026 was a 3,500-year-old copper figurine in Bhimtal — rare evidence of ancient "Copper Idol Art."

## PART I: CRAFTS OF UTTARAKHAND (HARSHTSHILP)

## 9.33 WOOD CARVING & WOODCRAFT (KASHT SHILP)

The Raji (Vanrawat) tribe is the most proficient in woodcraft, specializing in wooden tools and utensils. Architectural wood art featuring ornate carvings on doors, windows, and temples with floral patterns, deities, and geometric shapes is widespread across the state. Pauri has been identified as a hub for wooden furniture under ODOP.

## 9.34 RINGAAL & BAMBOO CRAFT

Ringaal, a high-altitude bamboo, is the primary material for this craft, with regional hubs in Chamoli, Almora, and Pithoragarh. Products include Kandi (baskets), Mosta (mats), and Dala. This craft is a major livelihood source for the Raji tribe.

## 9.35 METALWARE (METALLIC ART)

Almora is globally renowned for its copper industry and is called "Brass City" (Marchula). The Tamta artisan community preserves ancient metalworking traditions in copper, brass, and aluminum. Bageshwar is promoted for copper products under ODOP, Champawat for traditional iron products, and Tehri for the Tehri Nath, a specialized gold jewelry form.


## 9.36 TEXTILES, WEAVING & WOOLEN CRAFT

The Bhotiya/Shauka community is exceptionally skilled in weaving woolen garments. Key products include Pankhi (shawls), Thulma (thick blankets), and woolen carpets. The carpet industry centres are Dharchula and Munsiyari in Pithoragarh. Tehri produces natural fiber products, and Udham Singh Nagar is known for Moonj grass crafts.

## 9.37 CLAY & LEATHER CRAFT

Colourful clay idols called Deekaare or Deekra are a traditional art form. The Baadayi and Sharki communities are the traditional leather workers of the region.

## 9.38 GOVERNMENT INITIATIVES FOR CRAFTS

Himadri is the official brand for marketing Uttarakhand handicrafts in other states. The House of Himalaya is the modern brand for global recognition of SHG products. A Craft Design Centre has been established in Kashipur to modernize designs. The Uttarakhand State Shilp Ratna Award recognizes master craftsmen, and the Shilpi Pension Scheme provides pensions for artisans over 60 years of age. The Uttarakhand Matsuri (Tokyo, February 2026) served as an international showcase of handicrafts and heritage. A Writers' Village in Thano/Dehradun serves as a creative economy hub, as the state integrates the "Orange Economy" (creative economy) concept.

## PART J: CURRENT AFFAIRS — CULTURE & IDENTITY (2025-26)

## 9.39 STATE IDENTITY & CULTURAL DEVELOPMENTS

The Gaura Devi Centenary marks the 100th birth anniversary of the legendary environmentalist — the strongest symbolic culture point in recent years. Ramman continues as a living-culture example in Saloor-Dungra, Chamoli. The Gaja Ghantakarna Mahotsav 2025 is a religious-cultural event in Tehri. Basanti Bisht has received Padma recognition for Jagar singing. The Adi Kailash Yatra attracted approximately 36,000 tourists following the PM's visit, with a marathon organized, village viewpoints planned, and linkage to winter tourism. A Cultural Heritage Building has been proposed at Kalapani. The Nanda Devi Yatra has been postponed to 2027, with concerns raised about Kumaon participation. Other cultural anchors include the Tehri Lake Festival, the Literature Festival (Nainital), and the Jauljibi Fair, Vikas Mela, and Virasat Mela.

The budget session in Gairsain carries strong symbolism of hill identity and balanced regionality. The Religious Freedom (Amendment) Bill 2025 focuses on conversion through force, fraud, inducement, false identity, marriage deception, and digital propaganda. A phone ban has been implemented in Char Dham temples to maintain sanctity. Trijugi Narayan is being developed as a global wedding destination (sacred Agnikund), and the Manaskhand Mandir Mala Mission has identified 48 temples for development under the circuit. Sacred corridors are planned along the Ganga and Sharda rivers.


## 9.40 THE "SANTULAN" INTEGRATED VISION — CULTURE + ECONOMY

### The Spiritual Economy

The tertiary sector contributes 50.46% to GSDP, with culture serving as the primary economic engine. The House of Himalaya brand markets traditional products globally to empower SHGs. The ODOP scheme converts crafts to commercial assets and reduces migration. A Special Spiritual-Economic Zone concept links the Manaskhand, Kedarkhand, and Trijuginarayan development corridors.

### Eco-Cultural Synergy

Sunderlal Bahuguna's slogan "Ecology is Permanent Economy" is now being quantified through the Gross Environmental Product (GEP) framework — Uttarakhand is the first state to measure natural capital in this way. The Harela Festival is a cultural ritual linked to plantation and environmental conservation. The "Pirul Lao-Paise Pao" campaign converts pine needles to biofuel, linking tradition to climate resilience. The Tourist Carrying Capacity Survey in Nainital and Mussoorie aims to protect ecology from 6 crore annual visitors.

### Digital Cultural Renaissance

AI and Bhashini tools are being deployed to digitize and translate endangered dialects (Garhwali, Kumaoni, Jaunsari). Smart Heritage initiatives through the Smart City programme and drones are being used for heritage asset management under the Naksha Project. As Uttarakhand approaches its silver jubilee (Uttarakhand @ 25), the state is moving toward a "Himalayan Model of Development" that is welfare-secured, market-linked, technology-enabled, and spiritually anchored.
