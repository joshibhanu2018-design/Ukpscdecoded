## 18.3 FLAGSHIP ROAD PROJECTS

The Char Dham Pariyojana is a ₹11,700 crore Central government project to build 889 km of all-weather roads to Yamunotri, Gangotri, Kedarnath, and Badrinath, including 25 bridges and 13 bypasses, with strategic importance for military movement to the China border. The Delhi-Dehradun Expressway is an eco-sensitive corridor featuring a 12 km elevated wildlife stretch through Rajaji National Park, reducing travel time to 2.5 hours. Bajrang Setu, near Laxman Jhula in Rishikesh, is India's first 132.30-meter glass-floor bridge. The Kandi Road is a strategic internal road connecting Garhwal and Kumaon via Ramnagar-Kotdwar.

## 18.5 ROAD TRANSPORT — ISSUES, CHALLENGES & WAY FORWARD

### Issues

Road maintenance costs in Uttarakhand are 3–4× higher than in the plains due to Seismic Zones IV/V, landslides, GLOFs, and what is termed "Sudden Asset Erosion." The ecological fragility of the terrain means that heavy blasting and vertical slope cutting destabilizes the angle of repose, triggering mass wasting. Unscientific muck dumping into river valleys chokes drainage, raises riverbeds, and increases flash flood risk. The carrying capacity of hill stations has been breached, with 7 crore tourists overloading Nainital and Mussoorie and causing traffic paralysis. Rising accidents on treacherous roads compounded by overloaded vehicles remain a persistent concern.

### Current Initiatives

Current initiatives include iRAD (Integrated Road Accident Database project), HAMS Software (automated driver testing), and the Rahgir Yojana (financial rewards to Good Samaritans helping accident victims in the "Golden Hour"). Green Road Engineering uses Cold Mix Technology and bio-engineering (coir geo-textiles with native grasses) to stabilize slopes.

### Way Forward

The way forward involves multi-modal integration (road base camps connected to Parvatmala ropeways for last-mile eco-sensitive zones), green roads using bio-engineering over heavy concrete, an air-bridge strategy with UDAN heli-taxi as monsoon alternatives, Vibrant Villages border road development linked to national security habitation, and strict daily vehicular entry limits for hill stations to enforce carrying capacity caps.

## PART B: RAILWAY TRANSPORT

## 18.6 EXISTING RAIL NETWORK

The existing rail network is concentrated in the Tarai and Bhabar regions — Haridwar, Dehradun, Pauri, US Nagar, Nainital, and Champawat. Major terminals include Dehradun, Rishikesh, Ramnagar, Kathgodam, and Tanakpur. The first rail link was from Rampur to Kathgodam in 1884. Modern services include the Vande Bharat Express on the Dehradun-Delhi route (May 2023) and the Dehradun-Lucknow route (March 2024).

## 18.7 RISHIKESH-KARNAPRAYAG RAIL LINK (UNDER CONSTRUCTION)

This broad-gauge line stretches 125.20 km with 12 new stations including Rishikesh, Shivpuri, Devprayag, Srinagar, Rudraprayag, Gauchar, and Karnaprayag. The project is effectively subterranean, with 104 km passing through 16 main tunnels and 12 escape tunnels for disaster safety. Total excavation reaches 202.70 km. As of late 2025, underground excavation is 95.16% complete with 40 of 46 tunnel breakthroughs achieved. Expected completion is 2028, delayed from 2026 due to geological "surprises" including shear zones. The primary environmental concern is aquifer puncturing — tunneling severs underground water veins, drying village springs above. The "Silkyara Protocol" now mandates integrated escape passages in all Himalayan tunnels.

## 18.10 HELI-CONNECTIVITY

The state has expanded from 2 heliports in 2022 to 7 currently (a 2× increase), with 118 operational helipads and 30 UDAN heli-routes including Haldwani-Munsiyari, Dehradun-Almora, and Pantnagar-Pithoragarh. Uttarakhand is the first state to launch a Heli Emergency Medical Service (HEMS) from AIIMS Rishikesh for "Golden Hour" evacuations. Annual air traffic has reached 15-16 lakhs (a 79× increase over previous records). ISRO-backed satellite live tracking is mandated for all helicopters on the Kedarnath route. The civil aviation budget allocation is ₹52.50 crore for capital works.

## PART D: HYDRO-ELECTRIC PROJECTS & ENERGY

## 18.12 MACRO ENERGY STATUS

Uttarakhand possesses a hydroelectric potential of 25,000–27,000 MW, of which approximately 4,045–5,000 MW is currently installed. The state receives 12% free electricity as royalty from any hydroelectric project within its territory operated by other agencies. Transmission availability stands at 99.72% (managed by PITCUL) with line losses of just 1.02% — a remarkable efficiency. The Green Energy Cess Act of 2014 imposes a levy on power transmitted outside the state. The Hydro Policy 2025 prioritizes Small Hydro Projects (≤25 MW) and Pumped Storage Plants ("Green Batteries"). A first-of-its-kind Geothermal Policy (2025) envisions hot-spring energy nodes.

## 18.14 ENGINEERING FIRSTS & INNOVATIONS

The Chibro Power Plant (1975) was the first underground power plant in North India, receiving water from the Ichari Dam on the Tons River. The Glogi Project (1890/1907) at Bhatta Falls in Mussoorie was the first HEP in North India. Ultra Low Head Micro-Hydro Power is a new technology demonstrated in 8,200 km of canals that generates power at less than 3 m head. The Gharat Modernization programme upgrades traditional watermills with metal turbines to produce 3-5 kW micro-hydro power, serving 15-20 families each, through UREDA and HESCO. Pirul-to-Power involves pine needle gasification for electricity generation. Solar-Hydro Pumps use solar energy for water lifting to reduce conventional electricity dependency.


## 18.15 WATER SCARCITY — CAUSES & SOLUTIONS

### The Paradox

While 42% of India's water resources originate in Uttarakhand, 8,800 of 16,000 villages in the state face acute water scarcity.

### Causes

The primary causes include aquifer puncturing (where tunneling and mining permanently sever underground water veins), climate change (12% glacier area loss and chronic winter snow deficit reducing spring recharge), deforestation (the replacement of Banj Oak with Chir Pine reducing percolation), steep topography (causing rapid surface runoff and limiting groundwater recharge), and urbanization (where concretization of recharge zones produces situations like the Joshimath crisis).

### Solutions & Interventions

SARA (established November 2023) received ₹125 crore for Springshed Management, mapping exact recharge zones, with ₹5 crore each allocated for the Shipra (Nainital) and Western Nayar (Pauri) projects. The Bhagirath App (2025) enables citizens to map endangered water sources. Chaal-Khal represents traditional micro-ponds on ridges for rainwater trapping and aquifer recharge. Naula and Dhara restoration revives ancient tiered stepwells and spouts. Ecological Flow (e-flow) mandates 20-30% minimum release from all HEPs to sustain Golden Mahseer habitat. Rooftop rainwater harvesting is now mandatory for new buildings in Dehradun and Nainital. The "Gramya" Watershed programme follows a ridge-to-valley approach with check dams, contour trenches, and artificial recharge structures. The "Catch the Rain" campaign combines awareness with infrastructure. The Hydrum (Hydraulic Ram) lifts water without electricity, specifically suited for hill terrain.
