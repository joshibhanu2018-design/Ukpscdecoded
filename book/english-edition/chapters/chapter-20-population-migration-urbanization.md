## 20.1 CENSUS 2011: STATE-LEVEL DATA

According to the Census of 2011, Uttarakhand's total population is 1,00,86,292 (1.01 Crore), constituting 0.83% of India's population. Males number 51,37,773 (50.93%) and females 49,48,519 (49.07%). The rural population accounts for 69.77% and urban for 30.23%. The decadal growth rate (2001-11) was 18.81%, down from 19.20% in the previous decade, against a national average of 17.7%. Population density stands at 189 per sq km (up from 159 in 2001; national average: 382).

The sex ratio is 963 (national: 943), with a notable divergence between rural (1000) and urban (884) areas. The child sex ratio (0-6 years) is 890, having declined from 908 in 2001 (national: 919). The overall literacy rate is 78.82% (male: 87.40%, female: 70.01%), with a gender literacy gap of approximately 17%. Workforce participation is 38.4% (main workers: 74.1%, marginal: 25.9%).

### The "Spatial Concentration" Problem

Over 50% of the population resides in just three plain districts: Dehradun, Haridwar, and Udham Singh Nagar.

### The Sex Ratio Paradox

Hill districts show "artificially high" sex ratios (Almora 1139, Rudraprayag 1114) — not because of better gender equity, but because working-age men have migrated out, leaving women behind. This drives the "Feminization of Hill Agriculture."


## 20.3 SCHEDULED CASTE DATA

The SC population is 18,92,516 (18.8% of the state, higher than the national 16.6%). Haridwar has the highest absolute SC population (4,11,274), while Bageshwar has the highest percentage (27.73%) and Dehradun the lowest (13.57%). ## 20.5 MIGRATION (PALAAYAN) — COMPREHENSIVE ANALYSIS

### Magnitude

Nearly 1 in 3 households (27.8%) has at least one migrant, rising to 34.3% in hill districts. Census 2011 recorded 1,034 villages with zero population (ghost villages), which increased to 1,768 by the RDMC survey of 2018 (734 new since 2011). Daily migration from villages averaged 138 people/day between 2008-18, accelerating to 193 people/day between 2018-22. In the 2018-22 period, 3.3 lakh migrated from villages. Between 2008-18, 5.02 lakh migrated, of which 1.1 lakh permanently shifted.

### Patterns & Characteristics

Approximately 12% of migrants are short-term (under 9 months), while 85% are long-term (over 9 months). Permanent migration creates ghost villages. About 63.4% of migrant households move to urban areas of other states (primarily Delhi and UP). Migration increasingly involves women and children for education and health. Notably, 75.5% of migrants remit money back, creating a "Money Order Economy."

### Multi-Dimensional Impact

The creation of 1,768 deserted ghost villages leaves an aging population behind. The feminization of agriculture means women perform 60% of agricultural labour, collect 72% of water, and earn 45% less than their plains counterparts. A "Silver Economy" is emerging in the hills due to the aging demographic, with the state training 1.5 lakh Care Sahayaks. A strategic vacuum develops as border districts (Pithoragarh, Champawat) depopulate, creating national security risks. Hills are losing demographic weight, meaning future delimitation may reduce hill seats, causing political marginalisation. Traditional Gul irrigation channels and Van Panchayat forest councils go unmaintained as communities disperse.

## 20.6 RURAL SETTLEMENT TYPES & PATTERNS

### Settlement Types

| Type | Location | Description |
|---|---|---|
| Nucleated/Compact | Terai + Bhabar plains | Dense clustering on flat fertile land |
| Dispersed/Isolated | High-altitude zones | Scattered near individual terrace farms; minimizes travel on slopes |
| Semi-dispersed | Middle Himalayas | Small hamlets with moderate spacing |

### Geometric Patterns

| Pattern | Where Found | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Linear | Along ridges/road-heads | Most dominant in hills; accessibility-driven |
| Terraced/Contour | Hill slopes | Follows contour lines; mirrors Sopan Krishi architecture |
| Triangular/T-Shaped | River confluences, road junctions | E.g., Devprayag (triangular at confluence) |
| Circular | Traditional tribal habitats | Defence-oriented; compact |

### Local Terminology

The term Chhani or Bhunda refers to high-altitude temporary dwellings used during transhumance. Gaon is a permanent village. Tola or Majra denotes a sub-hamlet within a village.

### Smart Cities

The Dehradun Smart City project is a flagship ₹1,000 crore initiative (initiated in 2017) featuring an Integrated Command Centre, electric buses, smart poles, and smart water monitoring. Three new Smart Hill Towns are planned, including Pithoragarh and Almora, designed as "counter-magnets" to overcrowded plains. The UIRUDP (ADB-funded at $118.48 million) develops shock-resistant water and sewerage networks for Dehradun and Nainital.

### Challenges

The "Plains Paradox" sees Haridwar and US Nagar generating high GDP but ranking low on Sustainable Development Goals due to civic strain from rapid urbanization. The carrying capacity of Mussoorie and Nainital has been exceeded. Hydrological mismanagement in Dehradun, where concretization chokes the Rispana and Bindal rivers, causes urban floods. Most Urban Local Bodies have near-zero own-tax revenue, remaining fiscally grant-dependent. Disasters periodically erase urban assets, imposing a "Development Tax" with maintenance costs 3-4× higher than in the plains.

## 20.9 HUMAN DEVELOPMENT INDEX (HDI)

Uttarakhand's state HDI is 0.718. In the SDG India Index (NITI Aayog 2023-24), the state ranks 1st, tied with Kerala. The Multidimensional Poverty Index (MPI) has dropped from 9.7% (2021-22) to 6.92% (2024-25).

### The "Plains Paradox"

The top 3 HDI districts are all in the plains, generating high GDP through industrialization (SIDCUL). However, they rank lower on SDG goals due to civic strain from rapid urbanization. Hill districts lag in income levels despite exhibiting better sex ratios and environmental quality.
