# Administrative System of Uttarakhand

## 12.1 STRUCTURAL HIERARCHY — TERRITORIAL ADMINISTRATION

### Administrative Divisions

Uttarakhand is divided into 2 administrative divisions: Garhwal and Kumaon. The Garhwal Division, headquartered in Pauri (established 1969), encompasses 7 districts. The Kumaon Division, headquartered in Nainital (established 1854-55), encompasses 6 districts. Together, the state comprises 13 districts, 110 tehsils (with Pithoragarh having the highest number at 13), and 95 development blocks (82 hilly and 13 plain).

## 12.2 THE STATE EXECUTIVE & CABINET

### The Governor (Constitutional Head)

The Governor of Uttarakhand derives authority from Article 154 of the Constitution. The current Governor is Lt. Gen. Gurmit Singh, serving as the 8th occupant of the office. The Governor functions as Chancellor of all state universities and appoints the Chief Minister and the Council of Ministers.

### The Chief Minister & Council of Ministers

The current Chief Minister is Pushkar Singh Dhami. The cabinet size is constitutionally limited to 15% of total assembly strength. The governance model adopted is described as "Saturation-style," meaning every scheme must reach 100% of eligible beneficiaries. The budget frameworks are organized under the acronyms SANTULAN (Balance), GYAN (Garib, Yuva, Annadata, Nari), and VIGYAN (Innovation).

### The Secretariat

The Secretariat serves as the nerve centre for policy coordination between departments, headed by the Chief Secretary — the senior-most IAS officer in the state. The current Chief Secretary is Anand Bardhan. Notably, Radha Raturi holds the distinction of being the first woman Chief Secretary in Uttarakhand. The Secretariat has undergone a significant digital shift with the implementation of the e-Office system for paperless, transparent workflow management.

## 12.3 THE REVENUE POLICE SYSTEM — A UNIQUE LEGACY

The Revenue Police system is one of Uttarakhand's most distinctive administrative features. Originally created in 1819 by G.W. Traill through the establishment of 9 Patwari posts with dual powers, it was formally established in its current form in 1874 by Commissioner Henry Ramsay. The system operates across approximately 60% of the state's area and is unique in India at this scale — no other state maintains such a system.

Under this arrangement, revenue officials (Patwaris and Kanungos) exercise police powers of investigation and arrest. The rationale was historically economic: the low crime rate in the hills made maintaining a separate police force uneconomical, so revenue and policing functions were combined in a single official.

The Revenue Police system has not been fully removed. It is currently undergoing a phased transition to regular policing in crime-prone, tourist, and ecologically sensitive zones. Critics point out that the system lacks modern forensic training and is inadequate for handling serious crimes in a contemporary context.

## 12.4 FIELD ADMINISTRATION — THE HIMALAYAN INTERFACE

### District Administration

At the district level, the District Magistrate (DM) serves as the pivot of law, order, and revenue administration. The Additional District Magistrate assists the DM in both judicial and administrative functions.

### Tehsil Level

The Tehsildar is responsible for revenue collection, land records management, and basic judicial functions at the tehsil level, with the Naib Tehsildar providing support.

### Block Level

The Block Development Officer (BDO) functions as Secretary to the Block Panchayat and coordinates all rural development activities at the block level.

### Revenue Court Management

A significant administrative challenge exists in the form of over 50,000 pending cases creating a substantial backlog in revenue courts. The digital solution being deployed includes the Revenue Court Management System (RCMS), e-Lekh, and e-Bhoonaksha platforms, all aimed at achieving paperless resolution of land and revenue disputes.

## 12.5 OVERSIGHT AGENCIES — UKPSC, LOKAYUKTA & VIGILANCE

### A. Uttarakhand Public Service Commission (UKPSC)

The Uttarakhand Public Service Commission, constituted under Article 315 of the Constitution, was established in April 2001 with its headquarters in Haridwar. Its first Chairman was N.P. Navani, and the current Chairman (as of April 2024) is Ravi Dutta Godiyal. The Commission's primary function is merit-based recruitment into state administrative and police services through the UCS examination. It also plays an advisory role, counselling the government on recruitment rules and disciplinary actions.

A significant recent reform backing the UKPSC's work is the Anti-Copying Law (2025) — the strictest in India, providing for life imprisonment for paper leaks. Strategically, the Commission serves as a critical counter-force against youth out-migration (Palaayan) by ensuring transparent employment pathways within the state.

### B. Uttarakhand Subordinate Service Selection Commission (UKSSSC)

The UKSSSC was established on September 17, 2014, with R.B.S. Rawat as its first Chairman. The current Chairman (as of April 2024) is Ganesh Singh Martolia. The Commission conducts recruitment for Group C posts in the state government.

### C. Lokayukta (Anti-Corruption Ombudsman)

The Uttarakhand Lokayukta derives its authority from the Uttarakhand Lokayukta Act, 2014. Its jurisdiction extends over the Chief Minister, Ministers, and Group A and B public servants. The first Lokayukta was S.H.A. Raja. The office wields Civil Court powers, including the authority to summon witnesses and mandate document production. However, prolonged vacancies and delays in appointments have historically weakened the institution. The current focus is on strengthening its autonomy and streamlining grievance mechanisms.

### D. State Vigilance Agency

Established in 2002, the State Vigilance Agency investigates corruption and abuse of office by state government officials. It operates under the Criminal Procedure Code, 1973, and the Prevention of Corruption Act, with designated police stations in Dehradun and Haldwani. The Anti-Corruption Helpline 1064 allows citizens to report bribery and graft directly via an application. A key challenge identified by the CAG is "Execution-Verification Collusion" — where the same officers who execute works also verify them, enabling fake claims.

## 12.6 SPECIALIZED ADMINISTRATIVE BODIES

### A. SETU Aayog (State Institute for Empowering & Transforming Uttarakhand)

SETU Aayog replaced the legacy State Planning Commission and is modelled after NITI Aayog at the national level. Its core philosophy represents a transition from tracking "Outlays" (money spent) to measuring "Outcomes" (actual public utility). The Aayog serves as the nodal agency for Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs) and prepares the State SDG Index. It fosters cooperative sub-federalism by linking block-level issues to state policy, with a current focus on hill-specific solutions including carrying-capacity assessments and Gross Environment Product (GEP) measurement.

### B. SARA (Spring & River Rejuvenation Authority)

SARA is a newly established authority with the critical mandate to map and revive "dying springs" (Dhara) across the Tehri and Pauri belts. Mountain springs serve as the primary water source for thousands of hill villages, making this work vital to the region's sustainability. The Authority works with local governance structures, combining traditional water management knowledge with modern conservation techniques. Van Panchayats and Panchayati Raj Institutions implement SARA's revival plans on the ground.

### C. UPNL (Uttarakhand Purva Sainik Kalyan Nigam Limited)

Established in 2004, UPNL is the premier agency for rehabilitation of Uttarakhand's veteran population, which includes approximately 1,38,372 ex-servicemen and 48,361 widows (as of March 2023). Its primary service involves outsourced employment in government departments, medical colleges, and corporations. The organization faces several challenges, including the "Equal Pay for Equal Work" crisis, contractual job insecurity, and skill mismatch (radar operators deployed as guards).

Related initiatives include the Him Prahari Yojana (2022), which provides assistance for veterans to settle in border districts, and the Eco-Task Forces established in 2008, which employ ex-servicemen for afforestation and forest fire management. A notable border integration achievement saw 355 farmers (including veterans) supply 1.46 lakh kg of meat and fish to ITBP, generating ₹6.58 crore via Direct Benefit Transfer.

### D. CPPGG (Centre for Public Policy and Good Governance)

The CPPGG functions as the state's internal think-tank for evidence-based policy formulation. Its key activities include conducting baseline surveys on carrying capacity (Nainital, Mussoorie) and managing the State Indicator Framework. It provides technical support to SETU Aayog for monitoring schemes like Lakhpati Didi and helped Uttarakhand achieve a rank of 1st in India for SDGs (2023-24). The Centre ensures policies align with the "spirits of the Hill State" — prioritizing ecological development over blind infrastructure expansion.

### E. Revenue Council

The Revenue Council functions as the apex body for land administration in the state, located in Dehradun.

### F. UCOST (Uttarakhand Council for Science & Technology)

UCOST organizes the World Summit on Disaster Management (WSDM) and coordinates research in disaster management, climate science, and biodiversity.

### G. Other Specialized Bodies

Several other specialized bodies contribute to the state's administrative ecosystem. UREDA (Uttarakhand Renewable Energy Development Agency) is headquartered in Almora. The Uttarakhand Biotechnology Council is established in Pantnagar. The Badrinath-Kedarnath Temple Committee (BKTC) serves as the statutory custodian managing pilgrim logistics, finances, and the sanctity of two Dhams, handling over 6 crore annual visitors. The Rural Development & Migration Prevention Commission, based in Pauri and renamed from the earlier "Migration Commission," focuses on reversing out-migration through economic development. The State Minority Education Authority is set to replace the Madrasa Board from July 1, 2026, mainstreaming minority education.

## 12.8 THE PEVD PARADOX — CRITICAL ANALYSIS OF ADMINISTRATION

The Comptroller and Auditor General (CAG) has identified systemic failures in the state's administration, which can be understood through the PEVD framework:

**Planning failures** manifest when projects are approved without ground surveys. An example is ₹10.79 crore wasted in Kedarnath due to lack of land sanction. **Execution failures** involve poor contractor oversight and incomplete works — illustrated by the Dehradun Smart City project where ₹76 crore was paid but ₹19 crore remains unrecovered. **Verification failures** occur when the same officer executes and verifies work, enabling collusion — ₹3.79 crore was detected in fake skill development claims through this mechanism. **Delivery failures** result in assets that are built but remain non-functional, such as Sewage Treatment Plants under Namami Gange that were constructed yet failed to improve water quality.

### Additional Administrative Challenges

The state faces several additional administrative challenges. Fiscal strain is significant, with approximately 60% of revenue consumed by committed expenditure (salaries, pensions, and interest payments). A persistent "Hill-Plain Norm Disconnect" exists where one-size-fits-all norms designed for plains fail in low-density, high-altitude regions. Chronic staff shortages, particularly of technical personnel, are being addressed in the 2025-26 recruitment drive. Budget surrender remains problematic, with ₹2,366 crore returned unspent due to DPRs prepared without ground reality assessments.

### Way Forward (Administrative Reforms)

The path forward for administrative reform involves several key interventions. Hill-specific norms must define service delivery based on distance and terrain rather than population alone. Audit must be decoupled from execution through mandatory independent third-party audits for major projects. Geo-tagging and AI verification should be mandatory before clearing contractor bills. Outcome-based budgeting, following the SETU Aayog model, should track results rather than expenditure. Cyber resilience requires scaling up the ₹15 crore IT security budget and expanding CERT-UTK. Finally, a CAG Compliance Cell within the CMO should ensure that audit findings trigger immediate FIRs, not merely departmental inquiries.
