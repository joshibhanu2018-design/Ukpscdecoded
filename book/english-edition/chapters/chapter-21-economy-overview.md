# Economy of Uttarakhand — Features, GSDP, Sectoral Structure & Income Sources

## 21.1 OVERVIEW: THE UTTARAKHAND ECONOMIC MODEL

Uttarakhand's economy has undergone a **26-fold transformation** since its formation on 9 November 2000, transitioning from a subsistence-based "Money Order Economy" (dependent on remittances from migrant soldiers and workers) to a diversified **Himalayan growth model** driven by manufacturing, tourism, and services.

At the time of state formation in 2000-01, the Gross State Domestic Product (GSDP) stood at approximately ₹14,786 Crore, which has since grown to ₹3,81,889 Crore at current prices in 2025-26 — a roughly 26-fold increase. Per Capita Income has risen from about ₹15,000 to ₹2,73,921, an 18-fold growth. Tax revenue has expanded from ₹233 Crore to ₹11,862 Crore (a 51-fold increase), while the total budget size has grown from approximately ₹5,000 Crore to ₹1,11,703 Crore — a 22-fold expansion. The number of MSME units has multiplied six times, from 14,163 to 79,394–94,595, and exports have risen from negligible levels to ₹14,952 Crore. The state now hosts over 1,100 recognized startups (up from zero at formation), and household electrification has achieved 100 per cent coverage, up from 60 per cent.

### Key Characteristics of the Economy

1. **Service-Sector Dominance:** Over 50% of GSDP from the Tertiary sector (tourism, trade, transport)
2. **Manufacturing Corridor in Plains:** Secondary sector concentrated in Haridwar-US Nagar-Dehradun belt (SIDCUL IIEs)
3. **Primary Sector Crisis:** Agriculture contributes less than 10% to GSDP despite employing approximately 58% of the rural workforce
4. **High Per Capita Income:** Consistently above national average due to small population combined with high industrial output
5. **Ecological Accounting Pioneer:** First state globally to index Gross Environmental Product (GEP)
6. **Tourism Dependence:** Approximately 7 Crore annual tourists for a population of roughly 1 Crore; tourism contributes 9% directly and 15% indirectly to GSDP
7. **Dual Economy (Hills vs Plains):** 86% of industrial output from 2 plain districts; hill districts face capital flight and out-migration

## 21.5 STATE BUDGET & PUBLIC FINANCE (2026-27)

### Macro-Budgetary Overview

The total budget size for 2026-27 stands at ₹1,11,703.21 Crore, crossing the ₹1 Lakh Crore mark for the first time. Total Revenue Receipts amount to ₹60,552.90 Crore, of which Own Tax Revenue constitutes ₹31,620.03 Crore and the State's Share in Central Taxes contributes ₹13,637.15 Crore. Non-Tax Revenue adds ₹4,873.38 Crore. The state maintains a Revenue Surplus of ₹2,536.33 Crore, confirming its status as a surplus state. The Fiscal Deficit remains within FRBM limits at 2.94–3.62% of GSDP, while the Primary Deficit stands at 1.61%. Total Debt Liabilities amount to 24.23% of GSDP. Capital Expenditure has reached a record ₹14,763.13 Crore.

### Revenue Sources

In descending order of priority, revenue comes from: (1) Own Tax Revenue, which is the largest source comprising GST, Excise, and Stamps; (2) State Share in Central Taxes at ₹13,637.15 Crore; (3) Public Debt through borrowing; and (4) Grants-in-Aid from the Centre.

### Expenditure Priorities

Notably, 61% of revenue receipts are consumed by non-discretionary committed items (Salary 33% + Pension 16% + Interest 12%). This severely restricts the state's fiscal flexibility for development spending.

## 21.6 STRATEGIC BUDGET FRAMEWORKS & ACRONYMS

### SANTULAN (संतुलन — Balanced Development)

SANTULAN serves as the central governing philosophy of Budget 2026-27. The acronym stands for: **S** — Samaveshi Vikas (Inclusive Development); **A** — Atmanirbhar Uttarakhand (Self-Reliant); **N** — Nayi Soch (New Thinking); **T** — Tivra Vikas (Rapid Development); **U** — Unnat Gaon evam Shahar (Advanced Villages & Cities); **L** — Lok Sahbhagita (Public Participation); **A** — Arthik Shakti (Economic Strength); **N** — Nyayapurna Vyavastha (Justice-oriented System).

### GYAN (ज्ञान — Targeted Welfare)

The GYAN framework identifies four key beneficiary groups: **G** — Garib (Garib Kalyan — Welfare of the Poor); **Y** — Yuva (Youth); **A** — Annadata (Farmers); **N** — Nari Shakti (Women).

### VIGYAN (विज्ञान — Modern Governance)

VIGYAN encapsulates the governance approach: **V** — Value-based; **I** — Innovation-driven; **G** — Good Governance; **Y** — Youth-powered; **A** — Accountability; **N** — New Uttarakhand.

### KEDAR (केदार — Rural Revitalization)

The KEDAR framework targets rural areas: **K** — Kaushal Vikas (Skill Development); **E** — Ecology Promotion; **D** — Dharohar Sanrakshan (Heritage Conservation); **A** — Awasthapana (Infrastructure Development); **R** — Reverse Migration.

### MANAS (मानस — Spiritual & Cultural Inspiration)

MANAS reflects the cultural vision: **M** — Mangalkari (Auspicious); **A** — Avsar-yukta (Opportunity-rich); **N** — Navonmeshi (Innovative); **A** — Adhyatmik (Spiritual); **S** — Sanskritic (Cultural).

### SAPTRISHI (सप्तऋषि — Seven Pillars of Development)

Under the NAMO Budget theme (Innovation, Atmanirbhar, Mahan Virasat, Ojasvi Manav Sansadhan), the seven pillars are: (1) Agriculture — high-value crops and market linkages; (2) Industry — MSMEs and Flatted Factories; (3) Energy — Small Hydro and Solar "Green Batteries"; (4) Infrastructure — record ₹14,763 Crore capital outlay; (5) Connectivity — Heli-grid and Rishikesh-Karnaprayag Rail; (6) Tourism — all-season model and Manaskhand Mission; (7) Ayush — Global Wellness Economy.

### Three Growth Drivers (Self-Reliant Uttarakhand)

Agriculture, Industry, and Tourism have been identified as the tripartite engine for economic self-reliance.

## 21.7 ECONOMIC REFORMS SINCE STATE FORMATION (2000–2026)

### Phase 1: Industrial Foundation (2000–2005)

The State Infrastructure & Industrial Development Corporation (SIDCUL) was established in 2002, leading to the creation of 7 Integrated Industrial Estates. The Special Federal Package of 2003 provided a 10-year central excise and income tax holiday that attracted major FMCG, Pharmaceutical, and Automobile giants. The granting of Special Category Status on 2 May 2001 provided a favourable 90:10 Centre-to-State funding ratio.

### Phase 2: Fiscal Modernization (2005–2017)

The FRBM Act (2005) established fiscal responsibility norms, capping the deficit at 3% of GSDP. VAT was introduced in April 2005, replacing the outdated sales tax. GST implementation in July 2017 brought 2,22,461 new registrations, a unified market, and ₹7,301 Crore in SGST collection. This period also saw the phased transition from the colonial-era Revenue Police to Regular Police.

### Phase 3: Digital & Governance Revolution (2017–2025)

The Apni Sarkar Portal now provides 954 services across 78 departments with over 1 Crore applications processed at a 99% rate. The Single Window System (investuttarakhand.com) achieved "Top Achiever" status in BRAP 2024. The Devbhoomi Parivar Yojana established a 360-degree family database for proactive benefit delivery. A Digital Forensic Lab with AI-integrated tax compliance was set up in Dehradun, along with the gamified tax enforcement app Bill Lao Inam Pao (BLIPUK). E-Office, e-Bhulekh, and e-RCMS have enabled paperless administration.

### Phase 4: Landmark Legal Reforms (2025–2026)

Uttarakhand became the first state in India to implement the Uniform Civil Code on 27 January 2025, establishing equal property rights for daughters. The Anti-Copying Law introduced life imprisonment for paper leaks. Bhu-Kanoon (2025) banned outsiders from purchasing agricultural land in 11 hill districts. The Religious Freedom Amendment (2025) prescribes 3–10 years imprisonment for forced conversion. On the economic front, the state pioneered the Gross Environmental Product (GEP) — becoming the first state globally to index ecological value alongside GSDP. The Green Hydrogen Policy (2026) targets 100 kilotonnes by 2030, and the Geothermal Energy Policy (2025) has initiated UREDA and UJVNL-led exploration.

## 21.8 TOURISM AS AN ECONOMIC ENGINE

Tourism contributes approximately 9% directly and 15% indirectly to the state's GDP. The annual tourist load stands at roughly 7 Crore visitors for a state population of about 1 Crore. Tourism accounts for 17.10% of all state employment. The Chardham Yatra 2025 recorded 51.04 Lakh pilgrims. The state has set a target of $10 Billion in tourism revenue by 2030, which would constitute 15% of GSDP. For context, while tourism contributes 7% to India's national GDP, Uttarakhand's 9% direct contribution demonstrates its high degree of specialization in this sector.

## 21.9 INVESTMENT & EXPORT LANDSCAPE

The Global Investors Summit (2023) resulted in MOUs worth ₹3.5 Lakh Crore, while the Investment Festival 2025 has an active Strategic Advisory Committee working on export policy and incubation centres. Exports have grown from ₹3,530 Crore in 2011-12 to ₹14,952 Crore in 2024-25, representing a 400% increase. On the Export Preparedness Index 2024, the state secured Rank 1 in the Himalayan Category. The state now boasts 29 GI Tags (including Aipan, Ringal, Badri Cow Ghee, Berinag Tea, Malta, and Bedu), a ₹200 Crore Venture Capital Fund for startups, and a "Top Achievers" ranking in BRAP 2024.

## 21.10 FISCAL ISSUES & HILL-STATE DISADVANTAGE

The 16th Finance Commission proceedings present a concern, as competitive urban-challenge funding may disadvantage smaller hill Urban Local Bodies. Expected Central Assistance amounts to approximately ₹24,000 Crore in the Finance Commission frame, though the discontinuation of Revenue-deficit, sector-specific, and state-specific grants increases the importance of disaster and local-body grants. The Credit-Deposit (CD) Ratio crisis persists, with the state average at 54.67% while hill districts like Bageshwar (24.66%) and Almora (26.97%) experience severe capital flight from hills to plains. The committed expenditure of 61% locks fiscal resources, limiting agile development spending. Additionally, the "Plains Paradox" sees Haridwar and US Nagar generating high GDP but ranking lowest on the SDG Index due to civic strain.

## 21.11 CURRENT AFFAIRS 2025-26 — ECONOMY HIGHLIGHTS

MSMEs currently contribute approximately 13% to GSDP. Rani Bagh HMT has been proposed as an IT Hub. The ₹200 Crore Venture Capital Fund supports the startup ecosystem, which already hosts 1,750 startups with a target of 1,000 more in five years. The state envisions startup culture as a pathway for converting SHGs into formal enterprises. The Aromatic Garden initiative operates in Lalkuan alongside the Centre for Aromatic Plants in Selakui. The AYUSH sector's integration with the Europe FTA promises wellness economy expansion. The House of Himalayas brand serves as an umbrella for local products including millets, kidney beans, red rice, turmeric, honey, herbal tea, Aipan, and Pichhora. The Mega Food Park and Patanjali facilities provide processing-side support. Eight sugar mills are currently operational. The Orange/Creative Economy is being discussed as a new economic sector, while the state positions itself as a first mover in Green Carbon Credits. The 6th State Finance Commission is chaired by N. Ravishankar.
