## 25.1 NATURE AND TYPOLOGY OF DISASTERS IN UTTARAKHAND

Uttarakhand's young, unstable geological formations (Himalayan orogeny), heavy monsoon dynamics, and anthropogenic pressures expose it to diverse natural hazards. The state sits on active tectonic zones, receives intense monsoonal rainfall, and faces growing developmental pressures — making it one of India's most disaster-prone regions.

### Master Disaster Classification Table

| Disaster Type | Nature / Mechanism | Vulnerable Zones | Recent Major Event (2025–26) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Earthquake** | Tectonic strain release between Indian & Eurasian plates | Entire state (Zone IV & V); Central Seismic Gap | Constant micro-tremors (2025–26); high risk of Magnitude 8+ event |
| **Landslide** | Slope failure due to gravity, rain, toe-cutting of hills | MCT & MBT belts; Chamoli, Rudraprayag, Uttarkashi | July 2026: 111 roads blocked state-wide |
| **Cloudburst (Badal Fatna)** | Extreme rainfall >100 mm/hr in small area; orographic lifting | Higher Himalayas: Rudraprayag, Uttarkashi, Chamoli | Aug 5, 2025: Dharali flash flood (61+ dead) |
| **Forest Fire (Vanalanal)** | Dry pine needle ignition during April–June | Pine belts: Almora, Nainital, Pauri, Tehri | Summer 2026: 1,000+ incidents; 300% spike |
| **GLOF** | Breach of unstable moraine-dammed glacial lakes | 500+ glacial lakes in High Himalayas | 2026 study: 500 expanding lakes identified |
| **Land Subsidence** | Ground sinking due to aquifer drainage/unstable debris | Joshimath, Nainital, Mussoorie | Joshimath: ₹1,658 Cr R&R Plan approved |
| **Avalanche** | Rapid flow of snow/ice/rock down high-altitude slopes | Gangotri, Kedarnath, Nanda Devi massifs (>3500m) | Oct 2022: Draupadi Ka Danda II tragedy |
| **Drought** | Erratic snowfall (Western Disturbances) & weak monsoons | Agricultural zones; spring-fed areas | 2025–26: Historic "Snow Drought" |

| Parameter | Details |

| Parameter | Details |

## 25.2 MAJOR LANDMARK DISASTER EVENTS

### A. The 2013 Kedarnath Disaster (The Himalayan Tsunami)

| Parameter | Details |
|---|---|
| **Date** | June 16–17, 2013 |
| **Location** | Kedarnath, Rudraprayag District |
| **Trigger** | Historic multi-day cloudburst + breach of Chorabari Tal (glacial lake) above Kedarnath |
| **Mechanism** | GLOF merged with swollen Mandakini and Saraswati rivers creating massive debris flows |
| **Casualties** | 4,000+ dead |
| **Impact** | Wiped out Kedarnath town's surrounding infrastructure; stranded tens of thousands of pilgrims; crippled tourism-dependent economy |
| **Notable** | Main Kedarnath shrine miraculously survived |
| **Legacy** | Accelerated deployment of state disaster response mechanisms; strengthened scientific monitoring of upper catchment zones; led to Char Dham All-Weather Road project |

> **KEY FACT BOX: Kedarnath 2013**
>
> The 2013 Kedarnath disaster remains the **benchmark disaster** for Uttarakhand's disaster policy. It fundamentally transformed the state's approach from reactive relief to proactive technology-driven preparedness. The Chorabari Tal breach demonstrated how GLOFs compound with cloudbursts to create "Himalayan Tsunamis."

### B. The 2021 Chamoli Disaster (Raini / Rishiganga Flood)

| Parameter | Details |
|---|---|
| **Date** | February 7, 2021 |
| **Location** | Chamoli District (Rishiganga & Dhauliganga valleys) |
| **Trigger** | Massive rock-ice avalanche broke off from Nanda Ghunti/Trisul area |
| **Mechanism** | Avalanche plunged into Rishiganga river → destructive debris-laden flash flood |

## 25.3 SEISMIC ZONES IN UTTARAKHAND

Uttarakhand is located in one of the most seismically active regions globally, sitting squarely across the active tectonic zone where the Indian Plate continuously thrusts beneath the Eurasian Plate.

### Seismic Zone Classification

| Zone | Intensity | Districts Covered | Risk Profile |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Zone V** (Very Severe) | Magnitude 7.0+ | Entire districts: Pithoragarh, Bageshwar, Chamoli, Rudraprayag; Northern parts: Uttarkashi, Tehri Garhwal, Almora, Champawat | Prone to earthquakes of Magnitude 8+; sits directly on active fault lines |
| **Zone IV** (Severe) | Magnitude 6.0–7.0 | Dehradun, Haridwar, Udham Singh Nagar, Nainital; Southern parts: Pauri Garhwal, Tehri | Severe threat from ground shaking and liquefaction, especially in sediment-heavy plains |

### The Central Himalayan Seismic Gap

| Parameter | Details |
|---|---|
| **Definition** | An area where historical stress accumulation suggests NO major release has occurred in centuries |
| **Location** | Significant portion of Uttarakhand's underground arc |
| **Implication** | High probability of a major or great magnitude earthquake in the future |
| **Scientific Basis** | Tectonic strain builds continuously; absence of periodic release means energy is "locked" |
| **Current Status** | Constant micro-tremors reported in 2025–26; seismologists warn of high risk |

### Key Tectonic Lines

| Tectonic Feature | Location | Significance |
|---|---|---|
| **Main Central Thrust (MCT)** | Passes near Joshimath, Kedarnath, Munsiyari | Most active earthquake/landslide zone |
| **Main Boundary Thrust (MBT)** | Passes near Nainital, Mussoorie | High landslide risk |

> **KEY FACT BOX: Seismic Vulnerability**
>
> The entire state falls in **Zone IV and V** — there is NO low-risk zone in Uttarakhand. The Central Himalayan Seismic Gap is considered one of the most dangerous geological features in India, with potential for a **Magnitude 8+ "Great Earthquake"** that has not occurred in recorded history.

## 25.4 ECOLOGICALLY SENSITIVE AREAS (ESAs) & CONSERVATION FRAMEWORKS

Given its fragile topography and rich biodiversity, parts of Uttarakhand are protected under strict environmental governance regimes notified by the Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change (MoEFCC).

### A. Bhagirathi Eco-Sensitive Zone (BESZ)

| Parameter | Details |
|---|---|
| **Coverage** | 4,179.59 sq. km stretch from Gaumukh to Uttarkashi town along the Bhagirathi river (100 km) |
| **Key Restrictions** | Total ban on new hydropower projects >2 MW; prohibition of riverbed mining; ban on polluting industries (Red Category) |
| **Significance** | Only stretch of the Ganga allowed to flow in its pristine, free-flowing form |
| **Policy Framework** | Landmark governance framework regulating commercial tourism and protecting upper Himalayan ecosystem |

### B. Doon Valley Eco-Sensitive Zone

| Parameter | Details |
|---|---|
| **Status** | Notified in 1989 — one of India's first ESZs |
| **Key Restrictions** | Ban on limestone quarrying/mining; strict limits on land-use change from Green/Agriculture to Commercial/Industrial |
| **Governance** | All major constructions require clearance from the Doon Valley Notification Committee |

### C. Protected Area ESZs (Buffer Zones)

Every National Park and Wildlife Sanctuary has a notified ESZ buffer (usually 1–10 km) where commercial activities are regulated.

| Protected Area | ESZ Regulation |
|---|---|
| **Jim Corbett** | Regulates resort density in Ramnagar/Mohan areas |
| **Rajaji** | Buffer zone restrictions on commercial development |
| **Gangotri** | Strict regulation of tourism and construction |
| **Govind Pashu Vihar** | Controls mining and industrial activity |
| **Nanda Devi Biosphere Reserve** | Strict no-entry core zone; regulated trekking in buffer |

### D. Gross Environment Product (GEP)

| Parameter | Details |
|---|---|
| **Nature** | Uttarakhand's pioneering administrative tool |
| **Purpose** | Quantifies ecological health — measuring services rendered by forests, water bodies, soil, and clean air |
| **Goal** | Balance economic growth with environmental preservation |

> **EXAM TIP:** The Bhagirathi Eco-Sensitive Zone is a frequently asked topic. Remember: **Gaumukh to Uttarkashi**, **4,179.59 sq. km**, and the key restriction is a **total ban on hydropower >2 MW**.

## 25.5 ADVANCED EARLY WARNING SYSTEMS (EWS) & TECHNOLOGY INTERVENTIONS

Uttarakhand has moved from a "reactionary" relief model to a "technology-driven" prediction and prevention system. Modern technology pipelines are deployed to mitigate loss of life from rapid-onset hydro-meteorological and seismic hazards.

### Summary Table: Early Warning Systems

| System | Technology & Function | Status (2026) |
|---|---|---|
| **Bhudev App** | Earthquake EWS: Sensors detect P-waves and send siren alert 10–40 seconds before destructive S-waves | Operational; expanding sensor network (170+ sensors) |
| **Doppler Weather Radars** | Detects cloud density and wind moisture to predict cloudbursts 30–60 minutes in advance | Operational at Surkanda Devi, Mukteshwar, Lansdowne; new radars approved for Haridwar, Pantnagar, Auli |
| **Siren Towers** | Flash Flood: Automated river-gauge sensors trigger loud sirens in riverbank towns | Installed in Kedarnath, Rishikesh, Haldwani |
| **SILAAS** | Satellite Integrated Landslide Assessment and Alert System | Operational via USAC |
| **FSI Fire Alerts** | SNPP-VIIRS/MODIS satellites detect heat anomalies; SMS alerts within 45 minutes | Highly active during summer 2026 |
| **Cell Broadcast EWS** | Forces loud siren on all mobile phones in specific tower radius (even on silent mode) | Tested in 2026 |
| **InSAR Monitoring** | Satellite radar tracks millimeter-level ground movement on highways | Piloted by MoRTH on NH-58 |

---

### A. Earthquake Early Warning System (EEWS) — "Bhudev" App

| Parameter | Details |
|---|---|
| **Collaboration** | Developed jointly by USDMA and IIT Roorkee |
| **Sensor Network** | 170+ subsurface strong-motion accelerometers across high-risk seismic zones |
| **How It Works** | Detects primary waves (P-waves — faster but non-damaging) before destructive secondary waves (S-waves) arrive |
| **Alert Delivery** | Official "Uttarakhand Bhudev App" broadcasts automated audio-visual warnings directly to smartphones |
| **Lead Time** | 10–40 seconds depending on epicenter distance |
| **Threshold** | Sends alerts for damaging earthquakes (>5.0 magnitude); notifications for minor tremors |
| **Special Feature** | Sirens loudly even on silent mode; sends GPS location to rescue teams |
| **Backend** | Provides support coordinates to USDMA for rapid search-and-rescue dispatch |

> **KEY FACT BOX: How Earthquake EWS Works**
>
> **P-waves** (Primary waves) travel faster but cause no damage → detected by sensors first.
> **S-waves** (Secondary waves) travel slower but cause destructive shaking → arrive seconds later.
> The system exploits this time gap to warn citizens BEFORE the destructive shaking begins. Even 10 seconds of lead time can save lives by allowing people to move to safe zones.

---

### B. Doppler Weather Radars

| Parameter | Details |
|---|---|
| **Deployed By** | India Meteorological Department (IMD) in coordination with state authorities |
| **Function** | High-resolution, real-time spatial tracking of clouds; tracking severe storms, cloudburst triggers, and heavy downpours |
| **Lead Time** | 30–60 minutes advance prediction of cloudbursts |

| **Output** | Feeds critical inputs into flash-flood early warning models |

| **Locations** | Installed along river banks in Kedarnath, Rishikesh, and Haldwani |

## 25.6 CHRONIC ANTHROPOGENIC (MAN-MADE) DISASTERS

Anthropogenic disasters are man-made crises caused by engineering failures, unplanned urbanization, or ecological neglect. In Uttarakhand, these are often **"Compound Disasters"** — where human intervention destabilizes fragile Himalayan slopes, turning natural weather variations into destructive events.

### A. The Joshimath Sinking Crisis (Land Subsidence)

| Parameter | Details |
|---|---|
| **Context** | Holy town of Joshimath — strategic gateway to Badrinath and Auli — built on ancient landslide-prone moraine deposit with low load-bearing capacity |
| **The Crisis** | Catastrophic land subsidence; wide structural cracks across 800+ houses, roads, hotels, and land parcels |
| **Anthropogenic Drivers** | (1) Unregulated multi-story construction overloading loose soil; (2) Absence of proper sewerage/drainage — wastewater soaked into ground, turning soil into fluid slurry; (3) Cumulative stress from hydroelectric tunneling (Tapovan-Vishnugad project breached major aquifer in 2009) |
| **Management Response** | Immediate evacuation; expert committees (IITs, Wadia Institute, ISRO); safety mapping (Danger, Buffer, Safe zones); ₹1,658 Crore R&R Plan |
| **Rehabilitation** | Red Zone residents permanently resettled to Gauchar and Pipalkoti; Yellow Zone houses retrofitted with stronger foundations |
| **2026 Status** | Town in "stabilization and retreat" phase; subsidence active in pockets |

---

### B. Unchecked Hydropower Projects (Chamoli 2021)

| Parameter | Details |
|---|---|
| **Context** | High-altitude rock-ice avalanches triggered catastrophic debris flows |
| **Impact** | Completely wiped out Rishi Ganga project (24 MW); heavily damaged Tapovan-Vishnugad project (520 MW); ~200 dead |
| **Anthropogenic Driver** | Proliferation of run-of-the-river hydro projects in narrow, volatile gorges ignoring cumulative ecological impact assessments |

---

### C. Silkyara-Barkot Tunnel Collapse (November 2023)

| Parameter | Details |
|---|---|
| **Context** | Part of Char Dham road-widening mega project |
| **Impact** | 41 workers trapped for 17 days |
| **Anthropogenic Drivers** | (1) "Shear Zone Ignorance" — tunnel crossed highly fractured unstable rock without adequate reinforcement; (2) No independent escape tunnel built (standard international practice skipped) |

### D. Forest Fire Surge & Pine Monoculture

| Parameter | Details |
|---|---|
| **Context** | Summer fire seasons now routinely burn thousands of hectares |
| **Anthropogenic Drivers** | (1) Colonial-era replacement of fire-resistant Oak forests with commercial Chir Pine; (2) Pine needles (Pirul) contain highly flammable resin forming thick forest floor blankets; (3) Intentional setting of fires by locals for grazing stimulation |
| **2026 Status** | Record surge — 1,000+ incidents in Jan–Apr 2026 due to "Snow Drought" |

---

### E. Char Dham Highway Slurry Disasters (Ongoing)

| Parameter | Details |
|---|---|
| **Context** | 900 km road-widening project meant to improve pilgrimage transit triggered massive wave of active landslide zones |
| **Anthropogenic Drivers** | (1) Vertical slope cutting at 90-degree angles instead of terraced slopes; (2) Millions of tons of excavated "muck" pushed directly into Alaknanda and Bhagirathi rivers, raising riverbeds and causing downstream flash floods |

---

### Underlying Causes of Man-Made Disasters

| Root Cause | Mechanism |
|---|---|
| **Unregulated Urbanization & Tourism Pressure** | Unscientific real estate booms; encroachment on river floodplains; violation of local carrying capacities |
| **Heavy Infrastructure Blasting** | High-intensity explosives for road widening and tunneling fracture unstable phyllite and quartzite formations |
| **Disruption of Natural Drainage** | Blockage of springs, rivulets, and sub-surface channels by debris dumping and concrete expansion; rainwater seeps into vulnerable slopes |
| **Lack of Geological Surveys** | Projects proceed without comprehensive seismic, geological, and hydrological risk assessments |

## 25.7 THE DISASTER MANAGEMENT ACT, 2005

Enacted in response to systemic gaps exposed by mega-disasters (1999 Odisha Cyclone and 2004 Indian Ocean Tsunami), the DM Act, 2005 provides the overarching legal and institutional architecture for disaster governance in India.

### Paradigm Shift

| Before DM Act 2005 | After DM Act 2005 |
|---|---|
| Reactive, post-disaster relief and rehabilitation | Proactive prevention, mitigation, preparedness, and capacity-building |
| Ad-hoc response mechanisms | Seamless multi-tiered statutory hierarchy |
| No dedicated response forces | Specialized NDRF created by statute |
| No dedicated funding streams | Two-tier financial architecture (Response + Mitigation funds) |

### Multi-Tiered Statutory Structure

```
NATIONAL LEVEL
├── NDMA (National Disaster Management Authority) — Chaired by Prime Minister
├── NEC (National Executive Committee) — Headed by Union Home Secretary
└── NDRF (National Disaster Response Force)

STATE LEVEL
├── SDMA (State Disaster Management Authority) — Chaired by Chief Minister
├── SEC (State Executive Committee) — Headed by Chief Secretary
└── SDRF (State Disaster Response Force)

DISTRICT LEVEL
└── DDMA (District Disaster Management Authority) — Co-Chaired by DM & Zila Parishad Chair
```

### Key Statutory Provisions

| Section | Provision | Significance |
|---|---|---|
| **Section 3** | Establishment of NDMA | Creates apex national body |
| **Section 14** | Every state must establish SDMA | Mandates state-level institutionalization |
| **Section 25** | District authorities to establish DDMA | Ensures ground-level coordination |
| **Section 33** | Emergency Procurement | Allows bypassing standard tendering during emergencies for quick acquisition of relief materials |
| **Section 44** | Establishment of NDRF | Creates specialized response force |
| **Sections 51–60** | Penal Provisions | Fines and imprisonment for obstructing rescue, raising false alarms, or misappropriating relief funds |

### Two-Tier Financial Architecture

| Fund Type | National Level | State Level | Purpose |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Response Fund** | National Disaster Response Fund (NDRF) | State Disaster Response Fund (SDRF) | Immediate rescue and relief works |
| **Mitigation Fund** | National Disaster Mitigation Fund (NDMF) | State Disaster Mitigation Fund (SDMF) | Long-term risk-reduction projects (slope stabilization, earthquake-resistant buildings) |

> **EXAM TIP:** The DM Act 2005 created a **three-tier structure** (National → State → District). Key sections to remember: **Section 3** (NDMA), **Section 14** (SDMA), **Section 44** (NDRF), **Section 33** (Emergency Procurement), and **Sections 51–60** (Penal Provisions).

## 25.10 ROLE OF NDRF AND SDRF

### Comparative Framework

| Parameter | NDRF (National) | SDRF (Uttarakhand) |
|---|---|---|
| **Established Under** | Section 44, DM Act 2005 | State Government (modeled on national framework) |
| **Administrative Control** | Ministry of Home Affairs (MHA) | State Government (Uttarakhand State Police) |
| **Nature** | Central, paramilitary-trained force | State-level force raised from state police |
| **Deployment** | Macro/severe crises requiring heavy machinery, specialized airlift | Immediate first responder; localized rapid response |
| **Response Time** | Deployed dynamically during peaks (pre-positioned near Char Dham, river valleys) | Significantly faster — stationed locally across vulnerable mountain sectors |
| **Key Strengths** | Advanced structural collapse extraction; aquatic disaster response; CBRN capabilities | High-altitude mountaineering rescue; swift-river maneuvering; localized fire containment |
| **Key Deployments** | 2023 Silkyara Tunnel (technical drilling); 2021 Chamoli (large-scale debris clearance) | 2025 Dharali flash flood; seasonal cloudburst rescue; Char Dham pilgrim rescue |

| Parameter | Details |

> **KEY FACT BOX: NDRF vs. SDRF in Uttarakhand**
>
> Think of it as: **SDRF = First Responders** (local, fast, terrain-expert); **NDRF = Heavy Reinforcement** (central, specialized, macro-crisis). SDRF is always the first to arrive because they are pre-stationed at district hotspots. NDRF deploys for severe/complex operations requiring advanced technical capabilities (CBRN, heavy structural collapse).

## 25.13 THE WAY FORWARD: STRATEGIC POLICY RECOMMENDATIONS

### Structural Reforms

| Recommendation | Rationale |
|---|---|
| **Himalayan-Specific EIAs** | Standardized, stringent Environmental Impact Assessment guidelines tailored to fragile mountain ecosystems — replacing copy-paste models used for plain terrain |
| **Eco-Centric Development** | Prioritize low-impact infrastructure (well-designed bridges, eco-friendly tunnels with non-invasive Boring Machines) over aggressive mountain-cutting and surface blasting |
| **Regular Geotechnical Audits** | Ban major infrastructure projects until independent panels complete comprehensive seismic, geological, and hydrological risk assessments |
| **Decentralized CBDRR** | Training local village task forces; establishing localized communication networks not reliant on vulnerable mobile towers; reviving traditional water harvesting (Naulas/Dharas) |

### Nature-Based Solutions

| Solution | Mechanism | Impact |
|---|---|---|
| **Bio-Engineering for Slopes** | Replace concrete retaining walls with geo-grids, steel mesh net-anchors, and Vetiver grass | Anchors topsoil naturally; allows safe rainwater drainage |
| **Eco-Sewerage Systems** | Mandate eco-sanitation and twin-pit waste systems for hotels/homestays above 1,500m | Stops wastewater softening glacial debris slopes |
| **Eco-Forestry Restoration** | Community-led programs via Van Panchayats to remove invasive pine and replant native Oak (Banj) | Restores soil moisture; revives springs; builds fire resilience |
| **Spring Rejuvenation (SARRA)** | Treat and recharge critical water sources | Stabilizes subsurface moisture; prevents land subsidence |

> **KEY FACT BOX: The New Paradigm**
>
> Uttarakhand's disaster management has evolved through three phases:
> 1. **Pre-2013:** Reactive relief-and-rehabilitation
> 2. **2013–2020:** Institutional building (SDMA, SDRF, NDRF deployment)
> 3. **2021–Present:** Technology-first + Nature-based solutions (Bhudev App, Bio-Engineering, SARRA, Pirul Bio-Economy)
>
> The state is attempting to **"decouple economic growth from ecological destruction"** through monetizing hazard removal, creating dedicated technical authorities, and enforcing hard engineering changes.
